Insights on what's playing

Sleevesight is a mobile app that knows what your music player is playing and shows short, sourced insights about the artist, song, and album. Every fact links to where it came from.

How it works

  1. Track

    Sleevesight is notified when your music player starts a track, and knows its title, artists, album and cover.

    Audio never touched.

  2. Trace

    Sleevesight finds the track's traces across the sources you choose.

    Nothing made up.

  3. Tell

    Sleevesight shares what makes the artist, song and album worth knowing, in a few lines written by the AI service you choose.

    Every fact cited.

Coming soon: beyond the basics

Read further details for the song, artists, and the album, each with an evidence-based insight. Ask follow-up questions about what is playing, answered by an AI service you enable with your own API key. Export the playlist history, with insights.

The examples are illustrations, written for this page from the sources named beside them.

  • Song

    The story of the song, how it was made, and how it was received.

    Your AI service writes it from the evidence your sources found, and cites each piece where it uses it.

    This example insight is based on two passages of Wikipedia's article on the song: W1 and W2.

    Song

    Michelle

    George Martin recalled composing the melody of the guitar solo, and showed Harrison the notes during the session., source W1.  It topped the charts in Italy for eight weeks, and in France for five, as the lead track of an EP., source W2. 

  • Artist

    Who the artist is, and why they matter, for each artist on the track.

    This example insight is based on three lines of Last.fm's biography of the band: L1, L2 and L3.

    Artist

    The Beatles

    An English rock band formed in Liverpool in 1960, whose core lineup was John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison and Ringo Starr., source L1, source L2.  They are widely regarded as one of the most influential acts in popular music, and played a central role in the development of 1960s counterculture., source L3. 

  • Album

    The album's release, and how it fared.

    This example insight is based on two passages of the band's own page on the album, found by web search: G1 and G2.

    Album

    Rubber Soul, 1965

    Released on 3 December 1965, just two weeks after its final mixing., source G1.  It topped the album chart in the UK, and spent six weeks at number one in the US from early January 1966., source G2. 

  • Language

    The languages a song is sung in, or none for an instrumental, and the story behind them.

    In this example insight, the languages come from MusicBrainz's entry for the song: MB1. The story is based on two passages of Wikipedia's article on it: W1 and W2.

    Language

    Michelle, The Beatles

    English, French, source MB1. 

    Paul McCartney sings it in English, with some lines in French., source W1.  The French words came from Jan Vaughan, a French teacher and the wife of his old friend Ivan Vaughan., source W2. 

  • Deep dive

    Questions of your own about what is playing, about anyone on the track or anything in its story.

    An AI service you enable answers them, on your own API key, and each answer names the model that wrote it.

    This example response is based on three passages of Wikipedia's article on Paul McCartney: W1, W2 and W3.

    Deep dive

    Paul McCartney

    You:  Tell me more about his career.

    AI:  He joined Lennon's skiffle group, the Quarrymen, in 1957, which became the Beatles in 1960., source W1.  After the band split, he released his first solo album, McCartney, in 1970, and formed Wings with his wife Linda and Denny Laine., source W2.  His songwriting partnership with Lennon remains the most successful in music history., source W3.  by Claude Haiku 5.5

You choose

  • Your player

    Sleevesight supports a variety of players, including Spotify and YouTube Music in the initial release, with more coming soon. It reads what is playing from the phone itself, so following Spotify takes no Spotify developer app and no Spotify login, whichever plan you have.

  • Your sources

    MusicBrainz, Wikipedia and Wikidata, Last.fm, and the Exa web search, with others coming soon. You provide your API keys where a source needs one.

  • Your AI service

    The AI service of your choice with the model and effort you select. You provide your API keys. Initial release supports Anthropic, OpenAI and Google, more providers coming soon. Each service's own terms apply, and may set a minimum age beyond Sleevesight's own (it is not directed at children under 13): Google's requires 18 or older.

Privacy

Nothing you play reaches the developer

Sleevesight does not require you to create a user account. It has no telemetry, crash reporting or analytics of its own; if any is added, it will never carry your data. It does not show ads, and does not send any usage data for personalized marketing.

Your API keys are stored encrypted on the phone and never logged.

The privacy policy says what each service receives, and what you can switch off.

What leaves the phone, and to whom

The sources you chose: the playing track's information; by default, the next track's title and artists to MusicBrainz, if enabled.

The AI service you chose: the track's title, artists and album, and what the sources found about those. Each insight is one request of its own, with no sustained conversation. Requests use your API key, and are handled under the terms for your AI service account.

All of it travels encrypted (HTTPS), your API keys included, each only to the service it belongs to.

Nothing else leaves the phone, unless you export your diagnostics, or your playlist history with its insights, to a file yourself. Your listening history stays on the device.

Install

Initial release of Sleevesight supports Android only, and is distributed on Google Play.
An iOS version may be released at a later time.

Sleevesight undergoes internal testing. Refer to the developer for further details.
