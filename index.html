Insights on what’s playing

Facts and stories about the song, artist and album you’re listening to, linked to their sources.

Sleevesight is a music insight companion to Spotify, YouTube Music, Qobuz, Tidal, or another player of your choice.

How it works

  1. Track

    Sleevesight is notified when your music player starts a track, and knows its title, artists, album and cover.

    Audio never touched.

  2. Trace

    Sleevesight finds for you information about the track across the external sources you choose.

    Nothing made up.

  3. Tell

    Sleevesight shares with you what makes the artist, song and album worth knowing, in a few lines written by the AI service you enable, to read or to listen to in the phone's own voice.

    Every fact cited.

What it shows

Sleevesight shows the playing track with its cover, and a key insight: a few lines on why the track is worth knowing. The rest opens on a tap.

  • Song, Artist, Album

    Each with an insight of its own, written from what the sources say about it. The artist and album insights are written once and shown again on each of their tracks.

  • Dates

    When the track and its album first came out. For a remaster or a later edition, the original release first, then the edition’s own.

  • Genres

    The track’s genres, each with a description, and, on a tap, why the track is of a specific genre.

  • Lyrics

    Links to the lyrics and, on a tap, what the song is about, written from pages about it, each sentence citing its source.

  • The cover’s back

    A tap flips the cover over to show the album’s tracklist, from MusicBrainz.

  • Sources

    What each source found, and where. When a source could not be reached, Sleevesight says so, with an option to retry the search.

You choose

  • Your players

    Sleevesight follows Spotify by default, and any other music player on the phone you switch on. It reads information about what is being played from the phone, not the player. Sleevesight does not talk to your player directly, and does not require you to have a premium account.

  • Your sources

    MusicBrainz, Wikipedia and Wikidata, Last.fm, LRCLIB and the Exa web search, each enabled if you choose to. Last.fm and Exa need an API key of your own; the others need none.

  • Your AI services

    Anthropic, OpenAI or Google, with the model and reasoning effort you select, on your own API key. Each service's own terms apply, and may set a minimum age different from what Sleevesight expects (Sleevesight is not directed at children under 13).

What it costs

Sleevesight is free and open-source: no price, no subscription, no usage charge.
The services you enable bill you directly, on your own keys and your own accounts with them. MusicBrainz, Wikipedia and Wikidata, Last.fm and LRCLIB cost nothing; Exa's web search and Google's Gemini API have free tiers.

See What it costs you for each service's prices and free tiers, and what a track costs with the defaults.

Privacy

Nothing reaches the developer unless you send it

Sleevesight does not require you to create a user account. It has no telemetry, crash reporting or analytics of its own; if any is added at a later time, it will never carry your data. It does not show ads, and does not send any usage data for personalized marketing.

Your API keys are stored encrypted on the phone and never logged.

The Privacy policy clarifies what each service receives, and what you can switch off.

What leaves the phone, and to whom

To the sources you chose: the playing track's information; by default, the next track's title and artists to MusicBrainz, if proactive sourcing is enabled.

To the AI service you chose: the track's title, artists and album, and what the sources found about those. Each insight, and each explanation you ask for, is one request of its own, with no sustained conversation. Requests use your API key, and are handled under the terms for your AI service account.

All information travels encrypted (HTTPS), your API keys included, each only to the service it belongs to.

Nothing else leaves the phone, unless you export your diagnostics to a file, send a report from your mail app, or have an insight read aloud by a text-to-speech engine that speaks through its server. While you type a key on a computer instead of on the phone, the phone announces itself over Bluetooth so that the computer can find it: devices nearby can see that it is there, but not the key, which travels encrypted.
Your listening history stays on the device.
