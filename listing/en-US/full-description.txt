Sleevesight shows short insights about the song you are playing: who made it, where it comes from, what is worth knowing about it. Each insight cites its sources, and the app checks every citation.

HOW IT WORKS
• Track. Sleevesight follows what your music player is playing, through the player's Android media session. It reads the title, artists, album, cover and playback state, and the queue up next, and never touches the audio.
• Trace. It looks the track up in the sources you switch on: MusicBrainz, Wikipedia, Last.fm and Exa web search. A match that fails an identity check is shown as rejected, not used quietly.
• Tell. Your AI service turns what the sources found into a few sentences, each citing its evidence. Once an insight is written, the app checks its citations: a sentence without a source, or citing evidence that does not exist, is flagged. With the model's own knowledge switched on (it is off by default), the model may add what it knows, marked unverified. When little is documented about a track, the insight is short. When the sources give nothing to write from, Sleevesight says so and makes no AI call, unless the model's own knowledge is on.

ON SCREEN
• The cover, title, artists, album and year of first release, in colors taken from the cover.
• Genre labels from the artists' tags, each saying where it came from.
• The insight, whose citations jump to their evidence.
• What each source found, rejected or could not verify.

WORKS WITH YOUR PLAYER
Spotify is followed by default, and any other player whose Android media session reports music can be switched on. Calls from the phone app are never read. Sleevesight reads the phone's media session, not Spotify's Web API, so it needs no Spotify developer app and no Spotify login, whichever Spotify plan you have.

YOU CHOOSE
• Which sources to use, each a switch. MusicBrainz and Wikipedia need no key; Last.fm and Exa need your own API key.
• Whether anything happens in the background. With “Look up in the background” off, as it is by default, lookups and writing happen only while the app is on screen.
• When insights are written: for every track, only when you skip, or only when you tap Write. Each insight is a request on your own AI key, so it is on your bill. With “Look up in the background” on, automatic writing pauses after five tracks with the app off screen; you can change the count or turn the pause off.

WHAT YOU NEED
• Android 8 or later.
• Notification access, which is how Android lets an app read what a player is playing. Sleevesight ignores the notifications themselves.
• For insights, your own API key for Anthropic, OpenAI or Google. Without one, the app still shows the track and what the sources found.
• Sleevesight has no minimum age of its own beyond the privacy policy’s, but an AI service’s terms may: Google’s Gemini API requires you to be 18 or older. Each key’s dialog says what its service’s terms require.

PRIVACY
Sleevesight has no server, no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no telemetry. The track's title and artists go straight from your phone to the sources you switch on (by default, the next track's title and artists also go to MusicBrainz, before it plays). The title, artists and album, with what the sources found, go to your AI service, on your own key. A cover given only as a web address is fetched from the server that address names. Nothing reaches the developer unless you send a problem report from your mail app. Your keys are encrypted on the phone, and the settings remove any one of them, or all at once. The privacy policy: https://sleevesight.app/privacy.html
