Sleevesight is an open-source Android app that reads what your music player is playing and shows short, sourced insights about the song, its artists, its album and its genres. Every factual sentence cites the source it came from, and the citations are checked in code.

**Where it stands.** The Android MVP is done, and the app is in closed testing on Google Play, which testers join by invitation, before its public release (Phase 1.5, [Roadmap](#roadmap)). The [product page](https://sleevesight.app/) shows what it does; the [documentation](https://sleevesight.app/docs/) says how to use it, how it works and how it is made.

## What it does

- Notices the track change in Spotify, or any other player you follow that publishes an Android media session.
- Shows the cover, title, artists, and album with that release's year, and a **Dates** button that says each date in full: when the album came out, where the track first came out, and for a cover, who first recorded the song and when; plus the genre labels of the most specific level found as chips; a tap on one opens its genre's panel, with the labels of the track, its artists and its album in a row each, under the track's title, the artist's name and the album's title, the genre marked wherever it appears, and where each row's labels came from. Above the table, what the genre is, the first two sentences of its Wikipedia article (credited and linked), or a line saying there is none from Wikipedia, and *Why is this …?*, which asks your AI service why this track has the genre, written from the track's evidence and cited like an insight; once written, it is kept with the track. For a genre only MusicBrainz gives, the button is greyed out with a line saying why: Sleevesight doesn't send MusicBrainz tags to AI services, because of their licence.
- Writes a few sentences on why the track is interesting. Every factual sentence links to the source it came from.
- Reads the insight aloud with a tap on the speaker beside its heading (and a genre's *Why is this …?* answer with the speaker under it), in the phone's own built-in text-to-speech voice (only as good as the phone's: not a natural or studio voice), for listening without looking at the phone: the citations left out, a sentence the sources do not back read as "Unverified:", the music lowered meanwhile, and stopped by a tap, a track change or leaving the app.
- Shows the sources themselves, behind a *Sources* chip that opens them in a panel like a genre's: a table of the sources that are on (and any that is off whose lines the insight kept from before still cites), each with the letter its citations use (a chip that shows or hides its lines), its name and what it found; the lines with a link to where they were found, what was found but could not be verified (8 at a time, paged by one group of buttons under them, *Show less*, *Show more* and *Show all*), and a note when a source's saved key can no longer be read, or it could not be reached or failed.
- Opens the player with a tap on its name: the player's own screen when it names one, else the app as its launcher icon opens it.
- With nothing playing, lists the players you have, A to Z, each a tap away (*Play on Spotify*; a player switched off links to *Players* to enable it, or is left out with *Only enabled players on Home*, under *Players*), and under them a small picture of the home screen while a track plays, with hand-drawn notes on what each part does: a tap on a note tells more about its part, and a tap on the picture, or its magnifier, opens it full screen, to pinch or double-tap to zoom. *Hide once* or *Dismiss* puts that guide away, for now or for good ([what each does](https://sleevesight.app/docs/tutorials/usage/#close-hide-once-and-dismiss)).

Planned ([Roadmap](#roadmap)): deeper insight on the song, each artist, the album, a genre or a similar artist; follow-up questions; playing, pausing and skipping from the app.

## How it works

1. **Read.** A notification listener gives the app the player's media session: which player it is; the title, artists, album, duration and cover; whether it is playing, and how far in; and the queue up next, with its name. Sleevesight never touches the audio. Android lets an app list what other apps are playing only with notification access, which cannot be limited to one app, so Android's screen for it warns that the app could read all your notifications. Sleevesight uses the access only to see what apps say they are playing, to find your music players, and follows only the players you switch on; it never reads your notifications, messages or contacts, and never acts on them or on calls. On Android 12 and newer it also tells Android not to deliver any notifications to it at all. The first-run screen explains this right above *Open notification access*, before Android's screen does, and the home screen's *Turn on notification access* says it in short.
2. **Look up.** The track's title and artists are cleaned up: tags the player appends, such as "2006 Remaster" or "feat. Van Morrison", are set aside, and featured artists are added to the artist list, so the song can be found under its plain name. It is then matched against the sources you switched on: MusicBrainz, Wikipedia and Wikidata, Last.fm, and the Exa web search. A pair the player names as one artist, such as "Derya Yıldırım & Grup Şimşek", is found on MusicBrainz where it credits the two separately. Matches that fail an identity check are rejected and never used; *Show grounding details*, a developer option, lists them with the reason. A source that cannot be reached says so; while the phone is offline the app says so, and once it is back online the playing track's unreachable sources are asked again.
3. **Write.** The AI service you chose turns the retrieved evidence into the insight, citing evidence ids, in plain text: a title it marks anyway is set in italics, bold shows as bold, and other formatting marks are left out. It is not allowed to add facts of its own unless you turn that on, and then they are marked unverified.
4. **Check.** The citations are checked in code: uncited sentences and citations to missing evidence are flagged in the insight, and a note under the insight says how many sentences the sources do not back, shown only when there are any. When the AI model corrects its own reply in the open, only the corrected version is shown once the reply is complete.
5. **Report.** *Report a problem*, in red, the last line under the insight, sends the developer a report of an insight that is wrong or offensive, by email from your own mail app, once you have seen what it holds ([Privacy](#privacy)).

When little is documented about a track, the insight is short instead of filling the gap, for instance by restating the album a source has already named. The insight stays on the track: what the sources say about the artist is sent to the AI service only for a track that has little evidence of its own, and a fact about the artist that one insight told is not sent again for the rest of the listening session, so an album played through does not repeat its artist's biography on every track. When the sources give nothing to write from, Sleevesight says so, and makes no AI call when it can tell beforehand. With the model's own knowledge on, the album the player reports is enough for a call, once the player has also reported the artists: the model may then add what it knows, marked unverified.

## You choose

Each of these is a setting; the [settings page](https://sleevesight.app/docs/tutorials/settings/) explains every one, with its default and why. The settings are in sections, each a chip with its name and, beside it, a line on what it holds; a tap opens the section as a panel in its place, with *Close*, and several can be open at once; a search box above them finds a setting by its name, what it says, its value or its choices: *Players*, *Sources*, *AI services*, *Secrets*, *Genres*, *Insights*, *History*, *Appearance* and *About*, with *Developer* after seven taps on *Version*, as on Android (from the start in the test build on Google Play's testing tracks). A setting with a few short choices, such as *Theme*, shows them in its row as buttons, one tap to choose; one with many or long choices opens a list of them. In developer mode, everything only it shows is outlined in teal with a small *Developer* tag, so a tester's screenshots show what other users will not see (*Mark developer items*, on by default).

- **Sources.** Each source is a switch, with a link to its website, and the AI service's own knowledge is a separate one, off by default (*AI service knowledge*: what the AI model chosen under *AI services* learned in training, at most one claim, marked unverified; *AI services* there is a link to that section, and with no AI service switched on the row says the source is not used). MusicBrainz and Wikipedia need no key; Last.fm and the Exa web search need your own API key, and are off until it is saved and you switch them on. Each line from the web search is a short quote from a page it found, at most 300 characters: a longer one is cut after a whole word and ends in *…*. Discogs comes in Phase 3. Each row starts with a plain badge with the letter the home screen's chips use (*MB*, *W*, *L*, *G* for Exa, *AI*), under *Genres* too; the AI services show their initial. The badge and the switch sit level with the service's name, not with the middle of its description.
- **Genres.** When *Show genres* is on, Sleevesight asks the genre sources for genres of the track, its artists and its album, and shows them on the home screen; when off, no genre information is sought from any source, and neither Last.fm's tags nor Wikidata's genres reach the insight, though a sentence quoted from a source may still name a style in passing. The genre labels are the tags Last.fm and MusicBrainz give the track, its artists and its album, kept only when they name a genre in a fixed list; each source is a switch, with a link to its website. MusicBrainz gives the track's (with no request of its own) and the artists', Last.fm all three. MusicBrainz's tags are by its contributors under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0: they are used for labels only, never sent to the AI service, and each label from them credits and links MusicBrainz.
- **Which AI.** Anthropic, OpenAI or Google (the Gemini API), with your own API key, model (shown by its name) and reasoning effort; one service at a time, and with none on, nothing is written. Each insight is a call the service bills on your key, as Exa bills each web search; Sleevesight itself charges nothing, and [What it costs you](https://sleevesight.app/docs/costs/) lists each service's prices and free tier. Switching off a service does not erase the most recently used model and effort settings. The model choice offers only the models the service lists for your key, and at start the app checks that the selected model (or the default) is still listed: if it is not, or a call is refused for it, the settings and the home screen say *The currently selected Anthropic model Claude Haiku 5.5 is not available. Please select another model or provider.*, and nothing is written until you choose one; on the home screen, *AI services* in it opens the settings at that service's model. A local model through Ollama comes in Phase 3. Google's [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) set what its free tier allows: on it, outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, Google uses what is sent to improve its products, and for apps made available to users in those three, the terms allow only the paid tier. Each service is used under its provider's terms, on your own account, and its key dialog says, in a line with a link, what they require: Anthropic's set no minimum age and are not for consumer use, though Anthropic welcomes individuals on the API; OpenAI's let minors use the API only with a parent's or guardian's consent; Google's require you to be 18 or older, and are not for consumer use. Sleevesight itself sets no minimum age beyond its privacy policy's (it is not directed at children under 13); the [privacy policy](https://sleevesight.app/privacy.html#children-and-the-ai-services-own-terms) quotes the terms, as checked on 24 October 2026.
- **When it writes.** Each insight is a call on your key (now and then two). *Stop writing* ends the call at once; if the request has reached your AI service, it still charges for what it has read and written so far. By default one is written for every track change (*On track change (auto)*); you can have insights written only when you skip to a track (*On track skip (auto)*), or only when you tap *Write insight* (*On demand*) (*Reactive*, whose choices are each described where you pick them). After the app starts, nothing is written automatically until you act in it: choose a mode, or tap *Write insight* or *Rewrite insight*. Under each insight, a button says what a tap does, the same in every mode: *Write insight* whenever the track has no insight and nothing will write it automatically, *Rewrite insight* for an insight kept from before with earlier source or model settings, or *Stop writing* while one is written; an arrow beside it opens the same three choices, so you can change *Reactive* where you read the insights. Every button that calls the AI service ends in a small, dimmed *AI*, greyed out or not, and a screen reader reads it as "<label>, uses AI"; *Stop writing* and the arrow make no call and carry none. Beside an insight already written, the arrow's menu begins with *Look again*: every source looked up afresh (with Exa web search on, a paid search), with no call to the AI service; when they found something new, *Rewrite insight* is offered, the paid call, made only if you tap it; otherwise the line under the insight says *As written previously. No update from sources.* An insight written earlier, not just now, says *As written previously.* on the first line under it, with what follows from it on the same line; the note on the sentences not backed by the sources is on the next line, and *Report a problem* on the last. Lookups and writing happen only while the app is on screen, unless you turn on *Look up in the background*, and automatic writing pauses after a few track changes away from the app (*Saver*); opening the app resumes it, and a track that played while it was paused is written only when you tap *Write insight*.
- **Reading aloud.** *Read aloud*, the speaker beside *Key insights*, makes no AI call. While it reads, the music is lowered, or paused and played on after it (*While reading aloud*: *Lower the music* by default, or *Pause the music*); the track's title and artists can be read first (*Read the track line first*, off by default); and each new insight can be read by itself as soon as it is written, only while the app is on screen and never while TalkBack or another screen reader is on (*Read new insights aloud*, off by default). It reads in English, the insight's language, and says so when the phone's engine has no English voice rather than read with a wrong one. The rows are at the end of *Insights*.
- **What it tells.** Within a listening session, each fact about an artist is told once, and a track with enough about the song itself gets none (*Don't repeat artist facts*, on by default), so an album played through does not repeat its artist on every track. Off, every insight may tell all of them. A new session starts after a break in listening, set under *New session idle trigger* in *History*, which the setting's description links to. Each artist is looked up once either way when *Keep what sources said about each artist* is on.
- **Which players.** Spotify by default, once it is installed; any player whose media session reports music can be followed, and opened from its row, which shows the app's own icon beside its name. *Players* lists them A to Z by name, as the home screen does while nothing plays; there, *Only enabled players on Home* (off by default) has the home screen list only the players switched on. An app you uninstall leaves the list; installed again, it comes back switched off. A player that reports music, playing or paused, is added to the settings switched off, and Sleevesight does not track what a player that is off plays. Calls from the phone app are never read. Sleevesight reads the phone's media session, not Spotify's Web API, so following Spotify needs no Spotify developer app and no Spotify login. Spotify playing on another device (Spotify Connect) shows as playing while the Spotify app is open on the phone. Tested on a phone: Spotify, YouTube Music, Tidal and Qobuz. Under the list, *Can’t see your player?* says what a player needs to be listed.
- **What is kept.** The history (the list of tracks you play: titles, artists, albums and when they played, never the music), the insights and genre explanations written, and the sources' findings stay on the phone, for as long as the settings say (the insights and genre explanations as long as the history); *Clear history*, under *Keep history for*, deletes at once everything kept about the tracks you played (the history, the insights and genre explanations written, and the sources' findings), and keeps your API keys and settings; *Keep what sources said about each artist* is off by default, since it amounts to a record of the artists you play.
- **How it looks.** Light or dark as the phone is, or always one of them (*Theme*), switched in one tap with the button beside the cogwheel on the home screen, which moves from light to dark to the system's and back to light (*Theme button*, on by default); the app's own font or the phone's; whether the sources and a genre's panel open in place on the home screen or each on a page of its own, with a back arrow (*Layout*: *Expandable boxes* by default, or *Separate pages*); the logo's intro at start, or none; and *Restore dismissed messages*, which shows again what you dismissed.
- **Your API keys.** Under *Secrets*; each AI service and each source that needs a key has a link to its key there (*Set your Anthropic API key here.*, or *Update your Anthropic API key here.* once it is saved). Until its key is saved, such a service's switch is off and cannot be switched on, and it says it needs the key; removing the key, or every key with *Purge keys*, switches it off again, and saving a key switches nothing on. Typed, scanned from a QR code (one key, or several at once, from a code made on a computer at [sleevesight.app/api-keys](https://sleevesight.app/api-keys), which *Load keys* links under its row with the guide to loading keys: *Load keys* first shows five words to type on that page, which seal the code: it opens only with them, so a photo or a screenshot of the code alone gives nothing away; the phone accepts a code once, within 5 minutes of showing the words, and refuses one made with other words; then it shows what the code holds and every problem with it before anything is saved), filled from your password manager, which offers its saved entries on each key's field but is not asked to save a key, or typed on a computer over Bluetooth ([sleevesight.app/keyboard.html](https://sleevesight.app/keyboard.html)), where the phone shows as an unknown device unless *Share phone's name with Type on a computer* is on: then it advertises its Bluetooth name while the session lasts, for anyone nearby to see; scanning and typing on a computer are icons in the key's field, and each opens a screen laid out as *Load keys*' is, which says what code to scan and where to make it, or what typing on a computer is and its steps, with a link to the page, above the camera's view; the camera's frame takes most of the picture, with a narrow dimmed margin. They are encrypted with a key in the phone's Android Keystore, never shown again once saved (the field's eye shows only a key being typed, never one read from a QR code, so that keys can be lent), never logged, and left out of backups; each can be removed, once confirmed (in its dialog, *Remove* is red, with a trash can icon), or all at once (*Purge keys*, which lists the keys it will delete in a table of their services and last four characters, the characters in a code font, and deletes the Keystore key with them, so no copy left can be decrypted; it does not revoke them, so another copy of a key still works). The key screens cannot be captured in a screenshot or a screen recording. In development, the CLI's `keys-qr` draws a setup code too ([Pipeline CLI](#pipeline-cli)).

## Privacy

The playing track's title and artists go to the sources you enabled (Wikidata is asked only by catalogue numbers (MusicBrainz and Wikidata identifiers), never by a name; for the genre labels, Last.fm also gets the album), and its title, artists and album, with what those sources found, to the AI service you chose, for each insight and each *Why is this …?* you tap (with the genre's name). A genre's panel asks Wikidata and Wikipedia for the genre's article, by the genre alone. By default, the title and artists of the next track in the player's queue also go to MusicBrainz, if you switched it on, ahead of its play. When a player gives the cover only as a web address, the app fetches it over HTTPS from the server that address names, while it is on screen (or with *Look up in the background* on), and the address itself may identify the album or episode; a plain `http://` address is not fetched. Each API key goes only to the service it belongs to. *Read aloud* hands the insight's text to the phone's text-to-speech engine, the one chosen in Android's settings, which may speak it on the phone or, as Google's network voices do, send it to its server to be spoken, under that engine's own policy. *Keep what sources said about each artist*, off by default, trades the two: on, what the sources said about each artist is kept on the phone and fewer requests about artists go out; off, none of it is kept and each track's artists are looked up again. Each track's own lookups send its artists' names either way. While you type a key on a computer, the phone advertises over Bluetooth a service for that session, which devices nearby can see, nameless unless you switch on *Share phone's name with Type on a computer*. Nothing else leaves the phone, unless you export the diagnostics to a file yourself, or report a problem with an insight: *Report a problem* shows the whole report (the reason and your note; the track as the player reported it, with its MusicBrainz ids; the insight and the evidence it was written from (for an insight kept from before, every evidence line of the track); the prompt version, AI service, model and effort; never your keys, the history or the settings) and hands it to your mail app, addressed to support@sleevesight.app; it reaches the developer only if you send it, and until then your mail app handles it as any message you write, drafts included. In developer mode, *Report button* (*Auto* by default only in the test build on Google Play's testing tracks, *Hide* in any other) puts a button on the home screen that does the same for a tester's report of the app's state: when it was tapped, the version, the settings (never your keys), what the player reported and what the app made of it, and the diagnostics, with the latest track changes only if you add them; you can edit or leave out each part before your mail app opens. Nothing the app sends on its own reaches the developer. No accounts and no ads, and no telemetry, crash reporting or analytics of its own; if any is added, it will never carry your data. Keys are stored encrypted on the device, and the settings remove any one of them, or all at once. The full [privacy policy](https://sleevesight.app/privacy.html) is on the site, with where Sleevesight stands under the GDPR (who is responsible for what, your rights and how to use them, transfers outside the EU) and the EU AI Act (the app's role, and how it shows that a model writes the insights). The first-run screen and the settings link to it.

## Requirements

Android only. iOS does not let one app read what another app is playing. Sleevesight is to be distributed through Google Play.

## Building

Requires JDK 21 or later (Robolectric's Android 16 runtime, used by the app's tests, needs it) and the Android SDK (`ANDROID_HOME`, or `sdk.dir` in `local.properties`).

```sh
./gradlew build                                                                  # compile, tests, Detekt, ktlint, Android lint
./gradlew checkDependencies checkDependencyPage checkManifest checkModuleGraph   # the structural checks
./gradlew :build-logic:functionalTest                                            # the build logic's functional tests (needs Maven Central)
./gradlew :app:assembleDebug                                                     # the debug APK, in app/build/outputs/apk/debug/
```

The structural checks enforce the design's constraints: only allowlisted dependencies (`config/dependency-allowlist.txt`), each shipped one on the [dependencies page](https://sleevesight.app/docs/dependencies/) (`docs/dependencies/index.md`), only allowlisted permissions (`config/permission-allowlist.txt`), and the module boundaries (`config/module-graph.properties`). After changing dependencies, refresh the lockfiles with `./gradlew resolveAndLockAll --write-locks`. CI keeps the debug APK of each run that builds as a workflow artifact, `sleevesight-debug-apk`. The debug build installs beside the Play build, as `app.sleevesight.debug`, and names itself apart from it: *Sleevesight Debug* in the launcher, App info and the notification-access list, a bug on its icon and in place of the record in Home's logo, and the version of the release it leads to with `-debug`, such as `0.1.7-debug` (`design/release.md`, *Versioning*). A pull request that changes only documents the build never reads, such as this README or the release notes, skips the build; the list is in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`. A scheduled workflow, `.github/workflows/default-models.yml` (Mondays), checks each AI service's default model with a spend-limited key stored as a repository secret: one model list and one short request each, failing when a default is no longer listed (or, for Anthropic, deprecated) or does not answer, so the default is updated before users meet it; a service whose secret is not set is skipped with a notice.

Git hooks in `.githooks/` catch most of what CI would find before it gets there. Install them once per clone with `.githooks/install`, from any of its checkouts. It writes a small dispatcher for each hook into `.git/hooks`, which every worktree shares, moving an earlier hook that is not one aside as `<name>.before-dispatcher` (one that is not Git LFS's no longer runs, as the installer says, and goes into `.githooks/` by hand); run it again to update them. Each dispatcher runs the hook of the checkout it runs in, so every branch gets its own version of the hooks. A branch from before them gets a fallback: the guard against commits on `main` and `dev-*`, and Git LFS's hooks. The hooks need Java, and [Git LFS](https://git-lfs.com) (the logo videos are stored in it), whose hooks they run as well.

- **Before each commit**, `pre-commit` formats the staged Kotlin files (`.kt` and `.kts`, outside `build-logic/`) with ktlint `--format` and stages what it changed; a partly staged file keeps its unstaged changes as they are, and a file committed with `git commit <path>` needs a `git add` afterwards, as the hook says. The commit fails only on what `--format` cannot fix. If ktlint cannot be downloaded, the commit goes ahead unformatted, with a warning. ktlint is its standalone jar, at the version `gradle/libs.versions.toml` sets for the Gradle plugin, downloaded from Maven Central on first use into `~/.cache/sleevesight/ktlint/` (under `XDG_CACHE_HOME` when set) and checked against a SHA-256 kept in the hook. The hook also refuses commits on `main` and `dev-*`, which move only through merged pull requests, and `pre-merge-commit` refuses merge commits there.
- **Before each push**, `pre-push` runs `./gradlew :<module>:staticChecks`, which is `check` without the tests (ktlint; Detekt, with type resolution in the modules whose `check` uses it; and Android lint in the app), for each module the pushed commits change. A change to the build itself (`build-logic/`, `gradle/`, the root Gradle files, `config/detekt/`, `.editorconfig`) checks every module, and a push that changes no module and no build file starts no Gradle. A merge counts with all it brings to its first parent, since combining two branches can break a check. Gradle checks the working tree, so the result is the pushed commits' only when they are what is checked out.

In October 2026, on GitHub's standard 4-core Linux runner, a CI run that builds took a minute or a minute and a half when no code had changed and it restored a current build cache. It took 4 to 11 minutes when code had changed or the restored cache was behind `main`, depending on how much of the module graph had to rebuild. In a run that rebuilt nearly everything (about 11 minutes), `./gradlew build` took about 8¾ minutes and the build logic's functional tests about 1¾; setting up the job and restoring the caches, the structural checks, the debug APK and its upload took under a minute together. Within that `build`, the longest stretches were the pipeline modules' compilation, checks and tests (about 3 minutes, in parallel) and compiling and running the app's Robolectric tests (about 2¾ minutes).

## Pipeline CLI

`:cli` runs the pipeline over the test playlist (`fixtures/below-soundtrack/`) and prints a coverage report: per source the count of each outcome, per track the evidence lines and genre labels, the insights with their grounding-check counts, the Phase 0.5 gate's criteria, and the diagnostics log. The exit status is 1 when a gate criterion fails, 2 for a usage error or a source request with no recording, and 3 when a criterion the run should have checked could not be (a source unreachable or failed, an insight call failed).

```sh
./gradlew :cli:run --args="replay"                         # recorded responses only: no network, no keys
./gradlew :cli:run --args="live --tracks 1,63"             # live sources and AI, with your keys
./gradlew :cli:run --args="live --tracks all --out .dev/coverage.md"
./gradlew :cli:run --args="record --record musicbrainz"    # refresh fixtures/http/ from the live service
./gradlew :cli:run --args="live --tracklist fixtures/tracklists/sundown.json"  # an album, in its order
./gradlew :cli:run --args="live --repeat 3"                # the regression tracks three times, for the gate
```

`replay` runs the design's regression tracks (the default `--tracks regression`) from `fixtures/http/`, and fails on any source request with no recording (a missing recorded AI reply is reported, not fatal); the build runs it as a test. `live` reads `SLEEVESIGHT_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `SLEEVESIGHT_OPENAI_API_KEY`, `SLEEVESIGHT_GOOGLE_API_KEY`, `SLEEVESIGHT_EXA_API_KEY` and `SLEEVESIGHT_LASTFM_API_KEY` from the environment or from `~/.config/sleevesight/keys.env`; a source or the AI without a key is reported as off. A keys file (that one, or one given with `--keys`) may also name a key as its provider usually does: `SLEEVESIGHT_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `SLEEVESIGHT_OPENAI_API_KEY` or `OPENAI_API_KEY`, `SLEEVESIGHT_GOOGLE_API_KEY`, `GEMINI_API_KEY` or `GOOGLE_API_KEY`, `SLEEVESIGHT_EXA_API_KEY` or `EXA_API_KEY`, and `SLEEVESIGHT_LASTFM_API_KEY` or `LASTFM_API_KEY`, the first listed winning when a file gives a key under more than one. The environment is read by the `SLEEVESIGHT_` names alone, so a key set in your shell for other tools is never spent unasked. The AI service is `--provider anthropic`, `openai` or `google`: by default the one `--record` names, else Anthropic. `openai` runs only `live`, since only Anthropic's and Google's replies are recorded (`--record anthropic`, `--record google`) and replayed. Each insight is a paid call on your key, and each web search a paid search; the cache (`.dev/cli/cache.db`) keeps web search results for a day, as it keeps the other sources' for theirs, so a track looked up again within that day costs no new search (when MusicBrainz was reachable the first time: without its resolution nothing is cached). What a source says about an artist (Last.fm's answers, the Wikipedia artist article) is kept per artist too, so another track by the same artist does not ask for it again; the report counts those hits. The report never quotes a web search snippet (Exa's terms), and it marks the insights written from web results, which are not to be committed or posted. `--tracklist` runs the tracks of a JSON file in place of the playlist, one after another as a player would report them (an album played through, or one artist's playlist; `fixtures/tracklists/`), and the report's *Artist-level evidence* section shows, per track, how much of the evidence and of the prompt is about the artist and which artist facts each insight cites again from the ones before it ([#239](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/239)). `--artist-facts-every-track` gives every insight all its artist's facts, as the app's *Don't repeat artist facts* off does, and writes every insight afresh, since the cache keys a card by the evidence, not the setting ([#407](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/407)); a default run after it may be served its cards, so compare with `--cache none` or a fresh `--cache` file. It is for `live`: the recorded replies `replay` serves answer the default prompts, so with it a track whose default prompt left lines out has no recorded reply. `--repeat <n>` runs the tracks n times, each pass a listening session of its own with every insight written afresh, and the gate pools the passes: the model's replies vary from one run to the next, so uncited sentences in live replies are judged over 50 sentences or more, from one pass or several (one pass of `--tracks all` is enough; the regression tracks need about three); on fewer, the run fails only at 15 percent uncited or more, and otherwise the report gives their count without a verdict, and how many passes would make the sample ([#399](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/399)). Run it with no arguments for the options.

`keys-qr` draws a setup code of your keys in the terminal, for the app's *Load keys*: the keys from the environment or the keys file, as `live` reads them; with `--keys <file>`, from that file alone, never the environment; or, with `--anthropic-from`, `--openai-from`, `--google-from`, `--exa-from` or `--lastfm-from`, only the keys in the files given, `-` for standard input (not combined with `--keys`). That way a key you hand someone else, such as a tester's, is neither saved beside your own nor typed on the command line, where the shell history would keep it. The code is sealed with the five words *Load keys* shows on the phone, typed at a prompt that does not show them (three letters of each are enough), only in a terminal whose output is not redirected, or read from `--words-from <file>` (`-` for a pipe on standard input, not the terminal, nor when a key is read from it; `<(…)` works too); never from the command line. Anywhere else it reads nothing and says so: piping the output (`| cat`) now needs `--words-from`, and so does Gradle's `run`, whose input and output are not the terminal. `--unsealed` draws the clear code instead, which is the keys themselves, for a key's own *Scan QR code*. Nothing is written to a file or sent anywhere, and no key or word is printed as text. A key that does not have its service's form is refused. Clear the terminal once the code is scanned.

```sh
./gradlew -q :cli:installDist
cli/build/install/sleevesight-cli/bin/sleevesight-cli keys-qr                                # asks for the words
cli/build/install/sleevesight-cli/bin/sleevesight-cli keys-qr --anthropic-from <(pbpaste)   # a key copied from the console
./gradlew -q --console=plain :cli:run --args="keys-qr --words-from words.txt"              # the words in a file: delete it after
```
