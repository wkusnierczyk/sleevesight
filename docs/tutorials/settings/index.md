# Settings

The settings screen opens from the cogwheel. This page goes through it from top to bottom, in the order the screen shows, and says for each setting what it does, what it is set to at first and why, and what changing it sends or keeps. The [usage guide]({{ '/docs/tutorials/usage/' | relative_url }}) says how the rest of the app works, and the [privacy policy]({{ '/privacy.html' | relative_url }}) what leaves the phone, to whom.

<!-- screenshot: the settings screen with every section closed, its chips, from the app version it is taken from -->

A few things hold for the whole screen:

- **The settings are in sections, each a chip.** Each section is a chip with its name, drawn as the chips on the home screen are, every chip as wide as the widest name, with the section's short description beside it (up to two lines). A tap on the chip opens the section as a panel in its place: its name as a heading, *Close* at its top right, a fuller explanation where one is needed, then its settings; *Close* turns it back into the chip. Any number of sections can be open at once, to read or compare them. Every section is closed when the settings open; while you stay, each stays as you left it, through a rotation too. A screen reader reads each chip as a heading and a button, by the section's name, "collapsed", and the panel's name as a heading; with a keyboard or a screen reader, opening a section moves to its *Close*, and *Close* moves back to the chip. The sections are *Players*, *Sources*, *AI services*, *Secrets*, *Genres*, *Insights*, *History*, *Appearance* and *About*, and, only in developer mode, *Developer*.
- **Search.** The box under the *Settings* title, *Search settings*, stays above the list as it scrolls. It looks, as you type and whatever the case, through the sections' names and descriptions and each setting's name, description, current value and the choices it offers (*dark* finds *Theme*), the players found on the phone and the AI services' models by name, in the app's language. While you search, only the sections with a match show, each open: all of a section whose name or description matches, else only the settings that match, with what you typed marked in their names. *Close* still closes a section; a link to another setting ends the search and leads there; × clears the search, and the sections are open again as they were before it. With nothing found, it says *No settings match "…"*. A screen reader reads the box as *Search settings*, and says how many sections match, or that none does. Nothing typed there is kept or sent.
- **A switch is level with its setting's name.** So is the app icon or the badge a row starts with: beside the name's first line, however long the description under it, at any text size.
- **Each change is saved at once.** There is no *Save* button for settings. If a change cannot be saved, because the phone's storage failed, the setting keeps its saved value, and a line above the list says *The last change could not be saved*; try again.
- **Some rows depend on others.** A row that has no effect under the current choices is shown but cannot be changed: *Reactive* while *Show insights* is off, for example, or a genre source while *Show genres* is off. Some say why.
- **Links.** *Open the app ↗* and *Open website ↗* open another app or a page in your browser; a screen reader names what each opens ("Open Spotify", "Open the MusicBrainz website").
- **The labels in italics** on this page are the labels on the screen.
- **Settings and keys stay on the phone.** Neither is part of Android's backup or of a transfer to a new phone.

## Players

*Music apps Sleevesight follows.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight reads what a player says it is playing (track, artist, album). It does not receive the audio or your other notifications. Android grants this with notification access. To find your music players, Sleevesight looks at apps that report what they play (playing or paused) and checks whether it is music (a track with a title and an artist); it stops checking an app for music once it has found some, until Sleevesight starts afresh (not each time you open it). A new player is added to this list switched off. Sleevesight does not follow a player that is off: it does not track what the player plays, and keeps or sends none of it. Switch on the players you want Sleevesight to follow. Calls from the phone app, and sessions Android marks as voice calls, are never read.* Each player is a switch, A to Z by its name (as the home screen lists them while nothing plays), with its app's own icon (as the phone draws it, so nothing is fetched; a row shows without it until it has loaded, and for good if the phone gives none) and its name, and under the name *Open the app ↗*, which opens the player; a player that has no launcher entry shows no link. A screen reader reads the row by the name, skipping the icon. In developer mode, its package name shows under its name too ([Developer](#developer)).

- **Default: Spotify on.** Spotify is listed once it is installed, switched on. Other players appear here by name, switched off, once one of them has reported music (a track with a title and an artist) while Sleevesight has notification access; switching one on follows it at once. Most video apps report no artist, so they are not listed. Until a player is listed, the section says *No players yet. A music app appears here, switched off, once you play music in it.*
- **An app you uninstall leaves the list.** When Android says the app was removed, its entry is dropped; installed again, it is listed again as any new player is, switched off, once it reports music. Spotify keeps its switch: switched off, it stays off when installed again.
- **A player Android does not show Sleevesight is hidden, not forgotten.** Sleevesight sees the apps that have a launcher icon, offer media browsing, or take media buttons, which almost every music app does. One it cannot see is listed only once it has reported music since Sleevesight started (by its name, without an icon), so a player you follow always has its switch while it plays. Otherwise it is hidden and keeps its switch: if it was uninstalled without Sleevesight hearing of it, it comes back as it was, on or off, when installed again.
- **A player that is off is not followed.** To find your music players, Sleevesight looks at apps that report what they play (playing or paused) and checks whether it is music, a track with a title and an artist. From an app not switched on, it reads what the app says it is playing only to tell whether it is music, and only until the app reports music; after that it reads nothing more of what the app plays until Sleevesight starts afresh (not each time you open it), or the app is uninstalled and installed again. A new player is added here switched off. Sleevesight does not track what a player that is off plays, and keeps or sends none of it; the player itself is kept in this list.
- **Calls are never read.** Calls from the phone app, and sessions Android marks as voice calls, are never read, whatever the switch. A calling app that shows its call as an ordinary media session is not recognised as a call. An app you follow that also makes calls (not the phone app, which is never read) may show leftover caller details as a track for a moment after a call, and a call Sleevesight did not see (after a restart, or while notification access is off) is read like any player's once Android no longer marks it.
- **No Spotify login.** Sleevesight reads the phone's media session, not Spotify's Web API, so following Spotify needs no Spotify account connection or developer app, whichever plan you have. Connecting a Spotify account, to see the whole playlist or album, is planned and not in the app yet.

## Sources

*External services providing information on artists and music.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight pulls information about the currently played music from services dedicated to music and from general information sources. Switch on the services you want Sleevesight to get information from.* Each service is a switch, with a line on what it is and, on a line of its own, *Open website ↗*. Each row starts with a plain badge, the size of a player's icon, showing the letter the home screen's chips and citations use for the source (*MB*, *W*, *L*, *G* for Exa, *M* for the AI service's knowledge); the rows under [Genres](#genres) have them too, and those under [AI services](#ai-services) their service's initial (*A*, *O*, *G*). A screen reader skips the badge and reads the row by its name. Letters, not the services' logos: each logo is another company's mark, with its own terms. A service that needs a key, Last.fm or Exa, has a link to it under its line, saved or not: *Set your Last.fm API key here.*, where *Last.fm API key* opens [Secrets](#secrets) and lands on that key's row, to save or change it. While the key is not saved, the service also says so, above the link: *Needs your Last.fm key.* The link lands on the key's row a quarter of the way down the screen, and marks it for a moment.

**Default: every service on, except the AI service's knowledge.** Turning a source off means nothing is sent to it and nothing from it reaches the AI service. What each one receives is the playing track's title and artists, cleaned of tags such as "2006 Remaster", unless said otherwise below; the album name goes to none of them. Each also sees your phone's IP address, as every service you connect to does. The credits, under [About](#about), list each source with its licence.

- ***MusicBrainz*** (no key). *An open music encyclopedia: recordings, releases, credits and release dates.* Identifies the recording and its artists, and gives its credits and release dates. It is also what the other sources' matches are checked against: with it off, they match by name only. It is asked at one request a second, as its rules ask, which makes it the slowest source. Its core data is under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/); its use is under the [MusicBrainz API's rules](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API) and [MetaBrainz's privacy policy](https://metabrainz.org/privacy). While *Proactive* is on, MusicBrainz also gets the next queued track's title and artists ([Insights](#insights)).
- ***Wikipedia and Wikidata*** (no key, one switch). *Articles on songs, albums and artists, and the facts behind them.* Sentences from Wikipedia articles about the song and the artists, and facts from Wikidata. Wikidata gets only identifiers (from MusicBrainz or Wikipedia), never a name. Wikipedia's text is by its contributors under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); Wikidata's data is under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/); both under the [Wikimedia Foundation's privacy policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Privacy_policy).
- ***Last.fm*** (your key). *Listeners' tags and artist biographies.* Artist bios, listeners, tags and similar artists. Last.fm gets the artists' names only, not the title. Without a key it is not asked, which is fine: the app works without it. Get a key by [creating an API account](https://www.last.fm/api/account/create) on Last.fm. Bios are by Last.fm users under CC BY-SA; the rest is under [Last.fm's API terms](https://www.last.fm/api/tos) and its [privacy policy](https://www.last.fm/legal/privacy).
- ***Exa web search*** (your key). *Searches the web for articles about the track.* Pages about the recording; Exa gets the title and artists, and the film or show a title says it is from. It searches only once its key is saved: the switch is for turning it off with its key saved, without removing the key. Get a key from the [Exa dashboard](https://dashboard.exa.ai/api-keys). Exa bills per search, with a monthly free credit ([What it costs you]({{ '/docs/costs/' | relative_url }})); its results are used under [Exa's terms of service](https://exa.ai/terms) and [privacy policy](https://exa.ai/privacy-policy). Another web search engine, once built, joins as a service of its own.
- ***AI service knowledge*** (**off by default**). *What the AI model chosen under AI services learned in training, frozen at that time. At most one claim, marked unverified.* *AI services* in it is a link that opens that section. With no AI service switched on, the row adds *No AI service is switched on at the moment, so this source is not used.* The model is the one chosen under [AI services](#ai-services). It sends nothing extra anywhere: it lets the AI service add one sentence from its own knowledge, which *Key insights* marks with an *M* chip instead of a source's, read by a screen reader as "From the AI model's own knowledge, not verified". Off by default because nothing checks such a sentence against a source. It has no website link.

Discogs, as a further source, is planned for a later phase and not in the app yet.

## AI services

*Large Language Model (LLM) services.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight uses LLMs to process, aggregate, and present information retrieved from sources. Use the settings to specify the model and effort; stronger models and higher effort provide better results at a higher cost. One service at a time. Switching off a service does not erase the most recently used model and effort settings.* The AI service writes the insights: a few sentences about the playing track, each tied to the evidence the sources found. Each service has a switch, its model and its reasoning effort; its key is under [Secrets](#secrets), and under the switch *Set your Anthropic API key here.* (or OpenAI's, or Google Gemini's) links to it: *Anthropic API key* opens *Secrets* and lands on that key's row, whether a key is saved or not. The services offered are Anthropic, OpenAI and Google; a local model, through Ollama, is planned and not in the app yet.

- **The switch.** One service at a time: switching one on switches the one on before off. With all of them off, no insight is written. **Default: all off.** The key and its bill are yours, so the app writes nothing until you choose a service and save its key. A service switched on without a saved key says *Needs your Anthropic key.* (or OpenAI's, or Google's), above its link to the key.
- **Switching off a service does not erase the most recently used model and effort settings**, nor its key, so switching back restores them.
- **What it receives.** The service switched on gets the playing track's title, artists and album, and what the sources found, with your key. Each insight is a request of its own, with no conversation kept between them, and only for the playing track: the next track in the queue may be looked up ahead, but nothing is written for it until it plays. The service handles the request under the terms of your account with it ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/policies/privacy-policy/), [Google](https://policies.google.com/privacy) with the [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)).

### The model

*Anthropic model* (and *OpenAI model*, *Google model*) shows the model in use by its name. **Default: each service's cheapest current text model**, marked *(default)*: Claude Haiku 5.5 (`claude-haiku-5-5`) for Anthropic, GPT-6 Luna (`gpt-6-luna`) for OpenAI, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite (`gemini-3.5-flash-lite`) for Google. An insight narrates a few lines of evidence in a few sentences, which needs no larger model, and the bill is yours.

Opening the row lists the service's models as the service itself reports them for your key, so the dialog sends your key to that service, and to no other, to ask for the list. Only models your key can use are offered: those in the list, less any the service has closed to new users (Anthropic's deprecated models, which still answer for an account that already uses them, and stay offered to it). Each model shows by its name, with its identifier under it, smaller. Anthropic and Google name their models in the list; OpenAI's list gives identifiers only, so Sleevesight names the OpenAI models it knows (`gpt-6-luna` as *GPT-6 Luna*), and shows any other by its identifier alone. The dialog says *Save the API key to list the service's models* without a key, and why when the list cannot be had (the key refused, no connection, the quota used up, the service not offered in your country). When the list cannot be had, the default stays offered, and so does a model you chose earlier; once it is had, each is offered only if the list holds it. Choosing the default keeps no choice of your own, so a later version that changes the default takes you with it.

**A model that is no longer available.** A service retires models, and a model can be listed and still refused for your account (OpenAI's organization verification to stream some models, a Google free tier with no quota for a model). So when the app starts with a service switched on, it asks the service for its list with your key, a request that costs nothing and carries nothing about your listening, and checks that the selected model, or the default, is in it; the model choice checks it too each time it lists the models, and so does every insight call the service answers that it does not know the model or refuses it for your account. When the model is not available, the service's model row and *Key insights* on Home say *The currently selected Anthropic model Claude Haiku 5.5 is not available. Please select another model or provider.* (its *AI services* opens the settings at that service's model row), and no insight is written, with that model or any other, until you choose another model or switch on another service. A model the service refused stays unavailable even though the list still holds it (OpenAI and Google list the models they refuse), until you choose another model or service, or save another key; each start of the app checks again. The check at start is not made while *Show insights* is off. A list that cannot be had (no connection, the key refused) says nothing about the model, and changes nothing.

### The reasoning effort

*Anthropic reasoning effort* (and the others): *How much the model may reason before it writes. Less is faster and cheaper.* Each service offers its own levels, as it names them, and *The service's default*, which sends no effort at all and leaves it to the service.

| Service | Levels offered | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic | *Low*, *Medium*, *High* | *Low* |
| OpenAI | *Low*, *Medium*, *High* | *Low* |
| Google | *Minimal*, *Low*, *Medium*, *High* | *Low* |

**Why low:** an insight is a few sentences from a few lines of evidence, and you wait for it on the now-playing screen, so speed matters more than depth. What keeps the text honest is the citation check the app runs on every sentence, not the model's deliberation. Low is also the least level every current model of each service takes, so the default means the same whichever model you choose, and cards from the services are compared at one effort. For more deliberate cards, raise it; for Google's fastest, choose *Minimal*.

### What it costs

Each insight written is a paid call on your key, now and then two. [What it costs you]({{ '/docs/costs/' | relative_url }}) gives each service's prices and free tier, and what an insight costs with the defaults. How many calls are made is up to the [Insights](#insights) settings: by default one for every track you play while the app is on screen, and none while it is not.

## Secrets

*Private API keys enabling external services.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight uses external services that require authentication via API keys, obtained from each service's own website.* Your keys are stored encrypted on this phone, and each is sent only to its own service. A key's service bills you on it, if it charges at all: [What it costs you]({{ '/docs/costs/' | relative_url }}) says what each one charges.

- ***Load keys***: *Save several API keys at once from one QR code.* Under it: *Make the QR code on a computer, at sleevesight.app/api-keys: the page sends your keys nowhere. More in the guide to loading keys.*, each a link: to the [page that makes the code]({{ '/api-keys.html' | relative_url }}), and to this guide's [part on loading keys](#several-keys-at-once-load-keys).
- ***Purge keys***, shown while any key is saved: *Safely delete all saved keys*. It opens a screen that says *These saved keys will be deleted from this phone:* and lists them in a table of two columns, *API Key* and *Ending*: a row a key, with its service (*Anthropic*, *OpenAI*, *Google Gemini*, *Last.fm*, *Exa*) and its last four characters in a code font (`a1B2`); TalkBack reads a row as one item, *Anthropic, ending a 1 B 2*. *Delete* and *Cancel* follow; *Delete* deletes them, and the phone's key for them with it, then says *These keys have been safely deleted:* above the same table, or, if a step failed, what was not, with *Try again*. *More about safe delete*, on the same screen, explains it: every saved key is removed from the phone's storage, and the key in the phone's Android Keystore that encrypted them is deleted, so no copy left behind can ever be decrypted. *Purging keys from Sleevesight does not revoke them. You can still use your API keys elsewhere if you keep another copy.* Until you enter a key again, the service it belongs to is not used. When a saved key can no longer be read, or a key could not be saved because the phone's key store failed, it says so instead: *A saved key cannot be read, or a key could not be saved: delete all saved keys, then enter them again.* Sleevesight never deletes your keys on its own. See [Where the keys are kept](#where-the-keys-are-kept).
- **Each service's key**: *Anthropic API key*, *OpenAI API key*, *Google Gemini API key*, *Last.fm API key* and *Exa API key*, each showing *Not set*, *Saved*, or *Saved, but it can no longer be read*; a saved key is never shown again. The links under the AI services and the sources that need a key (*Set your … API key here.*) land on these rows.
- ***Share phone's name with Type on a computer***, off by default: *Type on a computer lets you type an API key on a computer's keyboard, sent to the phone over Bluetooth: tap the keyboard icon in a key's field, with the page below open on the computer. Sharing the name lets the computer list the phone by its Bluetooth name while a session is open, instead of as "Unknown or Unsupported Device"; a name the phone cannot advertise is left out, and the session says so. Anyone nearby scanning for Bluetooth can see the name meanwhile.* Below it, a link to the page, [sleevesight.app/keyboard.html]({{ '/keyboard.html' | relative_url }}); [*Type on a computer*](#entering-a-key) says more. With it off, the phone advertises no name, and the computer lists it, the only device it lists, as *Unknown or Unsupported Device*. Off by default because the list holds the phone alone either way: the name only confirms it. A name is left out when it is too long or not set, or when the phone's Bluetooth refuses it; one a little long is shortened, and the dialog shows how it starts.

### The AI services' keys

Tapping an AI service's key row opens the key's dialog, which also says, in a line with a link to them, what the service's terms require: Anthropic's set no minimum age and say the API is not for consumer use, though Anthropic welcomes individuals and hobbyists on it; OpenAI's are for businesses and developers, and let minors use the API only with a parent's or guardian's consent; Google's require you to be 18 or older, and are for professional or business purposes, not for consumer use. The line opens, in bold, with the agreement: using the key means agreeing to them. The [privacy policy]({{ '/privacy.html#children-and-the-ai-services-own-terms' | relative_url }}) quotes them.

- **Anthropic:** create a key in the [Claude Console](https://platform.claude.com/settings/keys).
- **OpenAI:** create a key on the [OpenAI platform](https://platform.openai.com/api-keys).
- **Google:** create a Gemini API key in [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey). Google's [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) set what its free tier allows: on it, outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, Google uses what is sent to improve its products, and for apps made available to users in those three, the terms allow only the paid tier. A key from a Google Cloud project with billing on is on the paid tier.

The sources' keys, Last.fm's and Exa's, are described under [Sources](#sources).

### Entering a key

Tap a key's row. The dialog says who the key goes to (for Anthropic's: *Stored encrypted on this phone, and sent only to Anthropic. A saved key is never shown again. You can only preview the key you enter before it's saved.*), and offers a field and its ways to fill it: the field's icons are a QR code (*Scan QR code*) and a keyboard (*Type on a computer*), and, once something is entered, an eye. *Save* saves what the field holds; spaces around it, as a paste often brings, are dropped. The field is masked, what is typed is kept only in the dialog's memory, and the dialog, like every key screen, cannot be captured in a screenshot or a screen recording. The eye (*Show key*, then *Hide key*) shows what is in the field, to check what was typed, scanned or typed on a computer; the dialog never loads a saved key, so the eye can show only what was entered in it, and the field is masked again whenever the dialog closes or you leave the app. On a phone the dialog takes the screen's width, less a margin, to leave the field room for its icons.

- **Typed or pasted** into the field.
- **Filled from a password manager**: the field is a password to Android's autofill, so your password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, Google Password Manager and the like) offers its saved entries on it, as on any password field; pick the entry that holds the key. It fills but is never asked to save: when the dialog goes, it ends the autofill request unsaved, so the password manager does not offer to keep the key as a new login. To keep a key in it, add the key there yourself. A fill you ask for by hand (*Autofill* in the field's menu) while the field holds a key sends that key to the password manager with the request, as Android does in any app.
- ***Scan QR code***: opens a screen laid out as *Load keys*' is: *1. Generate a QR code of this one key on your computer, as the instructions explain:*, with a link to [sleevesight.app/api-keys]({{ '/api-keys.html' | relative_url }}) (paste only this key in its field there), the camera's view, *2. Read the QR code with the camera.*, and *Cancel*. It reads a QR code holding the key, either the key alone or the page's code of this one key, and fills the field with it once it has that service's form: an Anthropic key starts `sk-ant-`, an OpenAI key `sk-` but not `sk-ant-`, a Google key `AIza` or `AQ.`, a Last.fm key is 32 hexadecimal digits, an Exa key 16 to 200 characters. Otherwise it says why, and leaves the field as it was. A code that holds other keys, or lines this version cannot read, is pointed to *Load keys*; one from a newer version asks you to update the app. Sleevesight asks for the camera only when you scan, and reads the code on the phone; no camera frame is kept or sent.
- ***Type on a computer***: type the key on a computer's keyboard instead of the phone's. The keyboard icon opens a screen laid out as *Load keys*' is, which says what it is (*A page on the computer sends what you type to this phone over Bluetooth, encrypted, and it appears in the key's field. The computer needs Bluetooth, and Chrome or Edge.*) and its steps, with the camera's view between them: *1. On the computer, open the keyboard page:* (a link to [sleevesight.app/keyboard.html]({{ '/keyboard.html' | relative_url }})), *2. Read the QR code the page shows with the camera.*, *3. Then, on the page, choose “Connect” and pick this phone.* So: open the page on a computer with Bluetooth, in Chrome or Edge (other browsers have no Web Bluetooth); tap the keyboard icon and scan the code the page shows (the QR code icon, scanning it, starts the session too); then choose *Connect* on the page and pick the phone, the only device listed: as *Unknown or Unsupported Device*, or by its Bluetooth name with *Share phone's name with Type on a computer* on (above). The field follows what you type on the computer, up to 480 bytes, and *Save* saves it as usual; *Stop*, or closing the dialog, ends the session. The text goes over Bluetooth alone, encrypted with a key the page makes for that visit and shows only in its QR code; no server is involved, the page is allowed no network request, and the phone sends nothing back but Bluetooth's acknowledgements. Each code works once: reload the page for a new one. Sleevesight asks for Bluetooth (*Nearby devices*) only then.

Every key's dialog has *Cancel* on the left, and *Remove* and *Save* on the right. *Remove*, there only while a key is saved, is red, with a trash can icon, so that it cannot be taken for *Save*, and TalkBack names the key it removes (*Remove the Last.fm API key*). It asks first: the key is removed only once you confirm, and *Cancel* there keeps it. A saved key's row has a delete button too, which asks the same. Saving a new key over a saved one replaces it.

### Several keys at once: Load keys

*Load keys*, under *Secrets*, reads a setup code holding several keys, and saves them together.

1. On a computer, open [sleevesight.app/api-keys]({{ '/api-keys.html' | relative_url }}). Give it your keys file (`NAME=value` lines, such as `SLEEVESIGHT_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-ant-…`; each key named as Sleevesight names it or as its provider usually does: `SLEEVESIGHT_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `SLEEVESIGHT_OPENAI_API_KEY` or `OPENAI_API_KEY`, `SLEEVESIGHT_GOOGLE_API_KEY`, `GEMINI_API_KEY` or `GOOGLE_API_KEY`, `SLEEVESIGHT_EXA_API_KEY` or `EXA_API_KEY`, and `SLEEVESIGHT_LASTFM_API_KEY` or `LASTFM_API_KEY`, the first listed winning when a file gives a key under more than one), chosen or dropped onto the page, or paste the keys one per service. It checks each against its service's form, leaves out and names those that fail, and draws one QR code of the rest, listing each key by its last four characters. The page is allowed no network request and stores nothing: the keys are kept only while the tab is open, and *Clear* removes them.
2. On the phone, tap *Load keys*. Its screen says the two steps, with a link to the instructions, and shows the camera's view with a frame that takes most of the picture, a narrow dimmed margin around it: hold the code inside it. When the code holds an AI service's key, the list adds that using it means agreeing to that service's terms, which its key dialog states.
3. Before anything is saved, the camera's view gives way to what the code holds (*Save these keys?*): a table of the keys, as on *Purge keys*, each by its service and last four characters, marked *New* or *Replaces the saved key*, and, under *Problems*, the keys refused and why, any names this version does not know, and any lines that could not be read. With every key good, *Save* saves them; with some refused, *Save these* saves the good ones only, and *Scan again* reads another code; with nothing to save, *Scan again*. *Cancel* saves nothing.
4. The screen then says which keys were saved; *Done* closes it.

Like the key dialogs, the *Load keys* and *Purge keys* screens cannot be captured in a screenshot or a screen recording.

Never make such a code with an online QR generator: it would receive your keys. A code that is not a Sleevesight setup code is refused, and one from a newer version of the page asks you to update the app.

### Where the keys are kept

Your API keys are encrypted (AES-GCM) with a key kept in the phone's Android Keystore, which never leaves it, and stored in a file of their own, apart from the settings. They are never shown again once saved, and never logged. Neither they nor the settings are part of Android's backup or of a transfer to a new phone. Each key is sent only to its own service, with each request to it.

If a saved key can no longer be read, as after the phone's Keystore is reset, its row says *Saved, but it can no longer be read*, and you enter it again. On Android 11 and earlier, while any key is saved, entering one then fails, and *Purge keys*, under *Secrets*, is the way out. If the phone's key store cannot save a key, or keeps failing, the same *Purge keys* removes every key, so you can enter them afresh. If the phone's storage fails instead, the key dialog says so, and you try again.

*Current music genres.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight collects genre information from sources and compiles a short list of representative categories.*

The rest of each lookup is as before: Last.fm's biography, listeners and similar artists, and Wikidata's record labels, awards and dates, still reach the insight, and a sentence quoted from Last.fm, Wikipedia or a web page may still name a style in passing.
- **A switch per source of genre labels**, each with where its tags come from. **Default: both on**, Last.fm and MusicBrainz. Kept only when they name a genre: Sleevesight keeps a tag only when it is in its fixed list of genres. With both on, Last.fm's labels come first on each level (the track, each artist, the album) and MusicBrainz's fill the rest, five at most a level, and a label both give is shown once, crediting both. Web search gives no tags.
  - ***Last.fm***: *Tags added freely by listeners, unmoderated; Sleevesight keeps those that name a genre.* It needs its key, in *Secrets*: without one it says *Needs your Last.fm key, in Secrets*. *Open website ↗* opens Last.fm's home page, as its row under *Sources* does: Last.fm has a page per tag, but none that explains its tags.
  - ***MusicBrainz***: *Tags added and voted on by MusicBrainz contributors; Sleevesight keeps those that name a genre. CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; shown only, never sent to the AI service.* *Open website ↗* opens MusicBrainz's page on its tags, their votes and its genre list. Each label from them credits and links MusicBrainz. They are on by default because MusicBrainz needs no key, and a new user, who has no Last.fm key, would otherwise get no labels at all. ## Insights

*Generated current track summaries.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight collects track, artist, and album information from sources and uses AI to generate readable descriptions based on the sourced evidence.* When an insight is written, and what is looked up ahead.

- ***Show insights*** (**default: on**). *Insights on the playing track, written by your AI service from the sources' evidence. Each insight written is a paid call on your AI service key. Stop writing ends the call at once; if the request has reached your AI service, it still charges for what it has read and written so far. Look again, in the menu beside Write insight, looks every source up afresh, a paid search with Exa web search on, and writes nothing; when the sources found something new, Rewrite insight writes the insight from it, a paid call, only if you tap it. When disabled, no AI calls are made; the evidence is still shown.* The master switch: with it off, nothing is written, and the rows about writing (*Reactive*, *Crossfade*, *Saver*, *Don't repeat artist facts*) cannot be changed. *Proactive* still can, and still looks the next track up at MusicBrainz ahead of its play while it is on.
- ***Reactive*** (**default: *On track change (auto)***). *Select what triggers writing an insight. The arrow beside Write insight, under Key insights, sets it too. Choosing an automatic mode starts automatic writing from the next track.* That arrow, on the insight card, opens a menu of the same three choices, by name: choosing there sets *Reactive*, and choosing here shows in the menu; the menu's *See Settings for details* opens this row. After the app starts, nothing is written automatically until you act in it; choosing an automatic mode, here or in the menu, the current one included, is such an action. The choices, in order of how much is written automatically, each saying what it does under its name in the dialog:
  - *On demand* (once *Now*): *Only when you tap Write insight.* Nothing is written until you tap *Write insight* under *Key insights*; the sources are still looked up for each track.
  - *On track skip (auto)*: *Writes automatically only when you skip to a track, not when one follows another by itself.* A skip in your player, or a track you pick in Sleevesight; the app tells a skip from a natural end by watching the playback (see *Crossfade*).
  - *On track change (auto)*: *Writes automatically on every track change.* Its lookups are mostly done ahead.
- ***Crossfade*** (**default: *Autodetect***; or a fixed 0 to 15 seconds), shown under *Reactive* only while it is *On track skip (auto)*, the only choice that uses it. *The player's crossfade, for telling your skips from natural ends under "On track skip (auto)".* With *Autodetect*, the app learns Spotify's crossfade from its track changes, on the phone; for any other player it goes by timing alone, taking a change with up to 15 seconds left as a natural end, so a skip in the last 15 seconds is not told apart. A fixed value is for a player whose crossfade you know.
- ***Proactive*** (**default: on**). *Looks up the next track in the player's queue ahead, so its sources come sooner when it plays. No insight is written ahead, and nothing is paid for.* On, the next track's title and artists go to MusicBrainz, if it is on, before that track plays, so MusicBrainz, the slow source, has mostly answered by then. No insight is written ahead: the queue, as the app reads it, gives no album or length, so an insight written from it could not be reused when the track plays, and would be paid for twice. Off sends nothing about the next track.
- ***Saver*** (**default: on, after 5 track changes**). *Stops automatic writing after a number of track changes while Sleevesight is not on screen, counted again from each time you open it. Opening Sleevesight resumes it.* Under it, while on, *Stop after*: 3, 5 or 10 track changes away. Switched on again, it is 5. Only while something is written automatically; otherwise it cannot be changed.
  - Why it is there: after the app starts, nothing is written until your first action in it. That action then lasts as long as the app's process, which the notification listener usually keeps alive after you leave the app, often for days: coming back from the player, with the screen turned on again, or from the launcher, keeps it, and writing goes on without a new action. A restart of the phone, an update of the app, or the system ending the process (on some phones, a swipe from Recents) starts afresh. So one action could keep paying for days.
  - With *Look up in the background* on, *Stop after* says: *While Sleevesight is in the background, it writes insights automatically for up to 5 track changes. After more than 5, it stops writing insights automatically until you open it again.* From the sixth on, nothing more is written. Opening the app resumes automatic writing from the next track; the track playing then, which played while it was paused, offers *Write insight*, with a line saying why it was not written. Those five are paid calls made while nobody is looking.
  - With *Look up in the background* off, as by default, nothing is written while you are away anyway, and *Stop after* says: *If Sleevesight is brought to the foreground within 5 track changes, it goes on writing insights automatically. After more than 5, the playing track's insight is written only when you tap Write insight, and automatic writing resumes from the next track.* Coming back within five writes the track playing; after more, that track waits for your tap, and the next ones are written as before.
  - Opening the app starts the count again, so coming back after exactly five keeps writing.
  - Off, automatic writing never pauses.
- ***Look up in the background*** (**default: off**). *Allows Sleevesight to look up information from sources while it is in the background, for every track played. With Reactive set to On track change (auto) or On track skip (auto), insights are then written, and paid for, whether you read them or not, up to the Stop after limit when Saver is on.* Under *On demand*, no AI call is made in the background; the sources are still looked up. When disabled, lookups and insights happen only while Sleevesight is on screen. Off by default, so there is no paid or network activity that nobody is looking at. On, the sources are asked, and insights are written automatically (within *Saver*), while the app is in the background too. It sits beside *Saver*, since both govern what happens while Sleevesight is away.
- ***Don't repeat artist facts*** (**default: on**). *When you play several tracks by the same artist, each insight would tell the artist's background again. With this on, an insight leaves out what an earlier one in the same listening session already said about the artist, and focuses on the song when there is enough to say about it. A new session starts after a break in listening, set under New session idle trigger in History.* *New session idle trigger* in it is a link that opens *History* and lands on that row. What the sources say about an artist is the same on every track by that artist; played through an album, each insight would otherwise retell it, most of all where the songs themselves are thinly documented. On, each fact is told once per listening session (see [*New session idle trigger*](#history) under *History*), and a later track's insight is shorter rather than repeated; a track with four or more lines about the song itself gets no artist facts at all, since its insight hardly used them. When leaving the facts out would leave nothing to write from, the insight gets them all. Off, every insight is written with all of its artist's facts, so each one introduces the artist, and repeats what the one before it said. Either way the facts are kept per artist: a track by another artist, on a compilation or as a featured artist, gets that artist's facts the first time the artist comes up. Like the rows about writing, it cannot be changed while *Show insights* is off.

Per-aspect switches (the highlights, the track, the artist, the album) wait for the song, artist and album cards of a later phase: today there is one insight. A setting for the insights' style, with presets or your own words for their length and tone, is planned; it will not loosen the citation rules.

## History

*Listening history, kept on this phone.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight keeps information about the tracks you play on this phone (titles, artists, albums, and when they played), to avoid repeating itself within a listening session and to learn which track changes are your skips. Nothing of it leaves the phone.* It is a record about the tracks, never the music itself. The history does not reduce traffic to the sources or the AI service: the kept findings below do, and they are not part of it.

- ***Keep history for*** (**default: 90 days**; also 7, 30 or 365 days, or *Forever*). *How long Sleevesight keeps, on this phone, the list of tracks you played, the track changes recorded to learn your skips, and the insights and genre explanations written for them, counted from when each was played or written. Anything older is no longer shown and is deleted. A shorter time applies at once.* How long the list of tracks you played (titles, artists, albums, and when each played) and the track changes are kept, counted back from today, and with them the insights and genre explanations written, counted from when each was written: one older is not shown again, and is deleted at the app's next start. The track changes are what skip detection learns from (today, Spotify's crossfade; learning from rarer signals, such as a tap on *Write insight*, is planned), so it wants a season of them rather than a week; 90 days keeps them to a few MB. A week is the least offered: shorter would empty the list of someone who listens once a week. A shorter lifetime applies at once.
- ***Clear history***. *Deletes everything Sleevesight kept about the tracks you played*, once you confirm: *The list of tracks you played, the track changes recorded to learn your skips, the insights and genre explanations written, and what the sources found are deleted from this phone. Your API keys and settings stay. Your music is not touched. This cannot be undone.* It deletes, at once, the list of tracks played and the track changes, with what skip detection learned from them; the insights and cards written, the genre explanations among them; what each source found for a track; what the sources said about each artist, and the artists' MusicBrainz identities; and what is held in memory about them (MusicBrainz's answers of the last half hour, the artist facts the listening session's insights told, the diagnostics' steps). The insight on Home goes at once. A lookup or an insight under way is stopped, and nothing it finds is kept. The track playing is not looked up again on its own, so no paid call follows the clear; the next track to play is looked up afresh. It sits right under *Keep history for*, which offers *Forever*, so the way to wipe the history is beside how long it is kept.
- ***New session idle trigger*** (**default: 30 minutes**; also 10, 20 or 60 minutes, or 2 hours). *A track that starts after a longer break begins a new listening session, counted from when the last track was heard. Earlier sessions stay in your history, for as long as "Keep history for" is set. Within a session, each artist fact is told once, unless "Don't repeat artist facts" is off. A change applies from the next track that starts.* Longer than a pause to answer the door or change trains, shorter than the gap between the morning's listening and the evening's. No play is discarded: sessions only group the plays kept. An artist fact the AI has already told in this session is not offered again for the next track by the same artist, while *Don't repeat artist facts* is on (under *Insights*).
- ***How long each source's findings are kept.*** What each source found for a track is kept on the phone for a while, so the same track is not looked up again, and is not asked for again from a paid search. Each source has its own lifetime, chosen from 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 or 90 days. **Defaults:** *MusicBrainz* and *Wikidata* 30 days, whose data changes slowly; *Wikipedia* and *Last.fm* 7 days, whose articles and bios are edited more often; *Web search* 1 day. A shorter lifetime means fresher findings and more requests; a longer one, fewer requests. Old entries are deleted at each start of the app, and are never used once expired.
- ***Keep what sources said about each artist*** (**default: off**). *Keeps what MusicBrainz, Wikipedia and Last.fm said about each artist on this phone, so an artist is not looked up again for every track. This keeps a list of the artists you have heard on the phone. When disabled, each track's artists are looked up again. Each track's own lookups send its artists' names either way. Turning it off deletes what was kept.* A privacy choice, so its title is shown in the theme's error colour. What the sources say about an artist (Last.fm's answers and tags, the Wikipedia artist article and its Wikidata facts, MusicBrainz's tags), kept together, amounts to a record of the artists you have played, so it is kept only if you choose.
  - **On:** what the sources say about each artist you play stays on the phone for as long as its sources' findings, so another track by the same artist does not look the artist up again, and fewer requests leave the phone. An artist's MusicBrainz identity is then kept for 90 days, a fixed lifetime. Each track's own lookups still send its artists' names.
  - **Off:** none of it is kept, per artist or with a track's findings (nor the sources' verdicts on artists' pages, nor the artists' MusicBrainz ids). Each track's artists are looked up again, and their names go to the sources you switch on with every track. Of a track's findings, only the web search's (pages about the recording, which may name or describe its artists) and those of a lookup that found no artist to look up are kept. So a track played before is looked up again at MusicBrainz (its match held in memory for half an hour only), Wikipedia and Last.fm, unless it has no credited artist; lookups are slower, and more requests go out.
  - **Either way**, what stays is the record of which tracks you played (the history's list of them, and the keys of the kept findings) and the insights written, which may retell some of what the sources said. MusicBrainz's answers are held in the app's memory for half an hour, for the track about to play, and never written to storage.
  - **Turning it off deletes what was kept**, and so does every start of the app while it is off, which also removes what earlier versions kept without asking.

Old entries go by the phone's clock. Each time Sleevesight starts, with the phone or after it was stopped, it takes the clock as right; if the clock is then moved by more than an hour, ahead or back, nothing is deleted until it is set right again. So a clock set ahead before Sleevesight starts deletes old entries by that clock.

Turning it off turns *Diagnostics* and *Show grounding details* off, sets *Report button* to *Hide*, *Link style* back to *Underline* and *Mark developer items* back on, so nothing hidden keeps running, and hides the section until the next seven taps.
- In developer mode, each player under *Players* also shows its package name under its name.

The diagnostics screen shows what Sleevesight did in its last few hundred steps since it started (each source's outcome and time, cache hits, AI calls, the citation check's counts), with the caches' sizes; the track changes recorded, which skip detection learns from; and what skip detection has learned, per player, with *Reset*, which makes it forget until it learns again and keeps the track changes. The steps are kept in memory only, and are gone when the app's process ends. *Export* writes all of it, every track change included, to a file you choose, which may be a cloud folder; that copy is yours, outside the app. Nothing else of it leaves the phone.

## What stays on the phone

Everything the app keeps is on the phone, and none of it is part of Android's backup or a transfer to a new phone. In short, with how long each is kept and how to clear it:

| What | Kept for | Cleared by |
|---|---|---|
| The history: the list of tracks you played (titles, artists, albums, when each played, and each track's length), and the track changes | *Keep history for* (90 days by default) | *Clear history* |
| What skip detection has learned, per player | Until reset | *Reset* on the diagnostics screen; *Clear history* |
| What each source found for a track | *How long each source's findings are kept* | Expiry, at the next start; *Clear history* |
| What the sources said about each artist | Only with *Keep what sources said about each artist* on; as long as its sources' findings, an artist's MusicBrainz identity 90 days | Turning that switch off; *Clear history* |
| The insights written | As long as *Keep history for* says, unless the caches' size limit (a fixed 50 MB) pushes out the least recently used first | Expiry, at the next start; *Clear history*; Android's *Clear storage* |
| The genre explanations written (*Why is this …?*) | As long as *Keep history for* says, within the same size limit | Expiry, at the next start; *Clear history*; Android's *Clear storage* |
| What each genre is, from Wikipedia | Wikipedia's lifetime under *How long each source's findings are kept* | Expiry; *Clear history*; Android's *Clear storage* |
| The diagnostics' steps | The app's current run, in memory only | Ending the app; *Clear history* |
| Your settings and API keys | Until you change or remove them | *Purge keys*; Android's *Clear storage* |

Android's *Clear storage* for Sleevesight, or uninstalling it, deletes everything it kept.
