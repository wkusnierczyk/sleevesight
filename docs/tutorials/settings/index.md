# Settings

The settings screen opens from the cogwheel. This page goes through it from top to bottom, in the order the screen shows, and says for each setting what it does, what it is set to at first and why, and what changing it sends or keeps. The [usage guide]({{ '/docs/tutorials/usage/' | relative_url }}) says how the rest of the app works, and the [privacy policy]({{ '/privacy.html' | relative_url }}) what leaves the phone, to whom.

<!-- screenshot: the settings screen with every section folded, from the app version it is taken from -->

A few things hold for the whole screen:

- **The settings are in sections, each folded.** Each section is a heading with a triangle, ▸ while folded and ▾ while unfolded, and a short description under it; a tap anywhere on it unfolds the section, with a fuller explanation where one is needed, then its settings, and a second tap folds it again. Every section is folded when the settings open; while you stay, each stays as you left it, through a rotation too. A screen reader reads each heading as a heading, with "collapsed" or "expanded". The sections are *Players*, *Sources*, *AI services*, *Secrets*, *Genres*, *Insights*, *History*, *Appearance* and *About*, and, only in developer mode, *Developer*.
- **Each change is saved at once.** There is no *Save* button for settings. If a change cannot be saved, because the phone's storage failed, the setting keeps its saved value, and a line above the list says *The last change could not be saved*; try again.
- **Some rows depend on others.** A row that has no effect under the current choices is shown but cannot be changed: *Reactive* while *Show insights* is off, for example, or a genre source while *Show genres* is off. Some say why.
- **Links.** *Open the app ↗* and *Open website ↗* open another app or a page in your browser; a screen reader names what each opens ("Open Spotify", "Open the MusicBrainz website").
- **The labels in italics** on this page are the labels on the screen.
- **Settings and keys stay on the phone.** Neither is part of Android's backup or of a transfer to a new phone.

## Players

*Music apps Sleevesight follows.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight reads what a player says it is playing (track, artist, album). It does not receive the audio or your other notifications. Android grants this with notification access. Switch on the players you want Sleevesight to follow.* Each player is a switch, by its name, and under the name *Open the app ↗*, which opens the player; a player that has no launcher entry shows no link. In developer mode, its package name shows under its name too ([Developer](#developer)).

- **Default: Spotify on.** Other players appear here by name, switched off, once one of them has reported music (a track with a title and an artist) while Sleevesight has notification access; switching one on follows it at once. Most video apps report no artist, so they are not listed.
- **A player not switched on** is only checked for a title and an artist, to be listed here; nothing of it is kept or sent.
- **Calls are never read.** Calls from the phone app, and sessions Android marks as voice calls, are never read, whatever the switch. A calling app that shows its call as an ordinary media session is not recognised as a call. An app you follow that also makes calls (not the phone app, which is never read) may show leftover caller details as a track for a moment after a call, and a call Sleevesight did not see (after a restart, or while notification access is off) is read like any player's once Android no longer marks it.
- **No Spotify login.** Sleevesight reads the phone's media session, not Spotify's Web API, so following Spotify needs no Spotify account connection or developer app, whichever plan you have. Connecting a Spotify account, to see the whole playlist or album, is planned and not in the app yet.

## Sources

*External services providing information on artists and music.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight pulls information about the currently played music from services dedicated to music and from general information sources. Switch on the services you want Sleevesight to get information from.* Each service is a switch, with a line on what it is and, on a line of its own, *Open website ↗*. A service that needs a key you have not saved says so under its line: *Needs your Last.fm key, in Secrets.* The keys themselves are under [Secrets](#secrets).

**Default: every service on, except the AI service's knowledge.** Turning a source off means nothing is sent to it and nothing from it reaches the AI service. What each one receives is the playing track's title and artists, cleaned of tags such as "2006 Remaster", unless said otherwise below; the album name goes to none of them. Each also sees your phone's IP address, as every service you connect to does. The credits, under [About](#about), list each source with its licence.

- ***MusicBrainz*** (no key). *An open music encyclopedia: recordings, releases, credits and release dates.* Identifies the recording and its artists, and gives its credits and release dates. It is also what the other sources' matches are checked against: with it off, they match by name only. It is asked at one request a second, as its rules ask, which makes it the slowest source. Its core data is under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/); its use is under the [MusicBrainz API's rules](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API) and [MetaBrainz's privacy policy](https://metabrainz.org/privacy). While *Proactive* is on, MusicBrainz also gets the next queued track's title and artists ([Insights](#insights)).
- ***Wikipedia and Wikidata*** (no key, one switch). *Articles on songs, albums and artists, and the facts behind them.* Sentences from Wikipedia articles about the song and the artists, and facts from Wikidata. Wikidata gets only identifiers (from MusicBrainz or Wikipedia), never a name. Wikipedia's text is by its contributors under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); Wikidata's data is under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/); both under the [Wikimedia Foundation's privacy policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Privacy_policy).
- ***Last.fm*** (your key). *Listeners' tags and artist biographies.* Artist bios, listeners, tags and similar artists. Last.fm gets the artists' names only, not the title. Without a key it is not asked, which is fine: the app works without it. Get a key by [creating an API account](https://www.last.fm/api/account/create) on Last.fm. Bios are by Last.fm users under CC BY-SA; the rest is under [Last.fm's API terms](https://www.last.fm/api/tos) and its [privacy policy](https://www.last.fm/legal/privacy).
- ***Exa web search*** (your key). *Searches the web for articles about the track.* Pages about the recording; Exa gets the title and artists, and the film or show a title says it is from. It searches only once its key is saved: the switch is for turning it off with its key saved, without removing the key. Get a key from the [Exa dashboard](https://dashboard.exa.ai/api-keys). Exa bills per search, with a monthly free credit at the time of writing ([Exa's pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing)); its results are used under [Exa's terms of service](https://exa.ai/terms) and [privacy policy](https://exa.ai/privacy-policy). Another web search engine, once built, joins as a service of its own.
- ***AI service knowledge*** (**off by default**). *What the AI model learned in training, frozen at that time. At most one claim, marked unverified.* It sends nothing extra anywhere: it lets the AI service add one sentence from its own knowledge, which the card marks with an *M* chip instead of a source's, read by a screen reader as "From the model's own knowledge, not verified". Off by default because nothing checks such a sentence against a source. It has no website link.

Discogs, as a further source, is planned for a later phase and not in the app yet.

## AI services

*Large Language Model (LLM) services.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight uses LLMs to process, aggregate, and present information retrieved from sources. Use the settings to specify the model and effort; stronger models and higher effort provide better results at a higher cost.* The AI service writes the insights: a few sentences about the playing track, each tied to the evidence the sources found. Each service has a switch, its model and its reasoning effort; its key is under [Secrets](#secrets). The services offered are Anthropic, OpenAI and Google; a local model, through Ollama, is planned and not in the app yet.

- **The switch.** One service at a time: switching one on switches the one on before off. With all of them off, no insight is written. **Default: all off.** The key and its bill are yours, so the app writes nothing until you choose a service and save its key. A service switched on without a saved key says *Needs your Anthropic key, in Secrets* (or OpenAI's, or Google's).
- **A service switched off keeps its model and effort**, and its key, so switching back restores them.
- **What it receives.** The service switched on gets the playing track's title, artists and album, and what the sources found, with your key. Each insight is a request of its own, with no conversation kept between them, and only for the playing track: the next track in the queue may be looked up ahead, but nothing is written for it until it plays. The service handles the request under the terms of your account with it ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/policies/privacy-policy/), [Google](https://policies.google.com/privacy) with the [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)).

### The model

*Anthropic model* (and *OpenAI model*, *Google model*) shows the model in use by its name. **Default: each service's cheapest current text model**, marked *(default)*: Claude Haiku 5.5 (`claude-haiku-5-5`) for Anthropic, GPT-6 Luna (`gpt-6-luna`) for OpenAI, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite (`gemini-3.5-flash-lite`) for Google. An insight narrates a few lines of evidence in a few sentences, which needs no larger model, and the bill is yours.

Opening the row lists the service's models as the service itself reports them for your key, so the dialog sends your key to that service, and to no other, to ask for the list. Each model shows by its name, with its identifier under it, smaller. Anthropic and Google name their models in the list; OpenAI's list gives identifiers only, so Sleevesight names the OpenAI models it knows (`gpt-6-luna` as *GPT-6 Luna*), and shows any other by its identifier alone. The dialog says *Save the API key to list the service's models* without a key, and why when the list cannot be had (the key refused, no connection, the quota used up, the service not offered in your country). The default stays offered either way, and so does a model you chose earlier. Choosing the default keeps no choice of your own, so a later version that changes the default takes you with it.

### The reasoning effort

*Anthropic reasoning effort* (and the others): *How much the model may reason before it writes. Less is faster and cheaper.* Each service offers its own levels, as it names them, and *The service's default*, which sends no effort at all and leaves it to the service.

| Service | Levels offered | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic | *Low*, *Medium*, *High* | *Low* |
| OpenAI | *Low*, *Medium*, *High* | *Low* |
| Google | *Minimal*, *Low*, *Medium*, *High* | *Low* |

**Why low:** an insight is a few sentences from a few lines of evidence, and you wait for it on the now-playing screen, so speed matters more than depth. What keeps the text honest is the citation check the app runs on every sentence, not the model's deliberation. Low is also the least level every current model of each service takes, so the default means the same whichever model you choose, and cards from the services are compared at one effort. For more deliberate cards, raise it; for Google's fastest, choose *Minimal*.

### What it costs

Each insight written is a paid call on your key, now and then two (the [usage page]({{ '/docs/tutorials/usage/' | relative_url }}) says when). What a call costs is set by the service's price for the model you choose: the fixed instructions, the track and its evidence go in, and a few sentences come out, more with a higher reasoning effort, which the services bill as output. Check your service's pricing page for the model you use. How many calls are made is up to the [Insights](#insights) settings: by default one for every track you play while the app is on screen, and none while it is not.

## Secrets

*Private API keys enabling external services.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight uses external services that require authentication via API keys, obtained from each service's own website.* Your keys are stored encrypted on this phone, and each is sent only to its own service.

- ***Load keys***: *Read multiple secrets from a QR code.* ([below](#several-keys-at-once-load-keys)).
- ***Purge keys***, shown while any key is saved: *Safely delete all saved keys*, once you confirm, and the phone's key for them with it. Until you enter a key again, the service it belongs to is not used. When a saved key can no longer be read, or a key could not be saved because the phone's key store failed, it says so instead: *A saved key cannot be read, or a key could not be saved: delete all saved keys, then enter them again.* Sleevesight never deletes your keys on its own. See [Where the keys are kept](#where-the-keys-are-kept).
- **Each service's key**: *Anthropic API key*, *OpenAI API key*, *Google Gemini API key*, *Last.fm API key* and *Exa API key*, each showing *Not set*, *Saved*, or *Saved, but it can no longer be read*; a saved key is never shown again.
- ***Show this phone's name to the computer***, off by default: for [*Type on a computer*](#entering-a-key). On, while a session runs, the phone advertises its Bluetooth name, so the computer's list of devices shows the phone by it; anyone nearby scanning for Bluetooth can see the name meanwhile. Off, the phone advertises no name, and the computer lists it, the only device it lists, as *Unknown or Unsupported Device*. Off by default because the list holds the phone alone either way: the name only confirms it. A name the phone cannot advertise, such as one too long, is left out, and the session's dialog says so; one a little long is shortened, and the dialog shows how it starts.

### The AI services' keys

Tapping an AI service's key row opens the key's dialog, which also says, in a line with a link to them, what the service's terms require: Anthropic's set no minimum age and say the API is not for consumer use, though Anthropic welcomes individuals and hobbyists on it; OpenAI's are for businesses and developers, and let minors use the API only with a parent's or guardian's consent; Google's require you to be 18 or older, and are for professional or business purposes, not for consumer use. Using the key means agreeing to them. The [privacy policy]({{ '/privacy.html#children-and-the-ai-services-own-terms' | relative_url }}) quotes them.

- **Anthropic:** create a key in the [Claude Console](https://platform.claude.com/settings/keys).
- **OpenAI:** create a key on the [OpenAI platform](https://platform.openai.com/api-keys).
- **Google:** create a Gemini API key in [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey). Google's [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) set what its free tier allows: on it, outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, Google uses what is sent to improve its products, and for apps made available to users in those three, the terms allow only the paid tier. A key from a Google Cloud project with billing on is on the paid tier.

The sources' keys, Last.fm's and Exa's, are described under [Sources](#sources).

### Entering a key

Tap a key's row. The dialog says who the key goes to (for Anthropic's: *Stored encrypted on this phone, and sent only to Anthropic. A saved key is never shown again. You can only preview the key you enter before it's saved.*), and offers a field and its ways to fill it: the field's icons are a QR code (*Scan QR code*) and a keyboard (*Type on a computer*), and, once something is entered, an eye. *Save* saves what the field holds; spaces around it, as a paste often brings, are dropped. The field is masked, what is typed is kept only in the dialog's memory, and the dialog, like every key screen, cannot be captured in a screenshot or a screen recording. The eye (*Show key*, then *Hide key*) shows what is in the field, to check what was typed, scanned or typed on a computer; the dialog never loads a saved key, so the eye can show only what was entered in it, and the field is masked again whenever the dialog closes or you leave the app. On a phone the dialog takes the screen's width, less a margin, to leave the field room for its icons.

- **Typed or pasted** into the field.
- **Filled from a password manager**: the field is a password to Android's autofill, so your password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, Google Password Manager and the like) offers its saved entries on it, as on any password field; pick the entry that holds the key. It fills but is never asked to save: when the dialog goes, it ends the autofill request unsaved, so the password manager does not offer to keep the key as a new login. To keep a key in it, add the key there yourself. A fill you ask for by hand (*Autofill* in the field's menu) while the field holds a key sends that key to the password manager with the request, as Android does in any app.
- ***Scan QR code***: reads a QR code holding the key, and fills the field with it once it has that service's form: an Anthropic key starts `sk-ant-`, an OpenAI key `sk-` but not `sk-ant-`, a Google key `AIza` or `AQ.`, a Last.fm key is 32 hexadecimal digits, an Exa key 16 to 200 characters. Otherwise it says why, and leaves the field as it was. A code of several keys is pointed to *Load keys*. Sleevesight asks for the camera only when you scan, and reads the code on the phone; no camera frame is kept or sent.
- ***Type on a computer***: type the key on a computer's keyboard instead of the phone's. Open [sleevesight.app/keyboard.html]({{ '/keyboard.html' | relative_url }}) on a computer with Bluetooth, in Chrome or Edge (other browsers have no Web Bluetooth); tap the keyboard icon (*Type on a computer*) and scan the code the page shows (the QR code icon, scanning it, starts the session too); then choose *Connect* on the page and pick the phone, the only device listed: as *Unknown or Unsupported Device*, or by its Bluetooth name with *Show this phone's name to the computer* on (above). The field follows what you type on the computer, up to 480 bytes, and *Save* saves it as usual; *Stop*, or closing the dialog, ends the session. The text goes over Bluetooth alone, encrypted with a key the page makes for that visit and shows only in its QR code; no server is involved, the page is allowed no network request, and the phone sends nothing back but Bluetooth's acknowledgements. Each code works once: reload the page for a new one. Sleevesight asks for Bluetooth (*Nearby devices*) only then.

A saved key's row has a delete button that removes that key once you confirm; so does *Remove* in its dialog. Saving a new key over a saved one replaces it.

### Several keys at once: Load keys

*Load keys*, under *Secrets*, reads a setup code holding several keys, and saves them together.

1. On a computer, open [sleevesight.app/api-keys]({{ '/api-keys.html' | relative_url }}). Give it your keys file (`NAME=value` lines, such as `SLEEVESIGHT_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-ant-…`), chosen or dropped onto the page, or paste the keys one per service. It checks each against its service's form, leaves out and names those that fail, and draws one QR code of the rest, listing each key by its last four characters. The page is allowed no network request and stores nothing: the keys are kept only while the tab is open, and *Clear* removes them.
2. On the phone, tap *Load keys* and point the camera at the code. When the code holds an AI service's key, the list adds that using it means agreeing to that service's terms, which its key dialog states.
3. Before anything is saved, the app lists what it found (*Save these keys?*), each key by its last four characters, says which would replace a key already saved, and names the keys refused and any names this version does not know. *Save* saves them.

Never make such a code with an online QR generator: it would receive your keys. A code that is not a Sleevesight setup code is refused, and one from a newer version of the page asks you to update the app.

### Where the keys are kept

Your API keys are encrypted (AES-GCM) with a key kept in the phone's Android Keystore, which never leaves it, and stored in a file of their own, apart from the settings. They are never shown again once saved, and never logged. Neither they nor the settings are part of Android's backup or of a transfer to a new phone. Each key is sent only to its own service, with each request to it.

If a saved key can no longer be read, as after the phone's Keystore is reset, its row says *Saved, but it can no longer be read*, and you enter it again. On Android 11 and earlier, while any key is saved, entering one then fails, and *Purge keys*, under *Secrets*, is the way out. If the phone's key store cannot save a key, or keeps failing, the same *Purge keys* removes every key, so you can enter them afresh. If the phone's storage fails instead, the key dialog says so, and you try again.

## Genres

*Current music genres.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight collects genre information from sources and compiles a short list of representative categories.*

- ***Show genres*** (**default: on**). *Off: no genre information is asked for from any source, and the home screen shows no genres.* Off, Sleevesight asks no source for tags: not MusicBrainz for the recording's or the artists', nor Last.fm for the artists' top tags or the track's and the album's; and the home screen shows no genres at all. The rest of each lookup is as before, so the insight does not change.
- **A switch per source of genre labels**, each with where its tags come from. **Default: both on**, Last.fm and MusicBrainz. Kept only when they name a genre: Sleevesight keeps a tag only when it is in its fixed list of genres. With both on, Last.fm's labels come first on each level (the track, each artist, the album) and MusicBrainz's fill the rest, five at most a level, and a label both give is shown once, crediting both. Web search gives no tags.
  - ***Last.fm***: *Tags added freely by listeners, unmoderated; Sleevesight keeps those that name a genre.* It needs its key, in *Secrets*: without one it says *Needs your Last.fm key, in Secrets*. Last.fm has a page per tag, but none that explains its tags, so it has no website link.
  - ***MusicBrainz***: *Tags added and voted on by MusicBrainz contributors; Sleevesight keeps those that name a genre. CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; shown only, never sent to the AI service.* *Open website ↗* opens MusicBrainz's page on its tags, their votes and its genre list. Each label from them credits and links MusicBrainz. They are on by default because MusicBrainz needs no key, and a new user, who has no Last.fm key, would otherwise get no labels at all. Whether they stay on by default in the Google Play release depends on MetaBrainz's answer about how its tags may be used ([#100](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/100)).
- **A source gives labels only while *Show genres* is on and the source is on under *Sources***, since the tags come with its lookup: until then its switch shows off, cannot be changed, and says *Off while "Show genres" is off* or *Off under "Sources"*.

## Insights

*Generated current track summaries.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight collects track, artist, and album information from sources and uses AI to generate readable descriptions based on the sourced evidence.* When an insight is written, and what is looked up ahead.

- ***Show insights*** (**default: on**). Off: no AI calls at all; the evidence is still shown. The master switch: with it off, nothing is written, and the rows about writing (*Reactive*, *Crossfade*, *Saver*, *Tell an artist's facts once per session*) cannot be changed. *Proactive* still can, and still looks the next track up at MusicBrainz ahead of its play while it is on.
- ***Reactive*** (**default: *On track change***). *When an insight is written without a tap. Each insight written is a paid call on your AI service key.*
  - *On track change*: an insight is written as each track starts, with its lookups mostly done ahead.
  - *On skip*: only when you skip a track, which the app tells from a natural end by watching the playback (see *Crossfade*).
  - *Manual: only when I tap Write*: nothing is written until you tap *Write insight* on the card.
- ***Crossfade*** (**default: *Autodetect***; or a fixed 0 to 15 seconds), shown under *Reactive* only while it is *On skip*, the only choice that uses it. *The player's crossfade, for telling your skips from natural ends under "On skip".* With *Autodetect*, the app learns Spotify's crossfade from its track changes, on the phone; for any other player it goes by timing alone, taking a change with up to 15 seconds left as a natural end, so a skip in the last 15 seconds is not told apart. A fixed value is for a player whose crossfade you know.
- ***Proactive*** (**default: on**). *Looks up the next track in the player's queue ahead, so its sources come sooner when it plays. No insight is written ahead, and nothing is paid for.* On, the next track's title and artists go to MusicBrainz, if it is on, before that track plays, so MusicBrainz, the slow source, has mostly answered by then. No insight is written ahead: the queue, as the app reads it, gives no album or length, so an insight written from it could not be reused when the track plays, and would be paid for twice. Off sends nothing about the next track.
- ***Saver*** (**default: on, after 5 track changes**). *Stops automatic writing after a number of track changes while Sleevesight is not on screen, counted again from each time you open it.* Under it, while on, *Stop after*: 3, 5 or 10 track changes away. Switched on again, it is 5. Only while something is written automatically; otherwise it cannot be changed.
  - Why it is there: after the app starts, nothing is written until your first action in it. That action then lasts as long as the app's process, which the notification listener usually keeps alive after you leave the app, often for days: coming back from the player, with the screen turned on again, or from the launcher, keeps it, and writing goes on without a new tap. A restart of the phone, an update of the app, or the system ending the process (on some phones, a swipe from Recents) starts afresh. So one tap could keep paying for days.
  - With *Look up in the background* on, up to five tracks are written while you are away, and from the sixth on, nothing more is written until you tap *Write insight*, with a line on the card saying why. Those five are paid calls made while nobody is looking.
  - With *Look up in the background* off, as by default, nothing is written while you are away anyway; the count decides whether coming back writes the track playing (back within five track changes) or waits for your tap (back after more). The text under *Stop after* says which applies.
  - Opening the app starts the count again, so coming back after exactly five keeps writing.
  - Off, automatic writing never pauses.
- ***Look up in the background*** (**default: off**). *Off: lookups and insights only while Sleevesight is on screen. On: insights written automatically are paid calls on your key, made while Sleevesight is not on screen.* Off by default, so there is no paid or network activity that nobody is looking at. On, the sources are asked, and insights are written automatically (within *Saver*), while the app is in the background too. It sits beside *Saver*, since both govern what happens while Sleevesight is away.
- ***Tell an artist's facts once per session*** (**default: on**). *On: a track's insight leaves out the artist's facts an insight earlier in the listening session told, and a track with enough about the song itself gets none. Off: every insight may tell all of them.* What the sources say about an artist is the same on every track by that artist; played through an album, each insight would otherwise retell it, most of all where the songs themselves are thinly documented. On, each fact is told once per listening session (see *New session idle trigger* under *History*), and a later track's insight is shorter rather than repeated; a track with four or more lines about the song itself gets no artist facts at all, since its insight hardly used them. When leaving the facts out would leave nothing to write from, the insight gets them all. Off, every insight is written with all of its artist's facts, so each one introduces the artist, and repeats what the one before it said. Either way the facts are kept per artist: a track by another artist, on a compilation or as a featured artist, gets that artist's facts the first time the artist comes up. Like the rows about writing, it cannot be changed while *Show insights* is off.

Per-aspect switches (the highlights, the track, the artist, the album) wait for the song, artist and album cards of a later phase: today there is one insight. A setting for the insights' style, with presets or your own words for their length and tone, is planned; it will not loosen the citation rules.

## History

*Listening history, kept on this phone.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight keeps the tracks you play on this phone, to avoid repeating itself within a listening session and to learn which track changes are your skips. Nothing of it leaves the phone.* The history does not reduce traffic to the sources or the AI service: the kept findings below do, and they are not part of it.

- ***Keep history for*** (**default: 90 days**; also 7, 30 or 365 days, or *Forever*). How long the tracks played and the track changes are kept, counted back from today. The track changes are what skip detection learns from (today, Spotify's crossfade; learning from rarer signals, such as a tap on *Write insight*, is planned), so it wants a season of them rather than a week; 90 days keeps them to a few MB. A week is the least offered: shorter would empty the list of someone who listens once a week. A shorter lifetime applies at once.
- ***New session idle trigger*** (**default: 30 minutes**; also 10, 20 or 60 minutes, or 2 hours). *A track that starts after a longer break begins a new listening session, counted from when the last track was heard. Earlier sessions are kept. Within a session, each artist fact is told once, unless "Tell an artist's facts once per session" is off. A change applies from the next track that starts.* Longer than a pause to answer the door or change trains, shorter than the gap between the morning's listening and the evening's. No play is discarded: sessions only group the plays kept. An artist fact the AI has already told in this session is not offered again for the next track by the same artist, while *Tell an artist's facts once per session* is on (under *Insights*).
- ***How long each source's findings are kept.*** What each source found for a track is kept on the phone for a while, so the same track is not looked up again, and is not asked for again from a paid search. Each source has its own lifetime, chosen from 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 or 90 days. **Defaults:** *MusicBrainz* and *Wikidata* 30 days, whose data changes slowly; *Wikipedia* and *Last.fm* 7 days, whose articles and bios are edited more often; *Web search* 1 day. A shorter lifetime means fresher findings and more requests; a longer one, fewer requests. Old entries are deleted at each start of the app, and are never used once expired.
- ***Keep what sources said about each artist*** (**default: off**). A privacy choice, so its title is shown in the theme's error colour. What the sources say about an artist (Last.fm's answers and tags, the Wikipedia artist article and its Wikidata facts, MusicBrainz's tags), kept together, amounts to a record of the artists you have played, so it is kept only if you choose.
  - **Off:** none of it is kept, per artist or with a track's findings (nor the sources' verdicts on artists' pages, nor the artists' MusicBrainz ids). Each track's artists are looked up again, and their names go to the sources you switch on with every track. Of a track's findings, only the web search's (pages about the recording, which may name or describe its artists) and those of a lookup that found no artist to look up are kept. So a track played before is looked up again at MusicBrainz (its match held in memory for half an hour only), Wikipedia and Last.fm, unless it has no credited artist; lookups are slower, and more requests go out.
  - **On:** what the sources say about each artist you play stays on the phone for as long as its sources' findings, so another track by the same artist does not look the artist up again, and fewer requests leave the phone. An artist's MusicBrainz identity is then kept for 90 days, a fixed lifetime. Each track's own lookups still send its artists' names.
  - **Either way**, what stays is the record of which tracks you played (the history, and the keys of the kept findings) and the insights written, which may retell some of what the sources said. MusicBrainz's answers are held in the app's memory for half an hour, for the track about to play, and never written to storage.
  - **Turning it off deletes what was kept**, and so does every start of the app while it is off, which also removes what earlier versions kept without asking.
- ***Clear history***. *Deletes the tracks played and the track changes*, once you confirm; it cannot be undone. Skip detection then forgets what it learned from them. It does not delete the insights written or the sources' findings: those go by their own lifetimes and limits ([What stays on the phone](#what-stays-on-the-phone)).

Old entries go by the phone's clock. Each time Sleevesight starts, with the phone or after it was stopped, it takes the clock as right; if the clock is then moved by more than an hour, ahead or back, nothing is deleted until it is set right again. So a clock set ahead before Sleevesight starts deletes old entries by that clock.

## Appearance

*App customisation.* Unfolded: *Sleevesight appearance options.*

- ***Theme*** (**default: *System***; or *Light*, *Dark*). *Light or dark. The system's follows your phone's dark theme.* *Light* and *Dark* keep the app so whatever the phone's dark theme is; the colours taken from the album art on the home screen, and the status and navigation bars, follow the choice too. The change shows at once. From Android 12 the splash screen as the app opens follows it as well; on earlier versions the splash screen follows the phone, and the app's own screen is as chosen from its first frame.
- ***Font*** (**default: *App font (Roboto)***; or *System font*). *The app's own font looks the same on every phone. The system font is the one chosen in your phone's settings.* Many phones' system font is not Roboto (Samsung's, for one), so without its own font the app would look different from phone to phone, and from its store screenshots. Roboto comes with the app, so nothing is downloaded. The change shows at once, in the whole app; the diagnostics screen's monospace text stays monospace.
- ***Keep Sources as you left them*** (**default: off**). *Off: the sources under each new track's insight start folded. On: they stay folded, or unfolded, as you left them on the track before; shown past their first 8 entries but not in full, they come back at their first 8.* Off by default so that the insight is what you see first on each new track: unfolding the sources to check one insight says nothing about the next. The fold is kept through a rotation, and when Android restores a screen it closed, not when the app starts afresh. Nothing is sent or kept either way.
- ***Logo intro at start*** (**default: on**). *When you open Sleevesight while it is not already open, its logo plays a few seconds of animation first; a tap, Back or any key skips it. It never plays while your phone's Remove animations or TalkBack is on.* Turn it off for no wait at all. It applies from the next time the app is opened.

## Developer

Shown only in developer mode, which seven taps on *Version* turn on, last, with no description: its options are for a developer or a tester, not for listening. Developer mode is a setting kept with the others: it survives a restart, and clearing the app's data returns it to its default. That is off, except in the test build that Google Play's testing tracks get, which starts in developer mode, so testers find *Diagnostics* without the seven taps; turned off there, it stays off.

- ***Diagnostics*** (**default: off**). *Shows the way to the diagnostics screen: what the pipeline did, and the track changes recorded.* While it is on, *Open diagnostics* appears under it.
- ***Show grounding details*** (**default: off**). *Under each insight, the grounding check’s counts in full: how many sentences are grounded, and how many come from the model’s own knowledge, are uncited, or cite missing evidence.* On, the line under the insight reads as the check counts, such as *2 of 3 sentences grounded · 1 uncited*. Off, it shows only when a sentence is not backed by the sources (*1 sentence is not backed by the sources.*) or the insight was kept from before: the citations already show how each sentence is grounded.
- ***Report button*** (**default: off; on in the test build**). *Shows a button on Home that collects what the app knows at that moment into a report, for you to review, edit and send from your mail app.* The button, small and with a bug on it, sits at the bottom right of the home screen, and only there. A tap takes, at that moment, when it was tapped (with the time zone), the app's version and build, the settings (never the API keys), what the player reports and what the app made of it (the sources' findings, the evidence, the insight and its citation check), and the diagnostics (its last 500 steps, and what skip detection has learned); then it shows the whole report, in parts. Each part has a switch that leaves it out and *Edit* to change its text (a part too long to edit in place, as the diagnostics often are, can be left out or edited in the mail app), and *What did you expect?* takes a note. The track changes recorded (the latest 100, with the tracks' titles and times) are a part too, off until you turn it on, since it lists the tracks you played. *Open in mail app* hands the report to your mail app, addressed to support@sleevesight.app, as *Report a problem* does, and it reaches the developer only if you send it from there; *Copy report* copies it instead. On by default in the test build, like developer mode, so testers find the button without looking for it; off in any other; a choice you made holds in either.
- ***Link style*** (**default: *Underline***). *How links are marked: a thin line below them, or a faint highlight behind them.* *Underline* draws a thin line a little below each link, clear of the letters that reach below the line (g, p, y); *Highlight* draws a faint rounded fill of the text's own colour behind it instead. Either way the link is in the colour of the text around it, and the choice holds for every link in the app: Home's sources and genre credits, *What leaves the device?*, the links under *Players*, *Sources* and *About*, the privacy policy and terms links, and the credits. It is here to try both on the phone, over real album art, before one is kept; then the other goes, or the choice moves to *Appearance*.
- ***Developer mode***. *Off: hides this section, and turns off what it holds.* Turning it off turns *Diagnostics*, *Show grounding details* and *Report button* off too, so nothing hidden keeps running, sets *Link style* back to *Underline*, and hides the section until the next seven taps.
- In developer mode, each player under *Players* also shows its package name under its name.

The diagnostics screen shows what Sleevesight did in its last few hundred steps since it started (each source's outcome and time, cache hits, AI calls, the citation check's counts), with the caches' sizes; the track changes recorded, which skip detection learns from; and what skip detection has learned, per player, with *Reset*, which makes it forget until it learns again and keeps the track changes. The steps are kept in memory only, and are gone when the app's process ends. *Export* writes all of it, every track change included, to a file you choose, which may be a cloud folder; that copy is yours, outside the app. Nothing else of it leaves the phone.

## What stays on the phone

Everything the app keeps is on the phone, and none of it is part of Android's backup or a transfer to a new phone. In short, with how long each is kept and how to clear it:

| What | Kept for | Cleared by |
|---|---|---|
| The history: the tracks played, with their length, and the track changes | *Keep history for* (90 days by default) | *Clear history* |
| What skip detection has learned, per player | Until reset | *Reset* on the diagnostics screen; *Clear history* |
| What each source found for a track | *How long each source's findings are kept* | Expiry, at the next start |
| What the sources said about each artist | Only with *Keep what sources said about each artist* on; as long as its sources' findings, an artist's MusicBrainz identity 90 days | Turning that switch off |
| The insights written | Until the caches' size limit (a fixed 50 MB) pushes out the least recently used | Android's *Clear storage* |
| The diagnostics' steps | The app's current run, in memory only | Ending the app |
| Your settings and API keys | Until you change or remove them | *Purge keys*; Android's *Clear storage* |

Android's *Clear storage* for Sleevesight, or uninstalling it, deletes everything it kept.
