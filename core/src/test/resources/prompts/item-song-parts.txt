STYLE

RULES, which hold whatever the style says:
- Write two parts, each under its heading on a line of its own, written exactly as given here: first "The music", then "This recording". Leave a blank line after each heading and between the parts.
- Under "The music", write about the work itself, as any recording of it has it: who composed it, when and why, what it is, where it sits in the composer's work, how it was received.
- Under "This recording", write about this performance: who plays it, when and where it was recorded, its release.
- Write 1 to 3 sentences in each part. Leave out a part, with its heading, when no line supports it. Another card gives the headline about the track; do not open with it.
- A line about another recording of the work (by other performers, or by these performers at another time) belongs to neither part: leave it out.
- A "composer" line is about the composer and a "performer" line about a performer, not about this work or this recording: use one only to place the music or the recording, naming whose it is.
- End every factual sentence with the id(s) that support it, like [W1] or [W2][S1].
- Use only the evidence: no names, dates, places or numbers that are not in it.
- A line marked with a language, like (Wikipedia, release, Hindi), is in that language: translate what you use of it into English, and cite it as usual.
- Never mention the track's length or running time: the player already shows it.
- An "original song" line is about the song as first recorded by someone else. You may say this track is a version of it, but never credit the original's facts to this artist.
- A web line may be about another song or artist of the same name; ignore what does not fit.
- A genre named only in a web line belongs to that page: if you mention it, attribute it to the page as the line describes it (say, "a store listing files it under blues"), never state it as the track's tag or genre.
- If the evidence supports fewer sentences than the style asks for, write fewer; a short insight is enough. Do not say what the evidence leaves out. Write no sentence without a citation.
- Do not use your own knowledge.
- Plain text only: no lists or markdown, and no headings but the two above, written as given.
