Leading designs

Five designs, each a header logo with its own intro, all kept so testers can compare them. The product page shows C on wide windows and B on windows 600 px or narrower; ?logo=a, b or c in its address picks one of those three and shows a switch in its footer. D and E are B's and C's intros, each ending on the other's header, so that a phone and a desktop can end on one mark; they are on this sheet only. Everything below them is the wider palette they come from.

Sleevesight

The eye and the record

Header: the eye, with "Sleeve" bold in ink and "sight" regular in red beside it.

Intro (about 11.6 s; the page is in at 10.5 s)
  1. Large in the middle of the window: a record slides out of its sleeve and the eye forms around it.
  2. The eye blinks, the record turns twice, and the eye blinks again.
  3. The sleeve comes back as the eye fades, and the logo shrinks into the header.
  4. There the sleeve slides away, the eye and the name appear, and the eye blinks once.

Hover: the eye blinks. See it at sleevesight.app/?logo=a. Video: assets/logo/sleeve-to-eye-light.mp4.

EC's intro, B's header · the record leaves on landing (#317)

The record, then the words

Header: the words alone, as B's. For wide windows in place of C, so a desktop ends on the same mark as a phone.

Intro (about 4.9 s)
  1. The mark fades in, large, in the middle of the window, the record at its centre.
  2. It turns twice about the record, the words going round with it.
  3. It shrinks into the header's top left.
  4. The rest of the page fades in, and the record turns out, shrinking as it fades, leaving the words.

C's intro and B's header, on B's timings, with the record's exit after them.

For the testers: which appeals more, and does B's turn read as anything? It borrows the record's spin without the record, so for someone who never played an LP or a CD it has to work as a plain flourish. C turns with the record in it: does that read better? And D or E: should a phone and a desktop end on one mark, and if so, with the record or without?

Marks with the eye

With the eye. The bold "Sleeve" carries the eye's weight; "sight" is regular, in ink or in the record label's red. The horizontal mark is for headers and narrow bands, the stacked one where there is height to spare.

Marks with the record

The 6c words, regular, "sight" red, right-aligned, with the record beside them on the side they align to: the record from the eye logo, without the eye. In 8a the record runs from the x-height of "Sleeve" to the baseline of "sight"; in 8b it is as tall as "sight", and centred on it. Turning, 8b goes round as one piece about the record's centre, as the record turns in the eye.

Reveals

Two ways of arriving at the 8b mark, for a splash, a video's end card or an intro. In R1 the record rolls in under "Sleeve" and uncovers "sight"; in R2 the tile is the sleeve, and the record comes out of it.

For marketing

The eye over the address, for posters, ads and merch, where the point is to send people to the site. Regular weight throughout, "app" in red; the eye as wide as the address, the two centred on one line.

Text tiles

No eye: the two words, right-aligned, on a rounded square. For avatars, app stores' secondary art, stickers and prints. White tile on light, black tile on dark.

Sizes

From a shirt print down to a favicon-sized tile. The words stay readable down to a tile of about 50px; at 32px the tile is a shape rather than words. The favicon (assets/favicon.svg) is the record alone, without the eye: at tab size the eye's corners would take most of its width.
