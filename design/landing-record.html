Liner notes for whatever you're playing

Sleevesight is an open-source Android app that reads what your music player is playing and shows a short, sourced insight about the song, with its genre labels. Every fact links to where it came from.

In development: not yet on Google Play

Try the simulator Source on GitHub

How it works

  1. Read

    Android tells the app what your player is playing: title, artists, album and cover. Sleevesight never touches the audio.

  2. Look up

    The track is cleaned of tags like "2006 Remaster" and matched in the sources you switched on. Matches that fail an identity check are shown as rejected, not used quietly.

  3. Write

    The AI service you chose turns what was found into a few sentences, citing the evidence. It may not add facts of its own unless you allow it, and then they are marked.

  4. Check

    Before anything is shown, the citations are checked in code. Uncited sentences are flagged, and the evidence is listed underneath.

When little is documented about a track, Sleevesight says so instead of filling the gap.

Planned: coming next

A later version adds detail for the song, each artist and the album, opened by tapping them, each with an insight written and checked the same way. It also lets you ask follow-up questions about what is playing, answered by your AI service on your own key, and export the tracks you listened to, with their insights, to a file. And it shows the languages a song is sung in, or none for an instrumental.

You choose

  • Your sources

    MusicBrainz, Wikipedia, Last.fm and web search, each a switch. The model's own knowledge is a separate switch, off by default.

  • Your AI service

    You bring your own API key and pick the model. Anthropic comes first; more providers, and local models, are planned.

  • Your timing

    Each insight is a call on your key. Have one written for every track, only when you skip, or only when you tap Write.

Privacy

Nothing you play reaches the developer

No accounts of our own, no telemetry, no crash reporting of our own, no analytics, no ads. Your API keys are stored encrypted on the phone and never logged.

The privacy policy says what each service receives, and what you can switch off.

What leaves the phone, and to whom

  • To the sources you switched on: the playing track's title and artists, and, by default, the next track's title and artists to MusicBrainz, if it is on.
  • To the AI service you chose: the title, artists and album, and what the sources found. Each insight is one request of its own, with no conversation kept between them; it is made with your key, so the service handles it under its terms for your account.
  • When a player gives the cover only as a web address: a request for that image to the server it names.

Nothing else leaves the phone, unless you export the diagnostics to a file yourself. Your listening history stays on it. The app's first screen says all this before it starts.

Get it

Sleevesight is for Android only: iOS does not let one app read what another is playing. It is in development and will be released through Google Play. Until then, the source code is on GitHub under the GPL-3.0, with instructions for building it.

The app is now in internal testing. Instructions will be available to approved testers soon.
