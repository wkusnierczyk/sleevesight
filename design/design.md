# Sleevesight

**Status:** Draft; Phase 0 passed, Phase 0.5 under way (platform findings in *Platform facts*)  
**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Owner:** Wacek  
**Platform:** Android (Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Material 3)  
**License:** [GPL-3.0](../LICENSE)  
**Repository:** [wkusnierczyk/sleevesight](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight)  
**Prototype:** [Sleevesight simulator](https://sleevesight.app/design/simulator.html)  
**Brand:** [Sleevesight brand sheet](https://sleevesight.app/design/brand.html)

Sleevesight is an open-source Android app that reads what a music player is playing, through Android's media session metadata, and shows grounded insights about the song, its artists, its album and its genres. It never touches the audio stream. Every insight is written by an AI service of the user's choice, from evidence retrieved from sources the user switched on, and every claim points back to its source.

## Summary

The phone already announces what is playing. Any app with notification access can read the title, artists, album, duration and album art of the current track from every player that publishes a media session, and can send play, pause, next and previous back to it. Sleevesight builds on that:

1. Read the now-playing metadata and normalize it: separate the song's own title and artists from what the player appends to them. Version tags such as "2006 Remaster" are kept as notes, the work a song is from is recorded as its source work, featured artists move into the artist list, and a list of artists is split into the credited artists. What remains is a clean key to look the song up by. See [Normalizer](#normalizer).
2. Resolve it to entities (recording, artists, release) and retrieve evidence from the sources enabled in Settings.
3. Have the user's chosen AI model write a short insight from that evidence only, with a citation on every factual sentence.
4. Check the citations mechanically before showing the text, and show the evidence underneath.

The prototype in the simulator covers the whole flow against a real 80-track playlist, with frozen lookups and, while it was hosted as a claude.ai artifact, live synthesis. The public copy linked above has no AI service, and its insight cards show the first facts found instead. This document records what it established and what still has to be verified on a real phone.

## Goals and non-goals

### Goals

- Show, within a couple of seconds of a track change, what makes the current song interesting, with sources.
- Make where information comes from a user decision: curated databases, open-web retrieval, and the model's own knowledge are separate switches.
- Let the user choose the AI provider and model and bring their own API key. Sleevesight is the author of the text; the model is a supporting service.
- Work with any player that publishes a media session, with Spotify as the reference player.
- Control the player from the app (play, pause, next, previous) and hand off to the player for a song, artist or album.
- Fail visibly: when nothing is documented, say so; when a match is rejected, show why.

### Non-goals

- Listening to or analyzing audio.
- Accounts, telemetry, analytics, social features, streaks, notifications. None of these ship, and CI enforces it (see Structural constraints).
- Replacing the player. Sleevesight never plays music itself.
- iOS. iOS sandboxing prevents reading another app's now-playing information; see Platform facts.

## Platform facts

These were established while designing, and checked in Phase 0 with the platform probe (`spike/`, removed after the gate; history keeps it) on a Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra (SM-S938B, Android 16, API 36), with Spotify, Castbox (podcasts), YouTube Music (free tier) and a Sonos session, on 2026-10-12. Items marked **to measure** are still open.

### Reading now-playing

- `MediaSessionManager.getActiveSessions(componentName)` returns a `MediaController` for every app currently publishing a media session: Spotify, YouTube Music, Tidal, Deezer, podcast apps, browsers playing media.
- It requires a declared `NotificationListenerService`. Its `ComponentName` is passed to `getActiveSessions()`; without it the call throws `SecurityException`. The user grants access in Settings, Notification access. On Android 12 (API 31) and later, the service declares `android.service.notification.default_filter_types=""` and `android.service.notification.disabled_filter_types="conversations|alerting|silent|ongoing"` ([#739](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/739)), which sets its allowed notification filter bitmask in `NotificationManagerService` to `0` on both new installs and upgrades while leaving `MediaSessionService`'s `hasEnabledNotificationListener` check unaffected. Measured on a Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra with Android 17 (2026-10-30): no notification reaches the listener, fresh or upgraded, and the media sessions read as before (architecture, *Session layer*). On stock Android, the platform documents the disabled types as ones the user cannot turn on in its settings (not measured here); Samsung's One UI has no per-type setting to show.
- Use callbacks, not polling. `addOnActiveSessionsChangedListener` tracks sessions appearing and disappearing; `MediaController.Callback.onMetadataChanged` and `onPlaybackStateChanged` deliver track and state changes.
- **Spotify (measured)** fills `TITLE`, `ARTIST`, `ALBUM`, `ALBUM_ARTIST`, `DURATION`, the album art (a bitmap 300 pixels wide, usually 300×300, or 472×472, and an `ALBUM_ART_URI`), and `METADATA_KEY_MEDIA_ID` as a **`spotify:track:<id>`** URI. `ARTIST` is one comma-joined string of the credited artists ("Artist A, Artist B, Artist C"), as the normalizer assumes. It also sends its own keys: the playing context (`com.spotify.music.extra.CONTEXT_URI`, `CONTEXT_TITLE`, `CONTEXT_DESCRIPTION`, `CONTEXT_SHARE_URL`), an `ART_HTTPS_URI` on Spotify's image CDN, an extracted background colour, and explicit, download and advertisement flags. Its `CROSSFADE_ENABLED` and `CROSSFADE_DURATION` extras are **unreliable**: they read `false` and `5000` throughout, including a run with a 12 s crossfade, so nothing relies on them.
- **Castbox (measured)** fills `TITLE`, `DISPLAY_TITLE`, `ARTIST` (the publisher), `ALBUM` (the episode title), `DURATION`, `MEDIA_ID` (its own episode id) and, for the playing episode, `MEDIA_URI` and http(s) art URIs. **YouTube Music (measured, free tier)** fills `TITLE`, `ARTIST`, `ALBUM`, `ALBUM_ARTIST`, `DURATION`, an art bitmap, a rating (always unrated here) and video dimensions (always 0 here), but **no `MEDIA_ID` and no art URI**: a YouTube Music track is looked up and opened by its title and artists, and told apart by them and its album, never by its queue item (see *Track changes*). On the free tier playback stops when YouTube Music leaves the foreground, so its session is often `PAUSED` or `STOPPED`; a paused session is normal, not a change. A **Sonos** session sent only art and an empty media id.
- **Spotify republishes unchanged metadata constantly**: about 93% of same-track updates across the three probe runs repeated the previous metadata exactly (776 of 834: each Spotify metadata update compared with the previous update of the same track, by media id, on all metadata values), and most of the rest changed only the art, the length (sent as a placeholder first) or the extracted colour. The debounce and the lookup-key comparison in the pipeline are what keep this from triggering lookups.
- Spotify also has an opt-in "Device Broadcast Status" setting that sends broadcast intents on metadata, playback-state and queue changes. The media session carries everything it does and more, so it is not used.

### Album art

- Spotify's `ALBUM_ART_URI` is a `content://com.spotify.mobile.android.mediaapi/...` URI, and **another app can read it**. The bitmap and the https URI are there as well.
- Castbox publishes http(s) art URLs (and a small bitmap), which `:sources` fetches like any other network resource.

### Controlling the player

- `MediaController.getTransportControls()` gives `play()`, `pause()`, `skipToNext()`, `skipToPrevious()` and `seekTo()`.
- Only offer an action the session advertises in `PlaybackState.getActions()`. **Measured:** Spotify advertises play or pause, previous, next, seek, play from search and play from URI; play, pause, next and previous were sent and worked (the others were not tried). Castbox advertises play, pause, next, seek and its own set, but not previous, and usually only play/pause while in `NONE` or `BUFFERING`. YouTube Music advertises play, pause, previous, next, seek and play from search, URI or media id. Next and previous come and go in its advertised actions (they are missing, with seek, in its early `STOPPED` and `PAUSED` states); its session extras carry only two car display hints.

### Opening the player

- `MediaController.getSessionActivity()` returns a `PendingIntent` to the player's own screen **for Castbox, which opened it (observed by the tester), and for YouTube Music (not tried); Spotify publishes none.** So for Spotify the app opens the player through a `spotify:` URI or its launch intent instead. The home screen's player row falls back to the launch intent (`AppGraph.playerLaunchIntent`) whenever the session gives no usable screen, none or a cancelled one (#596), and says it has nothing to open only when there is neither.
- Opening a specific track, album or artist in Spotify needs a Spotify ID: `Intent.ACTION_VIEW` with `spotify:track:<id>`, `spotify:album:<id>` or `spotify:artist:<id>`. **Measured:** `spotify:track:` with the id from `MEDIA_ID` opens the track.
- When no ID is known, fall back to `spotify:search:<query>`, which always opens (**measured**). Log the fallback.

### Playlist context

The media session carries more context than the design assumed. In order of reliability:

1. **Spotify's metadata.** `CONTEXT_URI` and `CONTEXT_TITLE` name the playlist or album being played (`spotify:playlist:...` and the playlist's name), **without an account**.
2. **The session queue.** Spotify publishes `getQueue()` (the playing track, then the upcoming ones, with `spotify:track:` ids; 4 to 50 items, one fewer per track, reset when a track is opened through a deep link; the playing track's media id matches the queue's first item in every capture, while its active queue item can lag a new track's queue for a moment) and `getQueueTitle()` ("Play Queue"). Castbox publishes its queue too (episode ids), and YouTube Music its "Up next" (13 to 25 items, titles without media ids, starting with the playing track rather than after it).
3. **Session history.** The record of every track the app has seen play. Always available, but it only knows the past.
4. **Spotify Web API, optional account.** `GET /playlists/{id}/items` returns the whole playlist, not only what is queued.

The track lists can therefore say "In this playlist · <name>" without the optional account; the account adds the full playlist beyond the queue.

### Track changes

Measured for decision D5 on 21 Spotify track changes (of 24 logged; the other three were two Castbox changes and one at the start of a probe session): at each change, the previous track's remaining time (its length less its position, extrapolated from the last playback state), and the playback states before the new metadata (the probe kept the last 3 s; the table counts the last 1.5 s).

| Change | Count | Remaining in the previous track | `PLAYING` states in the 1.5 s before |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ended on its own, no crossfade | 4 | −87 to −39 ms | 6 |
| Ended on its own, crossfade 12 s | 3 | 11.945 to 11.987 s | 6 |
| Skipped in Spotify, with earphones or from the notification | 12 | 20 s to 429 s | 0 |
| Skipped with the probe's buttons, just after pressing play | 2 | 175 s and 369 s | 3 or 4 |

- **No skip state.** Neither Spotify, Castbox nor YouTube Music reported `STATE_SKIPPING_TO_NEXT` or `STATE_SKIPPING_TO_PREVIOUS`, for any kind of skip. Skips in the Spotify app, from earphones and from the notification look the same to the session.
- **A natural end lands exactly at the crossfade.** Without crossfade the change comes within 0.1 s of the end; with a 12 s crossfade, 12.0 s before it, every time within 60 ms. Seeking towards the end beforehand changes nothing.
- **Timing alone is ambiguous inside the crossfade.** The remaining time is measured against the track's full length, so a skip 8 s before a 12 s crossfade reads as 20 s remaining. With a 5 s crossfade, a natural end and a skip 5 s before the transition would both read as 10 s: one change cannot say which it was.
- **The state clusters.** Before every natural end Spotify published two clusters of about six `PLAYING` states: one about 3 s before the change, and one 0.5 to 1 s before. Before a skip from the app, earphones or the notification it published none. Mid-track it publishes nothing but clusters around seeks and an occasional cluster of three. The late cluster therefore separates a natural end from a skip even where timing cannot. It is undocumented Spotify behaviour, so it is used with the timing, not alone. A seek shortly before a skip near the end is not measured.
- Skips made from Sleevesight itself need no detection: the app sent them. The probe-button rows show only that recent playback activity produces a few states (3 or 4), which is why the rule counts states rather than asking for any.
- Castbox, when skipped, sent the new episode's state (position 0) before its metadata; the probe's estimate handles both orders.
- **YouTube Music** (a short run on the free tier): one track listened to the end, then skips early, mid-play and late. It **re-sends the playing track with a new length** (in this run 106 s, then 278.8 s, then 279 s, for one track), so for this player the length is not part of a track's identity; the active queue item is, and it moved exactly at the tester's four changes. Read by the queue item, the natural end came at the track's end (within 0.02 s of it, by the latest length), with no cluster of states before it, so the clusters are Spotify's alone; the skips left 290 s, 131 s and **7.2 s**, the late one reading as a natural end by timing alone, the accepted trade-off. The probe, which compared lengths, first took the length updates for track changes; the lesson for the app then was to identify a track by its queue item, revised below by #297. A new length is an update, after which the time left is computed from the latest length. As with Castbox, the new track's first state (position 0) arrived just before its metadata, at the natural end and the skips alike.
- **YouTube Music, captured again (#297, 2026-11-03, free tier, two runs, one in split screen so it keeps playing):** in-queue changes (natural ends, skips) lead with the state: the new queue item (`PAUSED`, position 0), the metadata 15 to 160 ms later, then `PLAYING`; the title never came first and never lagged by more than 300 ms. The album changes during a play, to or from the artist's name: the metadata first carries the artist as the album, the real album 190 to 290 ms later; once, at the start of a session, real, artist, real within 0.7 s; videos carry the artist as the album throughout. A play from search resets the queue: item −1 and an empty queue, the title alone, the artists and album about 150 ms later, then a fresh item. A studio and a live take of one song differed by title there. Repeat-one keeps the item, sends no metadata, and moves the position back to 0. In the background the free tier pauses, and after about 2 minutes paused drops to item −1 with an empty queue, keeping the metadata. Ads never reached the session.
- **Track identity (#297)**, from that capture: Sleevesight cares what is playing, the recording, not how often it played nor which queue slot holds it. Different players are different tracks; a media id on both reports decides. Otherwise the title must match exactly; a report without artists says nothing, and known, different artists are another track; a new album within the first second of a play fills the play in (the placeholder above), and after that a new album under the same title and artists is another track (a studio, then a live take). Albums are never compared by name: an album named as its artist is an album like any other. Each report is compared with everything known of the play so far, so a field missing from one report is not a change and is kept, and a field learned once is never lost to a later report (in the session history either, across a pause or a restart). The queue slot is carried and recorded but never decides; it only times a change: when it moves under the title before and a new title follows within 1 s, the change began at the move, so the time left reads right and a natural end does not read as a skip. A repeat (repeat-one, or the same track again right after itself) is one play. The session layer tags each report with its play, and every consumer reads that tag (`architecture.md`, *Identity*).
- **Ads** ([#880](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/880)). Spotify Free plays its ads through its media session as if they were tracks (a tester's: title `tetesept`, artist `Advertisement • 2 of 3`, 28 s, the ad's own image as the cover). An ad is told by the two signals that cannot fire on music (`AdDetection`, one place): the metadata's `android.media.metadata.ADVERTISEMENT` (`MediaMetadataCompat.METADATA_KEY_ADVERTISEMENT`) set to 1, for any player; and, for Spotify, a `spotify:ad:` URI as the media id or, when the metadata names none, as the active queue item's media id (the metadata's own id decides when it has one: the active item can lag behind the track after an ad). A queue that arrives after the ad's metadata and state settles them again. **Told late** (only by such a queue): the ad was published as a track for a moment, and is then taken back. It gets no run, and a run of it already started is cancelled before anything more is asked, its view forgotten, with any *Ask AI* search for it; its row in the history and the change into it are deleted, and *Saver*'s count of it undone. What its run did before it was told stays: the sources' answers, and an insight if one was written by then, kept in the caches under the ad's own title and artist, which no track shares, so they are never served for music and expire as any; and skip detection may have learned from the change into it until the next start, which relearns from the record without it. Not the artist text (Spotify translates "Advertisement"), nor a missing album, a length of 30 s or less, or skipping removed from the actions, which music can show too. The session layer publishes an ad as no track, with the ad beside it (`SessionRepository.ad`, its player and length only): so nothing is looked up or written for it, no tap can start a call for it, it is not read aloud, prepared (an ad in the queue is skipped: the first track after it is prepared, during the ad too), kept in the history or the caches, and the changes into and out of it are recorded as none, so skip detection learns nothing from them and *Saver* counts none. The play before it is kept, so the track after it is judged against that play: a new track is a new play, analysed as usual. The signals are still to be confirmed by a capture of Spotify Free playing an ad (`SessionDump`, #897; `fixtures/sessions/ads/`, synthetic until then).

**Decision (D5, refined):** a user setting, *Crossfade*: **Autodetect** (the default) or a fixed value, 0 to 15 s. Changes that Sleevesight caused itself (its own transport buttons, selecting a track) are known to be user-driven and are not put through detection. The rules below are the floor; D15 lets detection learn per player on the device, within them (`architecture.md`, *Learning skip detection*).

- A fixed value *c*: a change is a natural end when the remaining time is at most *c* + 0.5 s; for Spotify, also when the late cluster is there with at most 15 s remaining, so a value set below Spotify's real crossfade does not read natural ends as skips.
- Autodetect, for Spotify (the only player where the clusters have been measured): a change is a natural end when the remaining time is at most 15 s and either the late cluster is there (at least 5 `PLAYING` states within 1.5 s before the change) or the remaining time is within 0.5 s of 0 or of the learned crossfade. Otherwise it is a skip.
- Autodetect, for any other player: timing alone. A change with at most 15 s remaining is a natural end. Whether other players publish such clusters, or another signal, is investigated as they are covered.
- The learned crossfade: the remaining time of natural ends; when two agree within 0.1 s, that is the value, and when two later ones agree on another value, it replaces it, so a changed crossfade is followed. It only adds natural ends that lack the late cluster (a probe connected late, a restart); it never turns a change into a skip.
- Detection errs towards "ended on its own" (decision D5), with one accepted exception: under Autodetect for Spotify, a natural end that lacks the late cluster before any crossfade has been learned reads as a skip. That is the price of catching skips inside the 15 s window, and it ends once a crossfade is learned.

### Notification access and installation

- Installed through `adb`, the probe got notification access without restriction (`packageSource` OTHER, initiating package `com.android.shell`).
- **Installing from a file (measured, D7).** The probe's APK, opened from My Files, was first stopped by **Google Play Protect** ("App blocked to protect your device": Play Protect has not seen an app from this developer before), which offers **Install anyway**. After that, **Advanced Protection** blocked it outright ("Advanced Protection only allows apps from Google Play and other app stores pre-installed on your phone"), with no override. On this phone the device setting was off and the Google account is enrolled in Google's Advanced Protection Program, so the block presumably comes from that enrolment. So:
  - for a user without Advanced Protection, installing Sleevesight from a file costs at least a Play Protect warning (and, the first time, allowing the file manager or browser to install unknown apps);
  - for a user whose Google account is enrolled in Advanced Protection (and, by the dialog's wording, presumably one with the device setting on, which was not tested), only Google Play (or a store pre-installed on the phone) can install it, which rules out GitHub releases and F-Droid for them.
- Restricted settings on notification access, for an app installed from a file, could not be measured on this phone, since the install itself is blocked; it was measured on a second phone (next item).
- **Restricted settings (measured, #29).** On a Samsung Galaxy S23 Ultra (SM-S918B, Android 16, API 36, One UI 8.5, build `S918BXXSAFZI1`) with no Advanced Protection, on 2026-10-16, the debug `:app` APK from CI was copied to `Download/` and opened from My Files. My Files first had to be allowed to install unknown apps, then Play Protect blocked the install and **Install anyway** went through, as on the first phone. Android recorded a file install: `packageSource` 3 (`PACKAGE_SOURCE_LOCAL_FILE`), installer and initiating package `com.google.android.packageinstaller`, originating package `com.sec.android.app.myfiles`. Android **did mark the app restricted**: the app op `ACCESS_RESTRICTED_SETTINGS` read `deny` (`MODE_ERRORED`, which Android sets on file installs; the op's default is `allow`). Yet notification access was then granted from the app's first-run link with only the usual confirmation: **the restriction was not enforced** on that screen. The link asks for `ACTION_NOTIFICATION_LISTENER_DETAIL_SETTINGS` and falls back to the listener list; which of the two One UI showed was not recorded, and the other was not tried. So on this phone a file install costs two warnings and no unlock. One phone, one installer and one grant path only. A browser download gets the same marker and was not tried. Since the marker is set, the other grant screen, a Pixel or another Android version is likely to enforce it, so onboarding should still explain the unlock if access cannot be granted (`architecture.md`, R8).
- **Developer verification (checked 2026-10-16, #29; rechecked 2026-10-19).** Since 2026-09-30, on certified devices in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand, installs from the [participating stores](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides#locations) (Google Play and several phone makers' stores) need a registered developer and package; installs from a file, and `adb` installs, are not checked yet ([FAQ](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq)). Google plans a global rollout in 2027. From then, an unregistered app would install only through `adb` or the *advanced flow* (developer mode, a check that nobody is coaching the user, a restart, a one-time one-day wait and a biometric or PIN confirmation, then installs allowed for 7 days or indefinitely, each still warning of an unverified developer). Short of full registration, a free *limited distribution* account registers packages whose builds install on up to 20 devices: no government ID is checked, but the account needs a Google payments profile (with a legal name and address), 2-Step Verification and a contact email; each device's user consents on the device, through a QR code or a link, and the device is registered in the Android Developer Console ([limited distribution](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/limited-distribution)). It suits development builds handed to testers. Apps on Play are registered through the Play Console, so under D7 this concerns builds handed out as files: once the package is registered for full distribution, such a build should install as it does today (the unknown-apps permission and the Play Protect warning above remain). The rules and dates are still moving; check them again before a channel outside Play.
- `adb` installs escape the Advanced Protection block and restricted settings, as the probe's `adb` install on the first phone showed (first item), and developer verification, as Google's [FAQ](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq) says. The Advanced Protection block measured is the one that comes from the account's enrolment, with the device setting off; whether an `adb` install escapes the block of the device setting was not tested. Not every check: with *Verify apps over USB* on (developer options), an `adb` install can still be stopped by an "Unsafe App Blocked" warning, with *Install anyway* ([Android Studio, known issues](https://developer.android.com/studio/gemini/journeys#known-issues)).
- **Decision (D7, revised):** Sleevesight is distributed through **Google Play** (owner, 2026-10-12), the one channel that reaches every user, Advanced Protection or not, without a warning. Play's review of an app that holds notification access becomes part of the release work.

### Spotify Web API constraints

Since the February 2026 changes, Development Mode apps need the app owner to have Spotify Premium, are capped at five allowlisted users per client ID, and lost several endpoints and fields (artist followers and popularity, track ISRCs, artist top tracks, new releases). Extended quota is available to organizations only.

For an open-source app this is workable with a bring-your-own model: each user registers their own Spotify developer app and pastes its client ID, the same way they supply an AI key. The cost is friction, so the Spotify account stays optional and nothing in the core flow depends on it.

## Architecture

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  P[Player app] -- media session --> L[Session layer]
  L -- transport controls --> P
  L --> N[Normalizer]
  N --> R[Entity resolution]
  R --> A[Source adapters]
  A --> E[Evidence set]
  E --> S[Synthesis via AI provider]
  S --> G[Grounding check]
  G --> UI[Compose UI]
  E --> UI
  W[Spotify Web API, optional] --> C[Playlist context]
  L --> C
  C --> UI
```

### Session layer

- `NotificationListenerService` plus `MediaSessionManager`. Follows only the players switched on under Settings, Players; events from other packages are dropped and logged as ignored. A player is listed there once its session has reported music, a title and an artist, so dialer calls and most video apps never are; phone calls (the telecom and dialer packages, or a session whose audio is voice communication, checked again before every read and report, and as reports settle) are never read, whatever the settings say (#435), from when Android marks them. A VoIP call in an ordinary media session is not recognised.
- **Debounce of 650 ms** on metadata changes. A person flicking through tracks produces a burst of changes; only the last one triggers lookups. In-flight retrieval and synthesis for a superseded track are cancelled.
- Seeking or pausing changes playback state, not metadata, and never triggers a lookup.
- Exposes transport controls and the session activity to the UI.

### Normalizer

Players report a song the way their catalogue files it, which is often not how sources such as MusicBrainz and Wikipedia name it: "Fisherman's Blues - 2006 Remaster" is the song "Fisherman's Blues", and "Baby, Please Don't Go (feat. Van Morrison)" has a second artist hidden in its title. Normalizing turns the title and artist strings into the song's plain title, its list of credited artists, and a lookup key, without losing what it removed: each removed part is kept, as a note, a source work, a variant or an added artist. Rules proven on the test playlist:

| Input pattern | Action | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A trailing `- 2006 Remaster`, `- demo`, `- Live`, `- Mono`, `- … Edit` (`- Radio Edit`, `- Promo 7" Edit`), `- … Version`; not `- … Mix`, often a recording of its own | Strip, record as a note | "Fisherman's Blues - 2006 Remaster" |
| `(feat. X)` in the title | Move X to the artists | "Baby, Please Don't Go (feat. Van Morrison)" |
| `(From X)`, a trailing `- From "X"` | Strip, record the source work | "The Departure (From The Leftovers)" |
| `(felt piano)`, `(acoustic)` | Strip, record as a variant | "And Dream Of Sheep (felt piano)" |
| `A, B, C` in the artist field | Split into credited artists | "Peleboy, Tony K" |

The key is the lowercased, accent-folded primary artist plus the normalized title, keeping letters and digits in any script, and `+` ("C++" is not "C"); other punctuation, symbols and emoji become a space, unless they are all there is (a title `★` keys as `★`). Accents are folded on Latin and Greek letters only: Cyrillic `й` is not `и`. Duplicates in a playlist hit the cache.

### Entity resolution

The hard part, and the place most failures came from in the prototype. Resolution produces a **typed outcome** per source, never a silent miss:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `song_article` | The source describes this recording |
| `artist_article` | The source describes the performer only |
| `original_song` | The source describes the song as first recorded by someone else; this track is a cover or version |
| `rejected(reason)` | A candidate was found and failed the entity check, for example a different artist with the same name |
| `none` | Nothing found |
| `source_off` | The user switched the source off |
| `unreachable` | Network or source error |

`none` and `source_off` are deliberately different: one is a gap in the world's documentation, the other is the user's choice.

Rules:

- **Resolve each credited artist separately.** The prototype only resolved the primary artist, so secondary artists' boxes were nearly empty.
- **Keep recording and release apart.** A release date belongs to the release the player filed the recording under, which is often a compilation or remaster. The prototype showed "Released 2002" for a 1978 song for this reason. MusicBrainz models recordings, releases and release groups separately; use it to find the first release date of the recording.
- **Look past a full first page for the earliest recording** (#906). A search gives at most 100 recordings, and a song an artist has recorded more often than that, mostly live, may come without the studio one: *Dream On* by Aerosmith (169 recordings, most scoring alike, so ranked differently from one request to the next), with the player reporting the 1973 debut *Aerosmith*, resolved to the 1978 live recording on *Live! Bootleg*, with its date and producers. Only a recording released no later than the first page's earliest one that could be chosen could beat it, so when the first page is full, a second search asks for those (by the first release date, `firstreleasedate:[* TO …]`): few, deterministic, one more request. The usual rules then choose among both, the earliest released first. Only the title and artist are sent, as for the first page. Paging (`offset`) was tried first and dropped: with most scores tied, pages overlap and skip recordings from one request to the next. The album the player reports is not used either: it would send the album to MusicBrainz, and a compilation it names often carries a later recording of the song.
- Entity matching is deterministic code (name similarity, duration tolerance, MusicBrainz relationships), never the language model.

### Source adapters

One interface, one adapter per source:

**The live test** (20 calls, about $0.10 by Exa's estimates): tracks 1, 12, 23, 30, 45, 61 and 77, with `auto` and `instant`. `instant` was as relevant as `auto` at a tenth of the latency (0.3 s against 3 s) and a little over half the price, so the adapter used it. This is a summary; no result text is kept in the repository (*Open on the terms*). The app's and the CLI's retrieval cache does keep web search results on the device, for web search's lifetime (D9: 1 day), like any other source's: during development, by the owner's decision of 2026-10-14 ([#81](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/81)), which reversed the #44 review's refusal, since without it every repeat lookup is a paid search.

**`lite` against `instant`** ([#608](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/608), 2026-10-27; one run of `:cli live --no-insights --tracks all` on each, 78 searches each, the request otherwise the adapter's). `lite` found the recording far more often: web search gave `song_article` on 77 of 80 positions against 63, and 352 lines against 213, more on 47 positions and fewer on 2. `lite` always returned 10 results; `instant` fewer on 19 of 78 searches, at the same price. On the six named tracks `lite` kept 4 or 5 lines each, mostly from the recording's own pages (streaming services, song databases, library catalogues; for 12, only Reem's song; for 77, also a Spotify playlist that lists it); `instant` kept 0 to 3, and returned mostly other songs of the same name: nothing kept for 30 and 61, and for 45 and 77 only a Spotify playlist that lists the track. `instant` did worse than in the spike of 2026-10-14, after Exa 3.0 made it legacy. Speed was about the same: Exa's own search time 239 ms for `lite` and 217 ms for `instant` at the median. One `lite` request got no answer within the 30 s socket timeout and ended `unreachable`. More results also reach the filter's known limit on titles made of common words: for track 4, *Location* by Peleboy, `lite` returned pages naming the artist and using the word, and the filter kept three. The adapter stays on `lite`. The comparison, track by track, is on #608; no result text is kept in the repository.

**Keeping results about the right recording.** The query is `"<title>" from <work> by <artists>, song`, the `from` part only when the player names the work (track 34, `Theme` from Coronation Street), which suits Exa's neural search; highlights asked for at 300 characters (`maxCharacters`), steered by the same words, are the snippet. Exa does not hold to `maxCharacters` (in the runs of 2026-10-27 about three results in ten came back longer, up to 3,000 characters; [#608](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/608)), so the adapter cuts each snippet to 300 characters, at a word boundary, and marks the cut with an ellipsis (`…`): the line stays a short verbatim quote (D1), and five lines come to about 1,500 characters at most ([#634](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/634)). The filter below judges the line as cut, not the whole highlight, so a result is never kept for words the line then leaves out. A track with no credited artist is not searched, since no result could be checked. Ten results are asked for, and at most five kept, in Exa's order. Phrases are matched whole in the result's title and snippet, after folding case, accents and punctuation, with `&` read as `and` and apostrophes dropped ("Don't" and "Dont" agree) except before a possessive "s", which stays a word of its own ("Lightfoot's" still names Lightfoot). A track whose title or artists fold to nothing (an artist named "!!!") is not searched either. A result is kept only if:

- it names the title, and the work it is from when there is one;
- it names a credited artist, and a distinctive one when the track has one: an artist named only with common words (Them, To Twelve) counts only when no credited artist is distinctive, so track 63 needs Van Morrison;
- its own title does not name such an artist without the song. A page titled after the artist alone is about the artist or another of their recordings (Yanjoco's "Caravan" page, which lists the song among the artist's others), so a mention in its snippet does not count. A page titled after neither, such as a catalogue entry or a soundtrack listing, is judged on its snippet, which keeps the thin tracks' best results. Results from the sources the pipeline reads through their own adapters (Wikipedia and its mirror Wikiwand, Wikidata, MusicBrainz, Last.fm, Discogs) are excluded in the query and dropped again by the filter, so their text arrives only through the adapter that verifies it. Kept results give `song_article`; results that all fail give `rejected`; no results give `none`. Known limits: an artist named only with common words, when no other credited artist is distinctive, is matched by those words; a result can quote lyrics; a page about another recording that names the song and the artist in passing still passes; and a listing titled with several artists, this one among them, but not the song (a soundtrack page titled "Ray Johnson And The Sou' Westers, Die Hexen and others") is dropped, even when it lists the song.

**Page boilerplate** ([#905](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/905), 2026-11-04). On thinly documented tracks much of a highlight can be the page around the facts: a video site's footer and channel stats, a streaming service's navigation and sign-in text, a store's rights line and list of supporters' user names, a chord sheet's tuning and capo fields, a lyrics site's format line. Sent as evidence lines, these drew sentences about themselves from every prompt wording tried in the [#767](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/767) phone round (a rights notice called "a useful detail", "an official video is on YouTube"), on four of ten tracks. *Where*: in the adapter's extraction (`WebBoilerplate`, through `ExaWebSearch.lines`), for the track's search and the album's, before the cut, so the room such text took goes to the page's own text; not when the prompt's evidence is chosen (`PromptEvidence`). So what *Sources* shows is what the model was given, the line count that decides whether the artist's lines are needed (`PromptEvidence.ENOUGH_OF_ITS_OWN`) counts only lines with something in them, and the caches keep the cleaned lines: the extraction version went to 3, the track's (`ExaWebSearch.EXTRACTION_VERSION`) and the album's kept web lines' (`AlbumSource.WEB_PART`), so lines kept before are looked up again. What that costs: the cards are keyed by the fingerprint of the evidence they were written from (`CardKey.evidenceFingerprint`, every line's text), so a card written from a web line that the cleaning changes or drops is not found again and is written anew when next shown, with a paid AI call. For a track that means its key insight, its song insight, its genre reasons and its genre list (the cards keyed by the track's own evidence; the artist's insight is keyed by the artist's evidence, which has no web line, and *Search with AI*'s answers by none); since a track's web lines last a day in the retrieval cache, they would have been looked up again (a paid search) and cleaned within a day anyway, and the version only brings that forward. For an album it means its insight, if it was written from web lines: those are kept as long as the insight (*Keep history for*), so without the new version they would keep their boilerplate for good; with it, each such album's web search is made again when its insight is next opened (a paid search), and the insight is written again (a paid call) unless the lines come out the same. Decided so on PR [#920](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/pull/920): a once-only cost, against cards written from boilerplate for as long as the history keeps them. The filter judges each result on its highlight as it came (cut, as before), not on what is left: a part naming only the song and the artist goes as boilerplate, yet it is the surest sign of what a page titled after neither (a soundtrack listing, a catalogue entry) is about, so the page keeps its facts. A result the filter keeps whose every part went gives no line, and the search's outcome is `none` rather than `rejected` when every kept result went so (it was about the recording, with nothing to say); its address is still kept (`SourceResult.pages`), never cited or sent, so the *Lyrics* panel still links a lyrics page whose highlight was only its title (`webPages`). The pages of *AI web search*'s own searches (`ExaPageSearch`: *Search with AI*, *Explain the song*, the genre list and reasons, the artist's insight under *Up to 3 times*) are cleaned by the same rules, before their cut to 600 characters ([#929](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/929)), for the names the question is about (`WebQuestion.subjectNames`, sent with each search: the track's title and artists, with its work for the genre list and reasons and its album for *Search with AI* and *Explain the song*; a genre's name for its definition), with no filter before them, as they are pages for the model to answer from. *Search with AI*'s lyrics pages are not cleaned at all (`subjectNames` null): their address is the answer even when the passage is only the page's title or its site's words. The artist's insight sends no names: its pages are judged by the artist's name in the passage as it came and only then cleaned for the artist (`ArtistWebLookup.pageLine`), as the app's own search judges a highlight. With none, a part goes only when its site's text and a page's words are all it has. A page whose every part goes is left out, and a search whose pages all go has found nothing, as one with no pages (*Once* makes no call, the search tool answers that it found nothing), with no new text. Answers kept before stay as they were written. *How*: a line's parts (the passages between the ` ... ` its highlights are joined with) go whole or stay whole; nothing is cut out of a part. A part goes when it is boilerplate through and through: with its site's known page text set aside, what is left has no digit, no credit (a role's label before a name, in any case: "Music by", "Lyrics:", "Written by"; not a streaming page's "song and lyrics by"), and no word beyond the subject's names (the track's title, its work and artists; the album and its artist) and a page's words ("official video", "chords", "lyrics", a site's name or address; not "single" or "album", which name release facts, so a listing's "- Single" or a video's "(Full Album)" is matched as its site's text instead, and "Album:" counts as a credit). The known page text counts only on its own kind of site, by the result's address: the footer, the channel's stats fields (each value bounded) and a transcript's "[Music]" on YouTube; navigation and sign-in text on the streaming services; the rights heading, the shows and the supporters' user names on Bandcamp; "Tuning:", "Capo:", "Chords used:" and runs of chord symbols on chord sites; on any page only a lyrics site's fixed sentences, whole, and a part that is only "All rights reserved". A line whose parts all go gives no result. Two earlier versions cut boilerplate out of a part and took facts with it, each review round finding more ("- Likes: 98K ## Description … in 1974." emptied, a credit naming the artist dropped, "is Am G D." losing its chords; PR [#920](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/pull/920)), so the rule is now whole parts only. On the 55 distinct web lines of the round's prompts (their sites inferred from their text, since the captures keep no addresses or titles), 5 went (four video pages with only their title and footer, one chord sheet's title), 7 lost one or more parts and 43 were kept as they came; with the filter run on the line alone, without the page's title the captures lack, 43 pass, of which 5 went, 5 lost parts and 33 were kept as they came. **Known limit**: boilerplate in a part that also has a fact stays, the part kept whole (Spotify's "Sign in to see lyrics …" beside the year, a Bandcamp rights heading in the same highlight as its release date, a transcript's markers between lyrics); so do page text worded differently from the rules or on a site not listed, a list of plain names without handles, and a page's view of the song (a meaning page), which is not boilerplate and is for the prompt to attribute.

**Open on the terms.** Exa's §4.2(a) forbids, without its written permission, to "download, modify, copy, distribute, transmit, display, perform, reproduce, duplicate, publish, license, create derivative works from, or offer for sale any information" obtained through its services. Read literally, it covers what the app does with a result on the user's own key: it displays the highlight, transmits it to the user's AI provider in the prompt, and creates a derivative work from it, the written insight. Showing results is what the API is for (§1.1 grants use "for the limited purposes set forth in the documentation"), but the terms do not say so of these uses. Before release, confirm with Exa that the app's use is within the terms, including the on-device retrieval cache, which keeps web search results for a day during development (owner, 2026-10-14), and the album's and the artist's insights' web search results (#885, #912), which are kept for as long as the album's or the artist's insight is (*Keep history for*, up to *Forever*), so its card is found again; the question is tracked in [#80](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/80) and must be settled before the app is published. Because the same clause covers stored copies, the test fixtures in `fixtures/http/web/` are synthetic: the requests are the adapter's own, the responses have Exa's shape with titles, URLs and highlights written by the project; live checks go through `ExaLiveTest`, which keeps nothing. A second provider can be added behind the same `SourceAdapter`, with the filter shared.

### Evidence model

```kotlin
data class EvidenceLine(
    val id: String,          // "W1", "L3", "S2", "P1"
    val source: SourceId,
    val scope: Scope,        // SONG, ARTIST, ORIGINAL_SONG, RELEASE, WEB
    val text: String,        // one claim: templated from structured data, or a short attributed quote, never a whole passage (D1)
    val url: String?,
    val retrievedAt: Instant,
)
```

`P` lines come from player metadata (the release). Scope is passed to the model so it never credits an original song's facts to a cover act.

### Synthesis

- **Provider abstraction.** `AiProvider` with implementations for Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and a local model through Ollama. The user switches one provider on, and sets each provider's key, model and reasoning effort, which it keeps while switched off.
- **Role of the model.** It narrates retrieved evidence. It does not resolve entities, rank sources, or decide what is true.
- **Citation contract.** Every factual sentence ends with the ids of the evidence lines that support it. No names, dates, places or numbers outside the evidence. No sentence goes uncited: where the evidence covers only part of what an insight would say, the insight is shorter, and says nothing of what is missing (prompt version 3, #193). Version 2 (#46) ended such an insight with the fixed, uncited sentence `Little else is documented.`, which ended nearly every insight and told the reader nothing; the grounding check still reads it as a hedge. When the evidence has nothing to narrate (`EvidenceSet.nothingToNarrate`: no line at all, or only the player's release, which repeats what the player reports), no call is made and the card says "The sources give nothing to write an insight from." With model knowledge on, a set of only the player's release gets a call once the player has reported the artists, since the model may add the one unverified `[AI]` sentence the user opted into. Before the artists (some players report the title and album first), it gets none, whatever the switch (#204): the card written then would stand for the track, since nothing is written twice for one track without a tap (D5), and its `[AI]` sentence would be written without the artist; with no call, the report with the artists is decided afresh. The model replies `NO_EVIDENCE` if the evidence it is given has nothing usable, and the card says the same. The text holds in every case it shows: sources that found nothing, failed or were unreachable, the release alone, or lines the model found unusable.
- **The insight stays on the track** (prompt version 4, [#239](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/239)). Artist-level lines (Last.fm's bio, the Wikipedia artist article, Wikidata's artist facts) are the same on every track by the artist, so an album played through sent them with every call, and where its songs are thinly documented the model filled the insight with them, the same facts track after track. The prompt now leaves some out (`PromptEvidence`), while *Don't repeat artist facts* is on, as it is by default ([#407](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/407); off, every prompt has every line, as before, for those who would rather each insight introduce the artist than be shorter): a track with at least four lines of its own (about the song, its original or from the web; the player's release does not count) gets no artist-level line, since its insights hardly used them; a track with fewer gets the artist-level lines that no other track's insight in the listening session cited (the history's session, *New session idle trigger*), so each artist fact is told once a session and a later track's insight is shorter rather than repeated. When leaving them out would leave nothing retrieved to narrate, the prompt has them all, with model knowledge on too, so the model's own unverified sentences never stand in for verified artist lines. Web lines count as the track's own; the measurement ran with web search off, and with it on most tracks reach the four. The lines kept keep their ids, and the Sources list shows the whole evidence. A fresh reply is checked against the lines its prompt held: a citation of a line left out is `unknown`, since the model never saw it, and its chip shows and reads as one of no line; such a card is shown but not kept, so a card served from the cache, checked against the track's whole evidence, reads as it did when it was written, and the track is written again, a paid call, the next time it plays. A `NO_EVIDENCE` reply to a prompt that left lines out is not shown and not kept: it is asked again at once, a second paid call, with every line, as the fallback above intends, and a Retry after that call failed or was stopped asks with every line too. Version 4 is a new prompt version though the template is unchanged, since the cards written before it on a thinly documented album are the repeating kind; each track kept is written afresh once. The synthesis cache keys a card by the whole evidence's fingerprint, not the part sent, so a track played again finds its card whatever the session had cited (*Caching and offline*). What the session cited is kept in memory, per session; after a restart the first insight may tell an artist fact again. It is kept with the setting off too, so switching it on mid-session leaves out what the session has already told. A card written with it on and served from the cache with it off (or the other way) is the card it was: the cache keys a card by the whole evidence, not the setting, so a track played again is not written again for it. A fact is matched by its source and its text folded (`ArtistFact`: case, spacing, quotation marks, apostrophes, dashes and a closing full stop ignored), since with the artist cache off ([#420](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/420), the default) each track's artist lines are fetched afresh, and a source may give a line back with its typography edited; a line whose words changed is another fact, and may be told again. Measured with the CLI's `--tracklist` on three albums and a one-artist playlist (2026-10-21, Claude Haiku 5.5): before, on the album with thin song evidence (*Sundown*), 8 of 10 insights cited an artist fact an earlier one had cited, and on the others none did, though artist lines were a quarter of every prompt; after, none did on any, input tokens fell by 29 percent over the 45 calls (93,318 to 66,407), output stayed level (8,627 to 8,773), and the grounding check found no unknown or uncited sentence either way.
- **The prompt's fixed part** (prompt version 5, [#399](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/399)). The instructions around the track and its evidence (the task, the data fences, the style and the contract) were about 2,800 characters, roughly 900 input tokens on Claude Haiku 5.5, on every call: more than the artist-level lines ever were. The contract was stated twice, before the style and again after it. It is now stated once, after the style, under a heading saying it holds whatever the style says, which keeps the guarantee the restatement gave; and its rules are shorter, each of the simulator's kept in substance. The fixed part is now about 1,500 characters, about 410 tokens fewer per call. Measured with the CLI over the four tracklists and the regression tracks (2026-10-22, Claude Haiku 5.5, web search off, both versions from the same retrieval, two to ten runs each): input tokens fell by 27 to 30 percent on every tracklist, and output tokens by 14 to 35 percent, as the thinly documented tracks' insights came out shorter; the grounding check found no unknown citation either way, and no uncited sentence on any tracklist. On the eight regression tracks with insights, version 4 had 3 uncited of 115 sentences over five runs, each run under 5 percent (at most 1 of 22 to 25), and version 5 had 3 of 85 over four runs, two of them at or over 5 percent (1 of 20, 2 of 24): its insights are shorter, so one uncited sentence weighs more in a run. On the three thinnest of them (30, 37, 48), run ten more times each, uncited sentences fell from 5 of 56 to 1 of 51. Since replies vary from run to run, the gate now judges uncited sentences over a full sample of at least 50 sentences, pooled over a run's passes (`--repeat`; `architecture.md`, *Testing*), where they must be under 5 percent; a smaller sample is not judged, but fails on a gross share of 15 percent or more of its own sentences (`Gate.GROSS_UNCITED`: a single run of 20 sentences fails from 3 uncited). Version 5 passes it in a three-pass run of the regression tracks (0 of 61); its four single runs, 3 of 85 together, are each under 50 sentences and under 15 percent (1 of 20, 2 of 24), so not checked. Wordings tried in the same runs and dropped: asking for an id on every sentence, before its full stop, made the model reason at length before a few insights of each album, so the first rule keeps "every factual sentence". On thinly documented tracks a sentence still restates the player's release line (`[P1]`) and adds nothing; none of the four rules tried against it was worth its cost: forbidding a sentence that cites only player metadata made the model reason at length; "give it no sentence of its own" made it correct itself in the open on one-line tracks ("Wait, ..."), uncited; "do not restate" it, with "write fewer, even one", answered `NO_EVIDENCE` for a track with one usable line; and "do not pad it with the title, artists or release" left most of the padding. The sentence is cited and true; it is now stopped in code instead (*No restated release*, below).
- **No restated release** (prompt version 6, [#424](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/424)). On thinly documented tracks the model padded the insight with a sentence citing only the player's release line, `P1` ("This recording is on that same "Sundown" release [P1]"), right after a sentence citing MusicBrainz's "first released ..., on the album "Sundown"". The prompt now leaves the `P` line out when MusicBrainz's first-release line says the song first came out on an album of the same name (`PromptEvidence.restatesRelease`), so there is nothing to restate. The album is read from its own slot of that templated line (`MusicBrainzEvidence.firstReleaseAlbum`: "..., on the album "Sundown"."), after its date and type, whole even when its title holds the template's own words, and never from the track's title or the artist credit the line quotes too. The two names are compared whole, folded as an `ArtistFact`'s text is (case, spacing, quotation marks, apostrophes, dashes and a closing full stop), the player's less its closing bracketed parts, balanced and nested ones included, as long as a name is left (`PromptEvidence.edition`: the player's "Rubber Soul (Remastered)" is MusicBrainz's "Rubber Soul"). MusicBrainz's name is taken as it is: a song first released on "Sundown (Live)" came out on another edition than the studio "Sundown" the player reports, so that `P` line stays. So "Greatest Hits" is not "Greatest Hits II", a title track first released as a single keeps its line (the album it is on is another release), and a short name such as the Beatles' "1" counts only as the whole album's name. A first release on a single or an EP does not count, nor does a player's "Sundown - Single", whose single is not the album. Free-text lines (Wikipedia's "from his 1974 album Sundown", the artist's lines, an original song's, the web's) do not count either: a name found inside a sentence may be part of a longer one, or another recording's. Where the song first came out elsewhere, as for a compilation or a soundtrack, the `P` line stays, and a sentence citing it tells the listener where this recording comes from ("It appears on the soundtrack for Bang: The Bert Berns Story [P1]"). It holds whether *Don't repeat artist facts* is on or off ([#407](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/407)). The release stays in the prompt's track block either way, and the `P` line in the track's evidence and its Sources list; it is left out as the artist-level lines are, keeping the ids, so a reply citing it is `unknown` and not kept, and a `NO_EVIDENCE` reply is asked again with every line. The other candidate, flagging a sentence that cites only `P` lines in the grounding check and dropping it from the card, was compared on #399's recorded version 5 replies (the four tracklists and the regression tracks, 2026-10-25, no new calls): of 38 sentences citing only `P1` over 22 runs, 15 were on tracks where MusicBrainz's first-release line names the album, 7 of *Sundown*'s 8 among them; the other 23 named a compilation, a soundtrack or a release no other line gives, and say something. Dropping every such sentence would have cut those as well, cut 14 of the 30 insights of the three thinnest regression tracks to one sentence, removed text the card had already streamed, and saved no tokens, the sentence having been written; leaving the line out spares its tokens in every prompt that had it, an estimated 1 to 3 percent of a tracklist's input. Version 6 is a new prompt version though the template is version 5's, as version 4 was: the cards written before it on thinly documented tracks are the padded kind, and each track kept is written afresh once.
- **No cached prefix** (#399; the providers' documentation, read 2026-10-22). A cached prefix would need the fixed part first, before the track and its evidence, and long enough. Anthropic caches a prefix the request marks (`cache_control`) from 512 tokens on Claude Haiku 5.5, writes at 1.25 times the input price and reads at 0.1 times, for five minutes from its last use; OpenAI caches automatically from 1,024 tokens on GPT-5.6 and later; Google's implicit caching, on by default from Gemini 2.5, lists minimums of 2,048 to 4,096 tokens and none for Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite. The fixed part is an estimated 490 tokens (the 1,262 characters left out were 407 tokens), about 520 with *Model's own knowledge* on, just over Anthropic's 512 minimum and under the others'. It is not cached all the same: until prompt version 9 the prompt gave the data first, so the fixed part was no prefix, and a warm cache would save about four thousandths of a cent a call. Since version 9 (#767) the fixed part goes first, as the system prompt, so it is a prefix, but no client marks a cache ([#330](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/330)). Version 10's system prompt is larger than the estimate above: 836 input tokens on Claude Haiku 5.5, 867 with *Model's own knowledge* on (measured with `sleevesight-cli prompt`, 2026-11-04), so over Anthropic's 512 minimum and under OpenAI's 1,024 and Google's; a warm cache would still save only a fraction of a cent a call. Should a fixed part grow past a minimum (the genre and sounds-like cards' JSON contracts), the change is the system prompt sent with Anthropic's mark, and the cache's token counts in `Usage`, since Anthropic's `input_tokens` leaves out the tokens read from or written to the cache.
- **Grounding check, in code, before display.** Each sentence is classified as `ok`, `hedge` (states a gap), `model` (cites `AI`), `uncited`, or `unknown` (cites an id that does not exist, or cites `AI` when model knowledge is off). A reply written before 0.1.8 cites the model's own knowledge `M`, and reads as citing `AI` (#668). Uncited and unknown sentences are underlined; the counts are logged, and the card's note counts the sentences the sources do not back (#437), the full counts behind *Show grounding details*.
- **A reply that corrects itself shows its last version** ([#597](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/597)). On thinly documented tracks the model sometimes notices an unsupported sentence of its own and says so in the reply, then writes the insight again ("Wait, that second sentence is not supported by the evidence, so I'll drop it. Here is the corrected insight:"); the card showed the draft, the comment and the correction, the same fact twice and a claim the model itself withdrew. Before the check, such a correction is taken out (`SelfCorrection`), when two things hold. First, structure, which prose seldom meets by chance: what follows the line is a revision of what came before it, citing at least one line and only lines the draft before it cited, and opening with a sentence that restates the draft (at least three quarters of its words, each counted once, are the draft's), since a correction drops the unsupported sentences and restates the rest with the same evidence; prose that goes on with evidence of its own (`[W2]` after `[W1]`) is never cut, whatever its words, nor is prose that goes on to say something new from the same line, as on a track documented by one line, where every paragraph cites it ([#606](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/606)). Second, the line says it corrects the reply. It holds whole sentences (the text before it ends one, and so does the line, so a sentence wrapped over two lines is never one), sits in no quotation, is not wrapped in emphasis (a lyric's or a title's `*…*`), and cites nothing; what it quotes, in double or single quotation marks, is not read (an apostrophe is no quotation mark, inside a word, before a digit or opening a word, as in *I'll*, *'90s* or *'em*, nor is a `"` after a digit, as in a 12" single; a single mark opens a quotation only when one after it in its paragraph can close it, a plural's apostrophe closes none while another such mark follows, and a single quotation ends with its paragraph at the latest). Then it opens as only an aside does (*Wait,*, *Oops,*, *Hmm,*, *On second thought*, *Correction*) and speaks of the reply's making (a sentence, the evidence, a citation, the rules, the reply, the insight, the draft); or it opens as an aside may (*Actually*, *Let me*, *I'll*, *So*) and withdraws or corrects a part of the reply in the first person ("I'll drop it", "let me correct that", "I should correct the second sentence"); or its last sentence is only an introduction of the corrected version ("Here is the corrected insight:", "Corrected reply:", in bold too). Only what follows the last such line is shown and checked, without quotation marks around the whole of it (only when they are its only pair), so the counts describe what is shown. Words such as *evidence*, *rule* or *sentence* alone are not enough after *Actually* or *Here is*: they are also music's (Monk's "Evidence", "Rule, Britannia", a prison sentence); and a lyric that withdraws something ("So I'll cut you loose, I'll drop the act") withdraws no part of the reply. The draft and the comment are kept for the developer: the diagnostics log says the correction was taken out, *Show grounding details* adds *AI model's self-correction taken out*, and the tester's report and the CLI's report give the text taken out. The cache keeps the reply as written and the check takes the correction out again when it is served. The rule is narrow so an insight's own text is never cut: an aside on the same line as the new version, one that opens otherwise ("That second sentence is not supported."), or one before a version citing a line the draft did not or opening with a sentence of new words, is not found, and the reply shows as written with the aside underlined as uncited. While the reply streams, the draft shows until the check replaces it. Measured on the CLI's recorded replies of #399's and #424's runs (917 distinct replies, prompt versions 3 to 6, 2026-10-21 to 2026-10-25, Claude Haiku 5.5, no new calls): six replies corrected themselves, all on the regression playlist's one-line tracks under trial wordings of version 5; it finds five of them and no other reply, and leaves the sixth as written, since its version adds a sentence citing the player's line, `[P1]`, which its draft did not; the grounding check's stored replies (`grounding/replies.json`) read as before. With the restatement and single quotations added (#606), it finds the same five and no other reply. None of the 138 version 6 replies corrected itself; the phone's did, on version 6. The prompt is unchanged: a wording against it would need live runs on thinly documented tracks to show it costs less than the asides (`InsightPrompt.GAP_RULE` records three that cost more), and asking again when the check finds commentary would pay a second call for what code can take out. What it cannot take out is asked for again (#798, *Appendix. Prompt contracts*, *Insight*).
- **Cards that use general knowledge.** Genre descriptions, artist comparisons and suggestions have no retrieved source today. They are generated as structured JSON, rendered in a separately labelled "general knowledge" section, and never mixed into the grounded text. See Open questions.

### Genre labels

- Derived from tags filtered through a fixed genre vocabulary: Last.fm's, and from Phase 1.0 MusicBrainz's (D12). Tags outside the vocabulary (places, decades, "seen live", noise such as "bagel" or "under 2000 listeners") are dropped. The vocabulary began as the simulator's 79 tags; [#1011](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/1011) widened it with most of the genres MusicBrainz's tags use most that it dropped (the genre cloud of MusicBrainz's 200 most used genre tags, read on 2026-11-06), the genres of the cases seen on the phone (psychedelic rock, neo-psychedelia, Anatolian rock with Turkish psychedelia as its synonym, Afrobeat, world music), a few common genres of other regions, and the spellings Last.fm's listeners and MusicBrainz use for some of them (*synthpop*, *rock n roll*, *trip hop*, *dark wave*). Each new genre is defined as the rest are (`genre-vocabulary.json`'s `about`, `GenreDefinitionsTest`). A wider list lets more of a source's tags through, so a row reaches its five labels sooner and a later tag may drop off it; on the fixtures' tags only Jessiah's changed (*metalcore*, *metal*, *post-hardcore* for *metal*), and on MusicBrainz's artist tags for the test playlist's artists and the tracklists' (2026-11-06, live) the rows of 22 of 34 tagged artists changed, none losing its last label: mostly a more specific genre in the place of a broad one (Chuck Berry's *rock and roll* and *rockabilly* for *soul* and *classic rock*; Tame Impala's *psychedelic rock* and *neo-psychedelia*), at times a minority tag in the place of a closer one (Arctic Monkeys' *baroque pop* for *pop*). The genres named in the artist's text ([#1000](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/1000)) are matched against the same list, and *Search with AI*'s genres search chooses from it: the prompt names one tag per genre, since the reply is read through the list, which folds the other spellings, so the names grew from 79 (804 characters) to 219 (2,498 characters), about 450 tokens more on each genres search.
- The vocabulary is one list of tags, with no grouping ([#1020](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/1020)). It used to put each tag in a family with a fixed hue (folk, rock, pop, hip-hop, soul and R&B, reggae, electronic, soundtrack and classical, jazz and blues, and from [#1011](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/1011) world), the project's own grouping from the first simulator, for chips coloured by family and for the genre cards. The labels are plain since #439 (a hue per family made related genres match, but the code was arbitrary and unknown to the reader), and the cards were never built, so nothing read the families, and #1020 dropped them; the list keeps their order. If the genre cards ever need a grouping, an established one fits better than ours: Discogs' top-level genres, or MusicBrainz's parent genres.
- Labels have a level ([#439](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/439)): the track's, each artist's, the album's. An artist's tags describe a career, not the track playing (The Beatles: rock, pop, psychedelic; "Michelle": closer to a ballad), and both sources tag tracks and albums too. The track's come from Last.fm's track tags and MusicBrainz's recording tags, the latter with the recording lookup the resolution already makes; the artists' from both sources' artist tags; the album's from Last.fm's album tags. MusicBrainz's release tags would take a request of their own, so it gives no album labels. Last.fm's track and album tags are one request each per track, asked for once Last.fm shows the artist and has not rejected the primary one; the album's are kept per album while *Keep what sources said about each artist* is on (`architecture.md`, *Genre labels*). At most five labels a level, and per artist; a genre found on several levels shows on each. Folded, the track's labels lead only when backed ([#799](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/799)): at least two net votes on its MusicBrainz recording tag, or the album's or an artist's row has the genre; otherwise the album's lead, else the artists', and the track's show only when no other level has any. One contributor's one-vote tags on a recording (*rock, pop, alternative rock, indie rock* on a folk singer's song) do not outweigh the album's and the artists' labels. Last.fm's track tags carry weights out of 100, not votes, so they lead only when another level has the genre. Weak labels stay in the *Genre tags* table, which shows every level's as before; the rest of the genres (*Why is this …?*, what the genre is, *Search with AI*'s facts) read every level's labels, not the folded ones. The genres the AI's genre list adds (#762, *Home screen*, *Genre labels*) are the track's, credited to the model alone; they follow the folded chips and neither lead nor back, so the chips shown before the list came stay as they were. Which labels open a genre card is unchanged: an artist's, as before, unless [#101](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/101) decides otherwise.
- Instrumental is weighed against signs that the track is sung ([#996](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/996)): an artist's Instrumental tag describes a career, and was shown on a sung track with a featured vocalist. Lyrics found (MusicBrainz's lyrics links for the work, or a page *Find the lyrics* found) drop Instrumental from every row, the track's own included. A language MusicBrainz gives the work (not `zxx` or `und`), a `vocal` credit on the recording, or, on an artist's or the album's row, a featured artist (the track credits several, and one of them, other than the row's own, has no Instrumental among their artist's tags, or no tags) leave it at most weak, its chip marked as a weak genre's and its line naming the sign: the vocalist, the language, or the featured artist, in that order. The AI's Instrumental (its genre list, or *Search with AI*'s genres) is weighed as the track's own: weak with a vocalist, a lyricist (#998) or a language, still marked AI. An artist's Instrumental counts as theirs past the five tags a source shows, so a featured artist tagged it sixth is not taken for one without it (a result kept before this decodes without it, until it expires). A track's own Instrumental with fewer than two votes, backed only by artists' or album rows the featured rule weakens, is weak too, with the same line. The recording marked instrumental by MusicBrainz, or only `zxx` works, leaves it as tagged. The signs come with MusicBrainz's result (`WorkLyrics`: links, languages, the instrumental mark, the vocal credits), which the resolution looks up anyway; every other genre is weighed as before.
- LRCLIB ([#997](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/997)), an open lyrics library (free, no key), is one more sign, for tracks MusicBrainz says nothing about. It is asked only when a row of the genres has Instrumental (a source's tag, or a genre the AI's list added) and MusicBrainz has not marked the recording instrumental: once per track, with the labels after the evidence (the AI's added genre from the genre's panel, `GenreNotes.libraryLyrics`), never for the next track in the queue, under *Look up in the background* as the labels are. `/api/get` takes the first artist, the normalized title, the album when the player names one, and the player's length in whole seconds, which LRCLIB matches within 2 seconds; with no length it is not asked, since a wrong match must not drop a real Instrumental, nor when normalizing the title removed anything but a suffix known to name the song's own recording (a remaster with or without its year, a radio or single edit, the single's or the album's version, mono, stereo, explicit, clean) or removed a variant (`LibraryQuery.removedVersion`), since "Song - Off Vocal Version" asked as "Song" would get the vocal mix of the same length. Lyrics found (plain or synced, not marked instrumental) drop Instrumental from every row, as MusicBrainz's lyrics links do; LRCLIB's `instrumental` mark leaves it as tagged and lifts the weak signs above (language, vocal credit, featured artist), as MusicBrainz's mark does, and where the two disagree the mark wins. A 404 is no answer, not instrumental. The answer, a 404's included, is kept with the track's findings in the retrieval cache under the whole query, artist, title, album and length in seconds (`PipelineCache.libraryLyrics`, LRCLIB's lifetime, 7 days, as Last.fm's), so a replay asks nothing and another album is asked again, its lifetime a row under *History* as every source's; a failure is not kept, and the *Genres* panel offers *Retry*, shown only while the genres still call for LRCLIB. A *Retry* of another source asks LRCLIB when it brings an Instrumental the run before never asked about. `/api/get` is used, the endpoint LRCLIB documents, with a 10 s timeout; a request with no album is answered (checked live, 2026-11-07). The panel asks about the AI's Instrumental only once the run's labels are in (so the run and the panel never both ask about one Instrumental), holds an answer no longer than LRCLIB's lifetime, and shows its own failure with a *Retry* of its own, the failure kept for the play, so reopening *Genres* asks nothing until *Retry* or the next play (a failure is never kept in the retrieval cache); one request per query is in flight at a time for both (`SharedLyricsLibrary`). Its switch under *Sources* is on by default. Its User-Agent names the app's site as LRCLIB asks, and a 429 is retried after its `Retry-After` plus up to a second of jitter (`HostPolicy.contact`, `HostPolicy.retryJitter`). An Instrumental only an artist's description names (#1000, its artist's hint) takes LRCLIB's answer as any other does: lyrics drop it, the mark lifts the hint; asking LRCLIB about it is #998's. The *Lyrics* panel could use the same answer (lyrics known to exist), out of scope for now.
- Genres an artist's description names ([#1000](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/1000)): a plain match of the vocabulary's names (each tag and synonym, folded as the labels are: case, punctuation and spacing aside) against the artist's own lines among those *Sources* lists (their Last.fm biography, Wikipedia's article on them, the web pages about them, #912), whatever has arrived, in every mode; no AI. On word boundaries, a hyphenated word whole (*electro-acoustic* names no Acoustic, *folklore* no Folk), a name of several words whole and the longest first. Not the track's or the album's lines, Wikidata's or MusicBrainz's templated facts, or Last.fm's tag and listener lines, and never the artist's own name (*Soul II Soul*). One per genre per artist, citing the first line that names it. In *Genre tags* they have a row of their own, *Described* (with several artists *Described 1*, *Described 2*, numbered as the artist's row), right after the artist's tags; in *Tag sources*, "{artist}’s description" and the citations of its lines, each a link to the line in *Sources*. A genre the artist's own tags give is left off that row; one another level's tags give stays on it, but its chip is the tag's. Their chips follow the tags', before the AI's genre list's, at most five an artist, as a row of tags, counted after Instrumental drops with lyrics found and after what the artist's tags gain; a genre a tag or the AI's list gives keeps that chip. A screen reader reads them "…, from the artist’s description". Under such a genre's definition, before *Why is this …?*: "Named in the artist’s description: “{quote}”" and the citation, `{quote}` the line's sentence that names it (within Last.fm's attribution, "The Last.fm biography of … says: "…"", the line's own quotation marks kept), at most five words either side of the genre, "…" where cut. Instrumental found this way is the artist's hint of [#998](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/998): weak, with "The artist’s description calls them instrumental, but no source says so of this track: “{quote}”" and the citation in place of that line, until #998's check of the track's vocals lifts or drops it; MusicBrainz marking the recording instrumental lifts it; #996's rules apply as to any Instrumental (lyrics found drop it; a sign of vocals keeps it weak and names itself). They join no genre list a prompt carries (*Search with AI*'s facts and the AI's genre list read the tags' rows; the insights read the lines themselves): only *Why is this …?* on one names it, as its question, asked on the track's own evidence and keyed by it as any genre's reason when that evidence holds the line it was read from (a Last.fm biography, a Wikipedia article), else (the artist's web lines, #912) with the lines *Sources* lists and keyed by their fingerprint. *Search with AI* still shows under them for a track with no tags. A genre name that is an everyday word too (*house*, *country*, *soul*, *dance*, *pop*, *score*, *alternative*, … one list in the code, `EverydayNames`) counts only in a music phrase: followed by a music noun (*music*, *band*, *singer*, *producer*, *guitarist*, *DJ*, *orchestra*, *sound*, *label* and the like, or their plurals), past other genres before it ("an ambient electronic duo", "a jazz and soul singer"): "a house in Berlin", "country of origin", "soul of the city" and "dance floor" name none, "a house producer" and "a punk guitarist" do. A music noun counts as written: a capitalised one is a name ("Sub Pop Records" names no Pop). Instrumental names no genre before an everyday or a work's word ("instrumental in", "an instrumental role", "two instrumental tracks", "an instrumental version") or as the noun ("their single was an instrumental."). A name of several words, and a word that is only a genre, match as they are, but for *new wave* and *new age*, guarded as those words are unless capitalised ("New Wave"); and several words name only a tag of several words ("dance hall" is no Dancehall, "high life" no Highlife). A plain match is still blind to negation ("not a jazz band").
- Whether a track is instrumental, where Instrumental is in doubt ([#998](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/998)): the free signs first, then a paid check. ### Caching and offline

- Retrieval is cached per source and lookup key, as the evidence and outcome the app uses, never the raw response; synthesis per kind, entity (the track; for an artist's or album's insight, the artist or album, #240), the exact evidence it was written from (the track's whole evidence, not the part the prompt sent, #239; for an artist's or album's insight, the evidence gathered for the artist or album), prompt version and style, provider, model and the model-knowledge switch (`architecture.md`, *Cache and history*).
- Offline, cached tracks still work fully; uncached lookups fail with `unreachable`, never with a blank card. The home screen says the phone is offline while a source is unreachable, and once it is back online the playing track's unreachable sources are asked again, once per return ([#192](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/192)).
- Each insight is a paid call on the user's key, so when one is written is a user setting, *Reactive* (D5; once *Write insights automatically*), in order of how much is written automatically: *On demand* (once *Now*, and *Manual* before it), *On track skip (auto)* (once *On track skip*, and *On skip* before it, [#615](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/615)), or *On track change (auto)* (the default; [#625](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/625) for the current names). Under *On track skip (auto)*, user-driven changes (a skip in the player, select, play) write, and tracks that change on their own get evidence and a Write button. The default went with writing the next track's insight ahead (D13); Spotify's queue gives no album or length to key it on ([#172](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/172)), so the card is written when the track starts, its lookups mostly done ahead (#20). After a cold start nothing is written automatically until the user's first action: a tap on *Write insight* or *Rewrite insight*, or an automatic mode chosen, in the split button's menu or in *Reactive* (the current one included, #625). That action lasts as long as the app's process, often days, so automatic writing pauses once the app has not been on screen for more track changes than a user setting, *Saver*, names (5 by default, or off) ([#203](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/203)); opening the app resumes it, but a track that came to play while it was paused, the one playing at the return included, is written only on a tap (#625). The insight card's split button (*Home screen*, [#615](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/615)) is the same setting: its menu holds *Reactive*'s three choices.

## User interface

Material 3, with dynamic color taken from the album art (material-color-utilities from the art bitmap), light or dark as the phone is unless the user chooses one (*Theme*, #444), and its text in Roboto, bundled with the app, unless the user chooses the system font (*Settings*, #377); the notes of Home's guide are in a hand-drawn font, Shantell Sans, bundled too ([#627](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/627), [#672](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/672)). The prototype is the reference for layout.

Links, headings and switches leave the palette's accent colour out ([#442](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/442)): the system's accent (on Android 12 and later, from the wallpaper or the chosen colour theme) is out of place beside the art's colours, and links were styled two ways. Every link is in the colour of the text around it, with a thin line a little below the text, clear of the descenders and under its glyphs only, in either writing direction (one style, `LinkedText`); focused from a keyboard, hovered or pressed, it takes a faint tint of the text colour. A highlight, a faint rounded fill of the text colour (10 %) behind the link, from above the capitals to below the descenders (darker than the background in light mode, lighter in dark mode), was tried as well and not chosen: on the home screen it reads as one more chip beside the genre chips, the citations and the sources' tags. Both stay to be tried on the phone over real album art, as the developer setting *Link style* ([#519](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/519)): *Underline*, the default, or *Highlight*. Once one is kept, the other goes and the setting with it, or it moves to *Appearance*. Settings' section headings are bold, in the text colour. A switch is in the text colour when on, its thumb in the background's (black and white, white and black in dark mode), and Material's greys when off. The text keeps WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1) and the switches' states 3:1, in the app's colours and the art's. The rest of the palette stays as it is; a fixed palette is a setting for later (#443).

**The developer mark** ([#536](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/536)): everything only developer mode shows is outlined, so a tester's screen and screenshots show what other users will not see. The rule: whatever appears only in developer mode is wrapped in the one `DeveloperMark` (`ui/theme/DeveloperMark.kt`), and nothing a user outside developer mode sees is. The outline is 1.5 dp, with rounded corners and a little room inside, and a small *Developer* tag (10 sp, in the mark's colour, on the surface colour, with a thin border of the mark's colour) sits across its top edge near the end; the content starts below the tag, so it never covers it. The tag is drawn, not composed, so a screen reader reads the screen as unmarked: the *Developer* section's heading already names the section. The colour is a token of its own, `DeveloperMark`: teal, `#00796B` in light mode and `#4DD0C0` in dark mode, fixed rather than taken from the dynamic colours or the art, which could take on any hue; blue was ruled out as the code font's and often the phone's accent. Its contrast is above the 3:1 an outline needs (WCAG 1.4.11) on every surface tone the app uses: in light mode 5.1:1 on the surface (tone 98) and 4.1:1 on the darkest container (tone 90), in dark mode 9.8:1 on the surface (tone 6) and 6.5:1 on the lightest container (tone 22), and the tag's text, on the surface, above 4.5:1. Marked: the *Developer* section as a whole, each player's package name, Home's report button, its menu and the message after its *Hide*, the tester's report's own part of its review, what *Show grounding details* adds on Home (the full counts, *Sources*' table with the sources that are off and the diagnostic statuses, the rejected matches), and the diagnostics screen with its reset's confirmation. A dialog opens in a window of its own, so one only developer mode shows is marked in itself. The outline is at least as wide as the tag, with narrower content (the report button) centred in it; marked or not, the content keeps its state and focus, as only the modifier changes. The developer setting *Mark developer items*, on by default, turns the mark off.

**The AI mark** ([#624](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/624)): a call to the AI service is paid for on the user's key, so every button whose tap makes one says so. The rule: its label is shown through the one `AiLabel` (`ui/home/AiMark.kt`), which ends it in a small, dimmed **AI**, and no other button's is. The mark is set as the source chips' letters were before *Sources*' table (#662) (*MB*, *W*: `labelSmall`, at three quarters of the label's colour), but after the label, inside the button, on the label's baseline rather than centred. It takes the button's content colour, so a disabled button keeps its mark, dimmed with it. A screen reader reads the button as "<label>, uses AI", and the mark itself is hidden from it; the two are read as one only inside a button, which merges them. Marked: the insight card's writes (*Write insight*, *Rewrite insight*), and *Why is this …?* once it comes (#523); and, the one mark that is not on a button, a genre the AI's genre list added to the chips and the *Track* row, and the model among a row's *Tag sources* (#762), where it says where the genre came from (read "Blues, from Claude Haiku 5.5" and "Claude Haiku 5.5, AI"; the same mark meaning two things was noted when it was picked); not *Ask AI* (#773; *Search with AI* until #802), whose plain label was picked, its name saying it, but in the *Lyrics* panel, where *Find the lyrics* and *Explain the song* are marked (#908); not *Look again* in its menu (#617), which only looks the sources up; not *Stop writing* or the split button's chevron, which make no call. Home's buttons, the split button and *Why is this …?*, are as tall as its genre chips, 32 dp, each answering a tap across 48 dp; the genre table's cells stay smaller.

**The AI credit** ([#811](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/811)): every part of a screen an AI model wrote names the model that wrote that text, not the one selected now (a part kept from an earlier play by another model names that model), by the name *AI services* lists it under (`ListedModelNames`), or by its id where it is no longer listed. A genre's reason (#762): under its text, once written, with *Read aloud* at the end of that row. An *Ask AI* answer (#773, #801): just under it, above "From the AI model's own knowledge, not verified" for an answer from the model's knowledge (#851). The exception: a genre the AI's genre list added to the chips (#762) shows only its **AI** mark, with no "by {model}"; a screen reader still names the model ("Blues, from Claude Haiku 5.5") and *Tag sources* lists it, as item 4 says. While the player line under the header says *Playing*, the header logo's record turns (#848), clockwise, at *Logo speed*'s 33⅓ rpm (an LP's, one turn in 1.8 s; the default) or 45 rpm (a single's, 1⅓ s). It gets up to speed in 1 s and comes to rest in 2 s, its speed changing evenly, as a turntable's, and rests wherever it stops: it never snaps back upright, and starts again from there. It goes by the same test as the player line's state (`playing`, `HomeLabels.kt`), so the two never disagree. It moves only on frames while the app is on screen: off screen it asks for none, and back on screen it goes on from where it was, the time away not counted; where it was, and how fast, is also kept while Home is not shown and across a configuration change. In a debug build the bug in the record's place (#652) never turns, neither as D's intro brings it in nor while music plays. Where the record would turn, the bug crawls: after a rest of 4 s it goes straight up out of its place and out of the header past its top, comes back in from the header's bottom, and crawls up to its place, swaying a little from side to side. It is drawn only inside the header's box, never over the status bar or what is under the header. A trip takes 1.5 s at 33⅓ rpm and is 1.35 times as fast at 45 rpm (about 1.1 s); the rest is the same at either speed, and a change of speed during a trip takes effect from there. When the music stops during a trip, the bug finishes it and stays in its place; it stays there under *Remove animations*, while the setting is off, and off screen. The settings' texts are the release build's either way: a debug build is the developer's own.

The record comes in three variants, each the product page's `#lp` symbol (16 units across, the rim at 7.5):

- **Medium, the header** (#848): the label 56% of the rim's radius (4.2 units), the white face scaled ×1.4 with it, the groove or core as the mark's, so the turning shows at the header's 24 dp. The header's logo and the logo the intro lands; the record's label grows from the mark's to the header's as it flies, so the hand-over does not jump.
- **Small, the spinners** (#785): the label 76% (5.7 units, out to where the core ends), no groove and no core, the face scaled with the label. Beside a message that something is under way, at text size.

Each message that something is under way (#785), on Home (*Looking…*, *Looking on MusicBrainz…*, *Gathering evidence…*) and elsewhere in the app, has a small turning record before it, the logo's record simplified, at text size, by the message's first line when it wraps. *Type on a computer*'s *Stop* is an outlined button at the right of its message, centred on it however many lines it takes. ### Settings screen

The settings (#444) are a list of sections, divided by rules, each opening as *Sources* and the genres do on Home (#649). Closed, a section is a chip with its name, drawn as Home's chips are (`ChipFrame`, unmarked), every chip as wide as the widest name (*Developer*'s included), measured in the chip's style with the app's language, font and font scale, the name centred; beside it, centred on the chip, the section's short description, up to two lines, ellipsised past them (*Developer* has none). A tap on the chip opens the section's panel in place of the chip and its description, drawn as Home's (`Panel`): the name as a heading, *Close* at its top right, then the fuller explanation where one is needed, then the section's settings; *Close* turns it back into the chip. Any number of sections can be open at once, so several can be read or compared; a list of sections each opening a page of its own was set aside, as it means going back and forth between pages. Every section is closed when the settings open; within one visit each stays as left, through a rotation, and nothing of it is stored (`SectionFolds`). A screen reader reads each chip as a heading and a button, by the section's name, "collapsed", and the panel's name as a heading; the focus moves to *Close* on opening and back to the chip on closing, a keyboard's and a screen reader's (`FocusHandoff`, as on Home). The sections, in order: *Players*, *Sources*, *AI services*, *Secrets*, *Genres*, *Insights*, *History*, *Appearance*, *About*, and *Developer*, which shows only in developer mode, last and with no descriptions. The sections are drawn by one composable (`FoldedSection`), so their style is set in one place.

**Descriptions fold to one line** (#847): every setting's description, a section's explanation at the top of its panel and the notes over a group of rows (*Read aloud…*) show folded, their whole text (paragraphs joined by a space) cut to one line, ending in an ellipsis, which alone marks them: no chevron, nothing in the open state. A tap on one unfolds it to its paragraphs and links, another folds it again. On a switch row the description's tap is its own: it folds and unfolds, and only the name and the switch toggle; its tap reaches only where it is drawn, not Compose's wider minimum touch area, which would reach over the name, and further below it into the padding under it, where nothing else is (#874), laid out as if it did not, so a tap a little low still folds it rather than toggling the switch: 8 dp where the description ends its row, 4 dp in a note over a group of rows (its own padding) and over a button group (the room before it, since the buttons' own taps reach past their bounds only by Compose's widening, which a tap within the description's would lose to), and none where a line follows it (a note, a link, an address). A disabled row's description still folds. A description that fits on one line does not fold: no tap and no fold state. Not folded: names, values, switches, the line under a button group saying what the chosen value does (*Stop after*'s too) and *No players yet* (#874), and the lines that say a setting's state or what it needs (a model found unavailable, *Needs your Exa key.*, *Exa web search is disabled.*, *See Settings → Sources.*, the key links, *Open website*), which a switch row or a value row takes apart from its description (`note`), a switch row reading it with the switch. Two rows under *Appearance*, right after *Song, Artist and Album on a new track*, set it: *Foldable descriptions* (on by default; off, every description shows in full and nothing folds, as before), and *Descriptions on return*, a button group, disabled while the switch is off: how the descriptions show on coming back, which is opening the settings again, a section again within a visit, or the settings again from a page they opened (*Credits*, *Diagnostics*, *What leaves the device*; #874), though not a rotation, which keeps them: *Folded* folds that section's descriptions again, *Unfolded* opens them (a tap still folds one), *As left* (the default) keeps each as it was left. A section's descriptions follow the choice as it was when the section was last opened, or the settings for one not opened since (`DescriptionFolds.modeOf`), so a change of the choice waits for the next return and nothing on the screen moves under the tap (#874). *As left* lasts across restarts: the descriptions left unfolded are stored with the settings (`Settings.unfoldedDescriptions`), each by its section and its row's name as shown (a provider's name in it keeps each provider's rows apart; a section's explanation and a note by their own string's name), so the first visit ever starts folded. Shown in the app's language, a row's name changes with it: after a change of language the descriptions show folded again, as on a first visit, which is all that is lost. The stored list keeps the latest 200, the oldest change going first, so names no longer used do not pile up. A tap on a description a search found folds or opens it for that search only, through a rotation too, and is not stored. Within a visit, and through a rotation, the taps are held in `DescriptionFolds`; the settings' links to those pages mark it as left (`DescriptionFolds.leave`), and restored with that mark, on the way back, it applies *Descriptions on return* as on opening the settings, so nothing changes while the settings leave. The mark is cleared once the settings are on screen again (resumed, `DescriptionFolds.comeBack`), which applies the choice where nothing was restored, as when Android's back closes a page still opening and the settings were never taken down; a rotation after that keeps the taps. A search shows each description it finds unfolded, the query marked in it; a tap folds it for that search only, and clearing the search leaves each as it was before. A screen reader reads a foldable description as a node of its own, the whole text whether folded or not, *collapsed* or *expanded*, with the action *Expand* or *Collapse*; the switch row's own node reads its name and state. *Restore dismissed messages* is the last row of *Appearance*.

**A few short choices are a button group** (#637): a setting with up to about four choices whose labels fit the row's width at the phone's normal font size shows them in its row, under its name and description, as a connected button group, after Material 3's (m3.material.io/components/button-groups): one tap chooses, with no dialog, and the row follows the stored value, whatever changed it. The group is one shared composable (`ChoiceButtonGroup`, `ui/theme/ButtonGroup.kt`), which also lays out a row of plain actions, each enabled or not (`ActionButtonGroup`), for a list's paging. Drawn as the app's other controls are: each button at least 32 dp high, as Home's chips and the insight card's split button, in a row at least 48 dp high, so each answers a tap across 48 dp; 2 dp between buttons; the group's outer corners round (16 dp) and the corners where two meet small (4 dp); the chosen one round all round, filled in the text's colour with the surface's for its label and a check before it, the others a light fill of the text's colour (`chipColours`). The buttons share the row equally when every label fits so, else each takes what its label needs and the room left is shared in proportion to it; every button keeps room for the check, so none moves when another is chosen. The group is always one row (#821; a stacked fallback looked bad and jumped between row and stack): where the labels do not fit it on one line, as at a large font size, each button is at least as wide as its label's longest word, the room left shared in proportion to what each still needs, and a label wraps in its button between its words, never inside one; where even the words do not fit with room for the check, as at twice the font size on a 360 dp phone, the group shows no check, the fill alone marking the chosen one. All of it is built from one measure (the labels in the buttons' style, the app's language, font and font size). Labels are kept to a word where they can (#821: *Boxes* / *Pages*, *App* / *System*, *Lower* / *Pause*), what they mean said in the setting's description. A screen reader reads a choice as one group of radio buttons (`selectableGroup`, each `Role.RadioButton`), the chosen one selected, each read after the setting's name ("Theme: Light"), so a button reached straight away says what it sets, and with its description where the choices have one (*Report button*'s, as its dialog read them, #550); the line under the buttons is a polite live region, so a new choice's description is announced, and while a search finds another choice's description, that one shows there too, by its label. An action reads as a button, disabled where it is.

**Dimmed: not applying, or set by another setting** (#947): a setting that does not apply while another is as it is (*Reactive* while *Show insights* is off, *Stop after* while nothing is written automatically) dims as a whole, its name, description and the line under it with its switch or buttons, and cannot be changed. A service with no key saved has its switch off and disabled, so it cannot be switched on: only the switch dims, its name, description and badge staying as they are, since the service still applies (#999). It says why above the link ("Needs your Exa key."), which a screen reader hears with the switch, as unavailable; a key stored but no longer readable counts as saved here, and the row says it cannot be read. The rule is on the stored settings, not only the screen (`KeyGate`): whenever the keys or the settings change, a service switched on whose key has no entry is switched off and saved so, which brings an earlier version's settings (a service on with no key) up off, and switches services off when their key is removed or every key purged; and a key saved where none was stored first writes the services that need it off (failing the save, as storage failing, if that write fails), so a key saved again after a failed write never brings its service back on, across a restart too. Saving a key switches nothing on by itself: the switch becomes usable, and the service is switched on only as the listener chooses right after the save, on *Load keys*' services to enable or in the key dialog (#763), so which AI service is on stays the listener's choice. A rule applied as the settings are read would leave the stored switch on, and saving the key would then switch the service back on by itself. On Home, a keyed source switched off says *off*, not that it needs its key; *needs your key* remains for a source on whose saved key can no longer be read. Where no link is shown (Last.fm's genre labels), the note names the place ("Needs your Last.fm key, in Secrets."). Every link from one setting to another works so: it opens the section it leads to as its panel and lands on the row, or on the panel's heading, whose *Close* takes the focus, as on opening. A model shows by its name, with its id under it in the model picker.

**Developer mode** works as Android's: *About* has a *Version* row (`versionName (versionCode)`), and seven taps on it turn developer mode on; from the fourth, a short message counts down ("3 taps from developer mode"), the seventh says "Developer mode is enabled", and later taps "Developer mode is already enabled". The *Developer* section then holds the options for a developer or tester (*Diagnostics*, *Show grounding details*, *Report button*, *Link style*, *Mark developer items*, and the players' package names under *Players*), and a *Developer mode* switch, which turns developer mode off, turns its options off with it (*Report button* to *Hide*, *Link style* to *Underline*, *Mark developer items* back on), and hides the section. What only developer mode shows is marked as such (*User interface*, the developer mark). Developer mode is a stored setting, so it survives a restart. It is off by default, except in a build for Play's testing tracks, which starts in developer mode so testers reach *Diagnostics* without the seven taps ([#451](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/451); `design/release.md`, *Test and production builds*); a choice the user made holds in either build. A later developer-only option goes in that section, and is turned off with developer mode.

**Load keys and Purge keys** (#446) open screens of their own, each a secure window (`FLAG_SECURE`): no screenshot or screen recording shows them. A screen reader reads each step as a heading, and says a result as it replaces what was there.

- *Load keys*' row says *Save several API keys at once from one QR code.*, and under it, apart from the row so that each link is a target of its own, where the code is made and the guide to it, both links (`sleevesight.app/api-keys`, and the docs' *Several keys at once: Load keys*; #541). The screen starts with the words that seal the code (#590): what they do, *1. On your computer, open the API keys page:* with its link (`sleevesight.app/api-keys`; the CLI's `keys-qr` is for the developer docs, not named in the app, #541), *2. Type these words there. The first three letters of each are enough.*, and five words drawn at random (`SecureRandom`) from the EFF's short wordlist 2 (`KeyWords`), each on a line of its own in `headlineMedium`, its first three letters bold, and that the phone takes one code made with them within 5 minutes (`WORDS_LIFETIME`, measured on `SystemClock.elapsedRealtime`, which counts deep sleep, and checked at the scan and when the words show) and forgets them when the screen is left; *New words*, *Next* and *Cancel*. The words live only in the screen's state, never in saved state, so a recreated screen draws new ones; expired, they are hidden and only *New words* is offered. *Next* shows the camera's step: *3. Then give the page your keys, and it draws the QR code, as its instructions explain:*, with the link, the camera's view in the middle, and *4. Read the QR code with the camera.*, with *Show the words* and *Cancel*. A sealed code is opened off the main thread (*Opening the code…*, about a second on a slow phone, the heading with the turning record before it and no progress bar, #785), and the words are then spent; one sealed with other words is refused, *The words don’t match this code*, with *Show the words* and *Scan again* (the words stay good); after they expired or were spent, *The words have expired* or *These words were used*, with *New words*; a code that cannot be read, *A damaged sealed code*. A clear code (version 1) is read as before, and says under its keys that it was not sealed. The view holds a rounded square frame, 0.9 of the picture's shorter side, so a code on a computer's screen fits it easily, the picture around it dimmed (a narrow margin; 0.7 until #541). Not blurred: the picture is a `SurfaceView`'s, which `RenderEffect` cannot reach; a blur would need a `TextureView`. Once a code is read, the view gives way to the keys to save (#763): the heading asks *Which of the following successfully scanned keys do you want to save?* (*Do you want to save this key?* for one, *No keys to save* for none, with no table and no *Next*), then a table, *Key*, *Group*, *Ending* and *Save* (headers at the start; cells start, start, end, centre), sorted by group (*AI*, *Music*, *Search*, dimmed), then name, each key's service with *Save as new* or *Replace existing* under it, and a checkbox, ticked; under it *Only the selected keys will be saved* and *Each is stored encrypted on this phone, and sent only to its own service.*, and, if any key did not scan, *The following keys did not scan correctly:* in the error colour with a list: a refused key by its name and ending (the setup code keeps only the last four characters of a refused key, and none of one no longer than that or named twice), its name alone with no ending, and the names not known with the lines not read as *n unknown keys*. An AI key's terms note and a clear code's warning stay under it. *Cancel*, *Scan again* (only when a key did not scan) and *Next*, which saves the keys ticked, with no confirmation, and is dimmed while none is; a save that fails names the keys not saved and tries only those again. Then *Which services do you want to enable?*: a table, *Key*, *Group* and *Enable*, of the services whose keys were saved, by group and name: a source (Last.fm, as a source and for genre labels; Exa web search, with web search) ticked; one new AI key, with no AI key saved before, ticked; with more than one AI service to choose from (an AI service on that needs no key, Ollama, counts, though it has no row, and *Your current AI service is …* names it), radios, one or none selected (a selected one can be unselected), a new AI key unselected unless it replaces the key of the AI service on; when the load holds an AI key, the AI services whose keys were saved before and not loaded now under *Already configured*, in the same table so their radios line up, the one on selected. Under it *Your current AI service is …* (when that one is on), *Only one AI service can be enabled at a time.* (with radios), and where the services are, with links to *Sources*, *Genres* and *AI services*, each of which leaves *Load keys* and opens its section. *Cancel* keeps the keys and changes no switch; *Next* writes the choices to the stored settings (in the key dialog, in the same work in `saves` as the key, so a rotation during the save does not lose them). A service is named everywhere as *Sources* and *AI services* name it (*Exa web search*, *Google*). Then the summary: *Keys saved* (*Key saved*), a table *Key*, *Ending*, *Saved* (*New* or *Replaced*), and *Services configured*, a table *Service*, *State* (*Enabled* or *Disabled*) of the services whose keys were saved and any AI service whose state changed, both by name, with *Done*; the services as written, or, when the phone's storage failed, *The services could not be switched: …* in their place, with *Try again*. These tables (`ChoiceTable`) have no dotted leaders or shading; each column but the first is as wide as its widest cell, the first takes the rest, and a screen reader reads a row as one item from its first cell, with its checkbox or radio: *Anthropic, AI, ending q 7 R k, Save as new*. *Purge keys*' list of keys is a table of its own (`KeyTable`, #542): two columns, *API Key* and *Ending*, each as wide as its widest cell and aligned to the start; a row names the key's service (*Anthropic*, *OpenAI*, *Google Gemini*, *Last.fm*, *Exa*), with a note under it where there is one (*Can no longer be read*), and its last four characters with no ellipsis, in a code font (monospace, in a blue of the theme's lightness on a faint fill of it; the app's own blue, not the wallpaper's accent). After *Delete*, it says the keys listed were deleted, or, when a step failed, which one (removing the entries from the phone's storage, deleting the Keystore key, or both), as `SecretStore.reset` reports each, and the keys left, with *Try again*.

Labelled by where they came from: "Played this session" by default, and "In this playlist · <name>" when the player names the playlist, as Spotify does in its metadata (see *Playlist context*); the optional Spotify account adds the tracks beyond the queue.

### Key dialog

A key's dialog (#432) says who gets the key, and holds its field, masked, with three trailing icons (Material Symbols, outlined, weight 400): an eye, then *Scan QR code* (`qr_code_scanner`) and *Type on a computer* (`keyboard_external_input`), each a 48 dp button whose name is its content description and its tooltip. The eye names what a tap does: the open eye (`visibility`, *Show key*) while the field is masked, the crossed one (`visibility_off`, *Hide key*) while it shows. The dialog never loads the saved key, so the eye can show only what was typed or typed on a computer in it; a key read from a QR code, a plain key's or a setup code's, is never shown (#702): keys are lent to a tester as a setup code, and *Load keys* shows them only by their last four characters, so a scanned key never enters the text field, which holds as many mask dots for it while the dialog keeps the key apart (`KeyField`, set by the scan, not found by comparing text) and *Save* saves it. Nothing that reads the text field gets it then: not the eye, which is not there meanwhile, nor the field's undo history, a copy or cut (a password field's anyway), the keyboard app through its input connection, an autofill request (Compose gives autofill the field's raw value whatever its content type, and an open autofill request is cancelled when the key arrives), or an accessibility service. Any edit replaces the scanned key whole: a deletion empties the field, and what is typed, pasted or filled in its place is the user's own, which the eye can show; so is text typed on a computer, which replaces the field's. To keep that plain, the eye is there only while the field holds something, the hint says *A saved key is never shown again. You can only preview the key you enter before it's saved.*, and the field is masked again when it empties, when the dialog closes, and when the activity stops (the app left, the screen locked; autofill is left alone then, since a password manager's unlock screen stops the activity too). The eye comes first, so the other two keep their place when it appears; they are off while a keyboard session runs, and all three while a save runs (a removal runs in the confirmation over it), but the eye stays on during a session, to check what arrives. While shown, the field stays a password to autofill (`ContentType.Password`) and to accessibility services alike: Compose marks it a password from its keyboard type, which stays `Password` while shown so that the keyboard neither learns nor suggests the key; a keyboard type that let TalkBack read the key as plain text would let the keyboard learn it too. A keyboard session's code scanned with the QR icon starts the session, since it cannot be a key. Its buttons are the same for every key (#543): *Cancel* at the start, away from the actions, a text button, and at the end *Remove*, only while a key is stored, and *Save*, the main action, a filled button. *Remove* deletes the key, so it is a danger action: an outlined button in the theme's error colour, outline and text, with the trash can (`ic_delete`), named for the key it removes to a screen reader (*Remove the Last.fm API key*). It asks first, with the confirmation the key's row uses (*Remove the Last.fm API key?*), whose buttons are laid out the same: *Cancel* at the start, the red *Remove* at the end; once the key is removed, both close, and a removal that fails is said in the confirmation. Where the line is too short, as at a large font scale, the buttons wrap in their order to the end of a line below. An AI service's terms open with the agreement, *Using this key means agreeing to …*, in bold, as a commitment; the rest of them plain. Each icon's scan is a guided screen laid out as *Load keys*' is (#541; a secure window too): *Scan QR code* says what code (*1. Generate a QR code of this one key on your computer*), links `sleevesight.app/api-keys`, then the camera, *2. Read the QR code with the camera.* and *Cancel*; a setup code holding this key alone, as that page makes from one key, fills the field; one naming another service, or with lines this version cannot read, is pointed to *Load keys*, one of a later version asks for an update, this key on two lines or refused says why, and a code of no keys says so, each leaving the field as it was. *Type on a computer*, which the user cannot guess from its icon, says what it is (the key typed on a computer's keyboard, sent to the phone over Bluetooth, encrypted; Chrome or Edge with Bluetooth), then *1. On the computer, open the keyboard page:* with the link to `sleevesight.app/keyboard.html`, the camera, *2. Read the QR code the page shows with the camera.*, *3. Then, on the page, choose “Connect” and pick this phone.* and *Cancel*. On every key screen (these, each step of *Load keys*, *Purge keys*), *Cancel* is at the start of its line and the other buttons at its end, as in the key dialog (#543). On a guided scan the camera's view fills the height the steps leave but never less than 200 dp: in a short window, in landscape or with large text, the title, the steps and the view scroll, so the whole view can be brought onto the screen, and only *Cancel* stays fixed. Under the field (#763), small, *Save as new* or *Replace existing*; then what saving switches on: *Enable {service}*, ticked, for a source's key or an AI key while no AI service is on; *Your current AI service is …* and *Switch the AI service to {service}*, unticked, for an AI key while another is on; *{service} is already enabled.*, with no checkbox, for the key of the AI service on. *Save* saves the key, applies the checkbox, and the dialog gives way to the same summary as *Load keys*' (*Key saved*, *Services configured*), with *Done*. With three icons, the dialog is the screen's width less 16 dp at each side, up to Material's widest dialog, 560 dp: so on a screen narrower than 592 dp it is full width, and a wider one keeps that widest width.

The cache size limit covers the evidence and the written insights. A track's evidence is about 5 to 15 KB and each written card 1 to 3 KB (estimates, measured at the Phase 0.5 gate), so 50 MB holds about 2,500 tracks with their insights. Evidence expires within 30 days, so on its own it reaches the limit only for someone playing over 80 new tracks a day; written insights are kept no longer than *Keep history for* ([#643](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/643)), 90 days by default, so they reach it only past some 25,000 cards in that time, or with *Keep history for* at *Forever*, where they accumulate until it. From then on the least recently used entries go first: the insights of the tracks not played for longest. It is not lower because an evicted insight costs a paid call to write again.

Source switches decide what the model may use. The chips in the Sources section's table only change what is displayed.

## Privacy and security

- Notification access ([#708](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/708), [#738](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/738), [#739](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/739)). Android lists other apps' active media sessions only to an app that holds it (`getActiveSessions` needs the listener). On the first-run screen the access part (*Notification access is off.* or *is on.* down to its button) sits in a panel of the theme's container colour. Right above *Open notification access*, under *Why notification access* (`access_why_title`), one calm, factual statement (`access_statement`) explains why: Android lets an app see what music apps are playing only with notification access, and Sleevesight uses it for that alone; on Android 12 and newer, delivery of notifications to Sleevesight is disabled at system level and Sleevesight receives no notifications (the listener's filter entry, #739); on older versions, Sleevesight ignores all notifications; it never reads messages or contacts. A second paragraph (`access_more`) points to the open source code as the way to check; the screen's privacy-policy line and link follow the panel. Home's *Turn on notification access* prompt, which leads to that screen, has a short form (`home_access_statement`). *What it reads* says that Android requires notification access, and a line of its own (`first_run_no_audio`) that Sleevesight receives no audio.
- What leaves the phone, and to whom. The first-run screen says this plainly.
  - To the enabled sources: the normalized title and artists, for the playing track; Wikidata is asked only by catalogue numbers (MusicBrainz and Wikidata identifiers), never by a name. With *Search in the track's language* (#902), for a track sung in another language, that language's Wikipedia gets the titles of the song's, its film's or album's and its artists' articles there (from Wikidata's sitelinks, or the title as the player reports it), and Exa, if on, one more search with those names in the language's script. For the genre labels (#439), Last.fm also gets the album name: its track and album tags are asked for by the primary artist with the title and with the album. For the album's insight (#522), once it is opened and not before, MusicBrainz gets the album's release group id and Wikipedia the album's name with the primary artist's (Wikidata only ids, as before). When the album's insight is written, Exa gets a search for the album (#885). When the artist's insight is written and the sources have no line on the primary artist (#912), Exa gets a search for that artist as *AI web search* says: the artist's name with *Once*, searches the AI service writes from the track's title, artists and album with *Up to 3 times*. When the song's insight is written and the sources only list the song (#958), Exa gets a search for that song as *AI web search* says: the title and first artist with *Once*, in the track's language when it is known and *Search in the track's language* allows it; searches the AI service writes from the track's title, artists and album and what the sources found with *Up to 3 times*. With *Proactive* on (D13, the default), the title and artists of the next track in the player's queue also go to MusicBrainz where enabled.
  - To the chosen AI provider: the title and artists, as the player reports them and as normalized; the album name as the player reports it; and the evidence retrieved from the sources. This is for the playing track only: the next one is not written ahead (D13, #20). Once the user's style (D14) is built, each prompt will also carry it, including any free text they wrote, and everything that says what leaves the phone will say so.
  - Each API key, only to the service it belongs to, with each request to it; a fill the user asks for by hand also passes the field's key to their password manager, on the phone (#376). The first-run screen leaves that out: it is Android's, for any field, not a request of the app's.
  - To Spotify, once a build offers connecting a Spotify account (Phase 3) and only if one is connected: requests for the playlist or album being played, made with the user's own token.
  - To the server a cover's web address names, when a player gives the cover only as a web address: a request for that image, over HTTPS. The address itself may identify the album or episode. A plain `http://` address is not fetched (#696): `ArtFetcher` takes only `https://`, `:net` follows no redirect from `https` to `http`, and the manifest refuses cleartext (`usesCleartextTraffic="false"`) on every supported version, Android 8 and 8.1 included, whose default allows it.
  - To the phone's text-to-speech engine, only when an insight or a genre's reason is read aloud (#325): its text, with the track line when *Read the track line first* is on. The app hands it to the engine chosen in Android's settings and sends it nowhere itself; an engine may speak on the phone or, as Google's network voices do, send the text to its server, under its own policy. The first-run screen says so.
  - To the developer, only when the user sends it: a report of a problem with an insight (#390), which Google Play's [AI-Generated Content policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13985936) asks for ("in-app user reporting or flagging features ... without needing to exit the app"). *Report a problem*, under a written insight, the last line of its footer, at the start, in the theme's error colour (#671), takes a reason (*Offensive or inappropriate*, *Wrong*, *Other*) and an optional note, and shows the whole report: the reason and the note; the track as the player reported it (title, artists, album, length) and the MusicBrainz ids resolved for it; the insight's text and the evidence lines its prompt held (for an insight kept from before, which keeps no record of them, every line of the track, as the report says), with their sources and addresses; and the prompt version, provider, model and effort it was written with (`WrittenBy`, from the card's key). Never the keys, the history, the settings, the player or anything about the phone. It is handed to the user's mail app as an `ACTION_SEND` to `support@sleevesight.app`, the subject and body in its extras, with a `mailto:` selector so only mail apps answer it (not an `ACTION_SENDTO`, whose body Gmail ignores unless it fits in the address, #732), and reaches the developer only if the user sends it there; until then the mail app handles it as any message, and some sync drafts to the mail account, which the texts say. *Copy report* is offered beside it, and with no mail app the dialog says so. No server: an in-app endpoint (a Worker forwarding to the address) would keep the user in the app, but it is a server that receives data, which changes the privacy policy and the data safety form, so it is built only if Play's review asks. The composition (`insightReport`) and the hand-off (`MailHandoff`, `ReportDialog`) are one path, which the tester's report uses too.
  - To the developer, only when the user sends it: a tester's report (#452), from a small floating button on Home, shown only in developer mode with *Report button* at *Show* or *Auto* (developer mode on and *Auto* by default in a build for Play's testing tracks, #451; under *Auto* the button is hidden while Home scrolls, #518). A tap opens its menu, *Report* and *Hide*; *Report* takes what the app knows then (`TesterSnapshot`): the time with its zone, the version and build; the settings, each by its name in `Settings`, which hold no key (the key store is never read); what the player reports and the pipeline's view, with the sources' outcomes, the evidence, the insight and its citation check, sentence by sentence; and the diagnostics, the events kept in memory and what skip detection has learned, in the export's lines (`DiagnosticsText`). The track changes, the latest 100 up to the tap, are read only if the tester turns that part on; it starts off, since it lists every track played. The review shows each part with a switch and *Edit*, a note on what the tester expected, a line on what is collected, and the whole mail, through `ReportDialog` and `MailHandoff` as the insight's report. Nothing can be sent or copied while a part kept is still being read, nor while the report is longer than 250,000 characters (`TesterReport.MAX_CHARS`: 500 KB as Android passes text, half of Binder's 1 MB), which the dialog says; a part longer than 10,000 characters when collected (the events, often) has no *Edit*, since one field holding all of it is slow to type in, and is left out or edited in the mail app. Plain text, in labelled parts.

  Nothing else leaves the phone, except a diagnostics export the user makes to a file of their choosing (the *Diagnostics* developer setting) and a report (of a problem with an insight, or a tester's) the user hands to their mail app; and, while a key is typed on a computer (#394), the phone's Bluetooth advertisement, which devices nearby can see: the session's service, and the phone's name only with *Share phone's name with Type on a computer* on (#433). The duration, the session history and the record of track changes used to learn skip detection (D15) stay on it. The cover is held only in memory, for the current track, and never stored (#491).
- API keys and tokens are stored encrypted with an Android Keystore-backed key and never logged.
- An API key can be typed on a computer (#394): `keyboard.html` on the site makes a one-time session (an id and an AES key) and shows it as a QR code; the phone scans it, advertises a Bluetooth service named for the session's id, and the page writes the whole text at each change, sealed with AES-GCM (the counter in the nonce, and a counter not above the last taken refused; a code scanned again is refused, its id kept on the phone (the last 512, never the key), so a rescan, which would start the counter over, cannot have recorded messages replayed). Chosen over a page served by the phone on Wi-Fi, which is plain HTTP on a shared network and which an `https` page may not reach, and over the text as QR codes, which is not live. Web Bluetooth is Chrome and Edge only. The page's policy forbids any network request (`connect-src 'none'`), and its QR encoder is its own; the protocol is `assets/keyboard/protocol.js` and `KeyboardSession`, checked against each other by vectors the page's code makes (`make_vectors.mjs`). `BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE` and `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` are asked for only then; Bluetooth is not a required feature. The advertisement holds the service's UUID and the flags (21 of its 31 bytes) and no name, so Chrome's chooser lists the phone, the only device listed, as *Unknown or Unsupported Device*; *Share phone's name with Type on a computer* (#433, off by default) puts the phone's Bluetooth name in the scan response (Android can advertise only that name, not one of the app's), read at each start of the advertising, which Android shortens past 26 bytes and refuses past 29 characters; the first start with the name that fails, for that or any other reason (a vendor's stack may refuse a scan response otherwise), or a later one refused as too long, is made again without it for the rest of that opening, and the dialog says so; a later restart's other failure keeps the name and is retried with it, as any restart's (`PhoneName`). Reading the name needs `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`, and Android's size check `BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE`: nothing more is asked for. The phone's Bluetooth address cannot be shown to match the chooser's line instead: Android gives apps a placeholder address, and advertises from a random one.
- An API key can be entered by QR code (#268): one key in its own field, or a setup code of several, which a page of the site draws in the browser (`api-keys.html`, #436: the format and key shapes ported to `assets/api-keys/keys.js`, checked against `KeySetup` and `KeyShape` by vectors the page's code makes, the QR encoder the keyboard's, grown to version 40; no network request, no storage) or the CLI in the terminal (`keys-qr`), and the settings confirm, each key shown by its last four characters, before anything is saved.
- **The setup code is sealed** (#589, which weighed a hardened clear code, the keys over #394's channel, words typed from the phone and the phone's public key read by a webcam, and chose the words; #590). A code on screen is exposed to screen recording, sharing and remote desktops, screenshots, a photo or a look, and the terminal's scrollback, and a clear code is the keys. So *Load keys* shows five words drawn at random from the EFF's short wordlist 2 (1,296 words, about 10.3 bits each, each word's first three letters its own; bundled as published, SHA-256 checked, credited under CC BY 3.0 US); the page (`assets/api-keys/seal.js`, WebCrypto) and `keys-qr` (which reads them from exactly two places: Java's console with `readPassword`, only when it is a terminal, or a file named with `--words-from`, `-` for standard input; anywhere else it refuses before reading anything, so piping its output needs `--words-from`; never from the command line) seal the version 1 text with them, and the code is version 2 (`SealedSetup`): `sleevesight-keys:v2:` and base64url of the PBKDF2 rounds (4 bytes), a 16-byte random salt, a 12-byte random nonce, and the text sealed with AES-256-GCM, the associated data the prefix; the key is PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 of the words, in lower case, joined by single spaces, 600,000 rounds (about 0.15 s on an M1's JVM; a low-end phone, which derives it again to open the code, takes several times as long), and a code opened may ask for 600,000 to 6,000,000, so the count can rise without a new version. Every guess is offline (no server, and the page's `connect-src 'none'`), so the words carry their full strength: about 52 bits, and the rounds add about 19 bits of work to each guess; a short code like Magic Wormhole's would need a PAKE. The code opens only with the words; the 5 minutes and the one use are the phone's rules, not the code's: the phone takes one code (the words spent once one opens; a mismatch keeps them, for a typo), within 5 minutes of drawing them by the clock that counts deep sleep, forgets them when the screen is left, never saves them, and the screen is secure, but anyone who has both the code and the words can open it with any PBKDF2 and AES-GCM tool, anywhere, at any time. With the code alone, the words must be searched for offline: about 2^51.7 guesses at 600,000 rounds each, some 2^71 HMAC-SHA256 calls, thousands of GPU-years at today's speeds: ample against an opportunist, not nothing. If a code and its words leak together, the keys are replaced at their services. On the page they are masked, since a screenshot of both would open the code, a code shown goes before another is sealed (`draw.js`), so a clear one never stays while a sealed one is made or after sealing fails, and *Clear*, `pagehide` and `beforeunload` empty them. A wrong word fails the tag (*The words don't match this code*). The page and the app agree by vectors the page's code makes (`make_vectors.mjs`: sealed codes from fixed words, salts and nonces, typed words, the list's hash), which `ApiKeysPageTest` checks. The clear code stays: the page makes it with sealing turned off, for a key's own *Scan QR code*, which shows no words (a sealed code there points to *Load keys*), and `keys-qr --unsealed`; *Load keys* reads it and says it was not sealed. Residual risks: the words and the code on one screen together (masked on the page; a keylogger on the computer sees the words); a photo of the phone's words with one of the code, which opens it at any time; and, since CSP has no directive against top-level navigation, a tampered page script could still leave with a key in the address, reachable only if the served page itself were changed. The camera is asked for only when the user taps a scan; frames are decoded on the phone (ZXing, on Android's own camera API) and neither kept nor sent. Not Google's code scanner: its ML Kit reports device and usage data to Google. The key dialogs and the scan are secure windows (`FLAG_SECURE`). A key's field is also a password to Android's autofill (`ContentType.Password`, #376), so a password manager offers its entries on it; `FLAG_SECURE` does not stop an autofill service. It fills but is never asked to save: Compose commits the autofill session as the field leaves the screen, which offers the manager to keep the key, so the dialog cancels it first, however it goes: when it closes itself, when it leaves the composition, and on the activity's `ON_DESTROY` (a rotation disposes the dialog's window composition first), but not on `ON_STOP`, which a manager's unlock screen causes mid-fill. Saving a key there would pass it through the manager's service, which scanning a key handed over by someone else avoids. A fill requested by hand (the field's *Autofill* menu) still carries the field's current value to the service, as Android does for any field.
- No telemetry, crash reporting, analytics or accounts of our own. Google Play's Android vitals reports a crash of a Play-installed app to Google when the phone's owner allows usage and diagnostics sharing; that is Google's, not the app's.
- The privacy policy ([`privacy.md`](../privacy.md), at `sleevesight.app/privacy.html`) also says where the app stands under the GDPR and the EU AI Act ([#295](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/295)): who is responsible for what, the rights and how far each can be used on the phone today, transfers outside the EU, and the app's role and transparency duties under the AI Act (Article 50). The first-run screen, last, and the settings, under *About* beside the credits, link to it.

## Structural constraints

Enforced in CI rather than stated as policy:

- A dependency allowlist check fails the build if an analytics, crash-reporting or ads SDK appears in the lockfile.
- A manifest check fails the build on any permission beyond the expected set (internet, the network's state, notification listener binding).
- Network calls go only through the adapter and provider layer; a lint rule forbids HTTP clients elsewhere.

## Diagnostics

- A pipeline log screen, hidden behind a developer setting, mirrors the simulator's log: session events, normalization, each adapter's typed outcome and latency, cache hits, model calls, grounding-check counts; and *Ask AI*'s questions (Dates, Genres, Lyrics, a genre's definition, *Why is this …?* and the AI's genre list before it): each one asked (what, by a tap or automatically, the model), how it ended (with its calls, web searches and time), each one started automatically but not asked as its gates closed before its call, and each answer served from what was kept, with when it was written (#851). Beside it, the track changes recorded with their features, and what skip detection has learned, with a reset that lasts across restarts and keeps the history (D15). Exported on request only, to a file the user chooses. Its bar has the back arrow, the title and *Export* (#549); no *Close*, since each tab is a list of its own.
- In developer mode with *Report button* at *Show* or *Auto*, a button on Home makes a tester's report of the moment: the same events and learning, with the settings, the player's report and the pipeline's state, and the track changes only if the tester adds them; reviewed, edited and sent by the tester from their mail app (*Privacy and security*, #452).
- A coverage report (Phase 0.5) runs the pipeline over a playlist fixture and reports, per source, how many tracks resolved to each outcome, and per track, how many evidence lines and genre labels it got.

## Phases and gates

### Phase 0. Platform spike

A throwaway app on a real phone. Outputs, written to a log file:

- Fields Spotify, YouTube Music and one podcast app actually put in their media session, including `METADATA_KEY_MEDIA_ID`.
- Whether Spotify publishes `getQueue()` and `getQueueTitle()`.
- Which `PlaybackState` actions each player advertises; whether transport controls work.
- Whether `getSessionActivity()` and `spotify:` deep links open the expected screens.

**Gate:** every unverified item in Platform facts is resolved and this document updated. **Passed 2026-10-13**, with one item moved to Phase 1 (owner, 2026-10-13): restricted settings on notification access for an app installed from a file, on a device without Advanced Protection (#29, measured 2026-10-16: marked restricted but not enforced on that phone). In light of the installation findings, D7 moved distribution to Google Play (owner, 2026-10-12), so this matters for development installs and for any later channel outside Play, not for the release. The probe is removed (#30).

### Phase 0.5. Pipeline CLI

A JVM Kotlin command-line tool sharing the pipeline module: normalizer, resolution, adapters, evidence, synthesis, grounding check. Input is a playlist fixture; output is the coverage report and the generated insights.

**Gate:** on the test playlist, zero `unknown` citations, uncited sentences under 5 percent, every same-name collision either rejected or correctly resolved, and the coverage numbers reviewed. **Passed 2026-10-15**, with the coverage numbers reviewed and D12 decided (#88). Since prompt version 5 (#399), uncited sentences in live replies are judged over at least 50 sentences, pooled over a run's passes; a smaller sample is not judged, but fails at 15 percent or more of its own sentences (`Gate.GROSS_UNCITED`; `architecture.md`, *Testing*).

#### Gate run (#46): findings

The three criteria the CLI checks pass with insight prompt version 2 (`d0e6986`), which the owner chose after the first runs (below); the review of the coverage numbers, and so the gate, was the owner's, on #88. Run on 2026-10-14 with `:cli live --tracks all` (80 positions, 78 unique tracks) and the owner's keys: MusicBrainz, Wikipedia with Wikidata, web search (Exa, `instant`), and Claude Haiku 5.5 at the app's default effort (low). Last.fm was off: no key exists. The reports stay in the gitignored scratchpad, since most insights were written from web lines (on 72 of 80 positions in the first run) (*Web search provider (D11)*, *Open on the terms*); what follows is counts. The run predates the three resolution fixes it led to (#76, #77, #78; PR #83): after them the regression tracks, the listed collisions among them, replay unchanged, and tracks 55 and 62 change as noted below.

**Cost.** Four runs: the first live throughout; the second the same run from its cache, the insights re-checked unchanged after the grounding fix; the third with a trial wording of the prompt; the fourth with prompt version 2 as adopted, its MusicBrainz and Wikipedia answers from the first run's retrieval cache, web search and insights live (one insight, track 70, failed when the model's stream ended early, and was written again alone). Anthropic, first, third and fourth runs (the second's insights came from the cache): 362,363 tokens in, 113,086 out, about $0.09 at $0.10 and $0.50 per million. Exa: 80 searches a run, 321 in all with the retry (web results were not cached at the time; #82 has since added a cache), $1.28 at Exa's $0.004 per `instant` search, highlights included (D11). **Uncited sentences, by cause** (first run, 35):

- *A gap stated in other words* (25): the prompt asks for one uncited sentence when little is documented, and the model wrote "Beyond that, little else is documented about this recording", "little more is documented", "the sources say little about…", "the evidence doesn't document much else". The check knew only "little is documented" and a few fixed phrases.
- *An abbreviation read as a sentence end* (3): "the U.S. Billboard Hot 100", and a writer's middle initial twice, as the simulator splits them.
- *Commentary on the evidence* (3): two sources disagree on a date, or may describe another version, and the reply says so without a citation. Uncited by the contract; arguably fair.
- *The model's reasoning in the reply* (3 sentences, one track, 77): a reply that corrected itself in the open and then wrote its insight. The check flags it, as it should; nothing in the check can prevent it.
- *A restatement* (1): a reply that said again, uncited, that its only fact was the release.

**The fix in code** (`2b25013`, the grounding check): an abbreviation (`U.S.`, `R.E.M.`, a middle initial, `Dr.`, `St.`, `feat.`, `vs.`) directly before the next word does not end a sentence, unless initials come before a word that usually opens one (`It`, `The`, `He`; added after the PR #86 reviews). Its cost: a sentence that does end in one before another word (`... toured the U.S. Billboard named ...`) is joined to the next, and if it was uncited the next one's citation hides it. None of the gate's replies ended a sentence that way. It deviates from the simulator on purpose; the simulator's test case that splits after `Dr.` was listed as a deviation and every other stored reply agreed with it (until #481, when the stored results became the check's own). The same commit let a stated gap say "little else" or "little more"; with it, re-checking the same 78 replies gave 18 uncited of 262 (6.9 %): 12 gaps in yet other words, 2 commentary, the restatement, and the 3 of track 77's reasoning. Widening the check's phrase list to every way of saying a gap makes the check guess, and a guessing check lets uncited claims through as hedges.

**The prompt fix, adopted** (`d0e6986`, prompt version 2; the owner's choice). The contract's rule on a gap now reads: "If the evidence covers only part of what you would say, write the sentences it supports. To state the gap, end with exactly this sentence, without a citation: Little else is documented. Write no other sentence without a citation." The grounding check accepts exactly that sentence as a hedge (`GroundingCheck.GAP_SENTENCE`), and the "little else"/"little more" widening is removed, so the hedge patterns are the simulator's again. A trial wording first ("…in these words: "Little else is documented.", or "Little is documented." when nothing is.") left 4 uncited of 258: a fact without its id, a paraphrased quote, a comment, a remark on a title's variant. With version 2, over all 78 insights: 282 sentences, 212 cited, 70 hedges (69 the fixed sentence, 1 matched by the simulator's patterns), no uncited and no `unknown` sentence; 2 positions (4 and 24) had no usable evidence, as before, and replied `NO_EVIDENCE`. No reply in this run had uncited commentary or reasoning in the open, as the first run's did; a later model or style may bring them back, and the check will flag them.

Prompt version 3 (#193) drops the fixed sentence: it ended nearly every insight (`hedge 1` on each in the Phase 1 exit test, #55) and added nothing for the reader. The prompt asks for only the sentences the evidence supports, all cited, and nothing said of a gap (*Citation contract*).

**Collisions, in detail.** Wikipedia gave no song article for the Peleboy track (8, 22), whose namesake is DMX's song; no artist article for the wrong Reem (12, 20); no song lines for the covers 45 and 48 (rejected: the articles are the original songs, and MusicBrainz does not mark these recordings as covers) or 76 (none found); no song article for Jessiah's "willow" (61). MusicBrainz's one rejection was track 50: the recording that matches its title and length is credited to "Mr. A Balladeer", and the player says "a balladeer". Probably the same act under another form of its name, so a false rejection, in the safe direction: a rejection shows, a wrong match would mislead. Followed up in [#79](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/79): a credit under an honorific variant of the name now counts when no fitting candidate credits the artist by name (`architecture.md`, step 1). The two MusicBrainz entries look like one act: "a balladeer" (a Dutch band) is credited on the instrumental edition of *Sorry, Kid.* and "Mr. A Balladeer" (a person) on the vocal one, both released 2010-02-12, and both recorded the song with Reyn Ouwehand in 2011; the recorded data holds no lookup of either artist, so no relationship between them is shown. The album title on 61 ("Taylor Swift Covers") was not weighed: nothing reads it, and the gate passes without it.

**A wrong original-song label, outside the listed collisions.** Wikipedia gave `original_song` on three tracks. Two are right: 40 (Siouxsie and the Banshees' "The Passenger", Iggy Pop's song) and 79 (a cover of "Dancing in the Dark"). The third is wrong: 55, Chuck Berry's own "Roll Over Beethoven". MusicBrainz links the recording's work to the Wikidata item of the song, whose performers (P175) are the Beatles and three other performers; Chuck Berry is on the item only as composer and lyricist. The identity check reads an item whose performers include no credited artist as the original song (`architecture.md`, step 5), so the article's lines went to the model scoped as another recording's, against a song that is this one. A rule to consider: a credited artist who is the item's composer or lyricist, on a recording MusicBrainz does not mark as a cover, makes it the track's own song. Not a gate criterion; followed up in [#76](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/76), fixed in PR #83 (a credited writer, or the track's artists' earlier recording of the work, makes the item the track's own song; `architecture.md`, step 5): track 55 is now `song_article`.

**Thresholds** (`ResolutionThresholds`; the search size is `MusicBrainzClient.SEARCH_LIMIT`). Evidence from each unique track's MusicBrainz search (78 searches, every candidate's title similarity, length gap and credited artists), replayed through the resolver's rules at other settings:

- *Title similarity* (0.85): every accepted track's best candidate scores 1.0, and no candidate with a credited artist and a fitting length scores between 0.7 and 1.0. Any value from 0.7 to 1.0 accepts the same 42 tracks, with the same choices; 0.6 would accept, for track 50, its instrumental version, 1 s off the player's length, where the plain recording matches the length too: probably the wrong one. **Unchanged**; the data neither needs nor tests it.
- *Duration tolerance* (7 s): the closest candidate of each accepted track is within 1.5 s, but one within 4.8 s. Any value from 2 to 15 s accepts the same 42 tracks, but the choice moves: at 2 or 3 s, on up to seven tracks, to a recording with no length or a later one; at 10 or 15 s, on one or two, to an earlier recording of another length, which may be another take. 20 s would accept a 43rd, track 50's 2011 recording with Reyn Ouwehand, 18 s shorter: another version. The near misses beyond it are other versions too: a demo against the album take (36 s), a different recording of a folk song (50 s). **Unchanged.**
- *Search size* (50): six searches filled all 50 results, and the chosen recording is often far down: at rank 31 to 42 on four tracks, so 25 would change four choices and 10 twelve, each to a later release. MusicBrainz's ranking also varies from one request to the next: in the gate run, track 55 ("Roll Over Beethoven") found Chuck Berry's May 1956 recording; in the tuning searches, a little later, it was not among the first 50 and the same rules chose a 1985 release, the very error *Entity resolution* warns of. Asked for 100 (MusicBrainz's maximum), it was at rank 44. **A reason to raise it to 100**, not done here: the request changes, so the recorded MusicBrainz responses in `fixtures/http/` must be re-recorded with it. Raised to 100 in [#78](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/78) (PR #83), with the fixtures re-recorded; track 55 resolves to the May 1956 single at rank 87.
- *Not a threshold, found on the way*: track 62 ("Misirlou", Dick Dale) chose a 2012 release because the 1962 original is credited to "Dick Dale and His Del-Tones", which does not match the player's "Dick Dale" by name. Matching a credited artist to a band named after them is a resolution rule to consider, not a tuning. Added in [#77](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/77) (PR #83), with the leader confirmed by a performer relationship on the band's recordings: track 62 now resolves to the April 1962 single.

### Phase 2. Detail and discovery

The song, artist and album detail beyond their insights (the tracks by an artist or from an album in the playlist or session; the insights are Phase 1.5, #522), opening as the layout setting says (Phase 1.5, #447); genre cards beyond the genre's panel (which has what the genre is and why the track has it since #523); sounds-like cards; the re-evaluation of MusicBrainz tags as a genre label source (D12, #101); transport controls and player handoff; the prompt style with its overrides per card (D14); learned skip detection (D15), here or later.

### Phase 3. Context and reach

Optional Spotify account; further players; local model through Ollama; Discogs.

## Open questions

- **General-knowledge cards and the LLM role.** Genre descriptions, comparisons and "more like this" suggestions come from the model's own knowledge, and the suggestions are the model choosing items at runtime. That sits uneasily with keeping the model to narration. ## Appendix. Prompt contracts

Each prompt has two parts (D14; `architecture.md`, *Prompt style*):

- **The contract**, fixed: the evidence format and ids, the citation rule, no names, dates, places or numbers outside the evidence, the scope rules, `NO_EVIDENCE`, plain text without markdown, and for the genre and sounds-like cards their JSON structure. It comes after the style, under a heading saying it holds whatever the style says, so a style cannot override it.
- **Each card's built-in style**: the lengths and the tone below (sentence and word counts, "plain conversational English"). The user's style (length, tone, focus, audience, free text) adjusts it: shorter or longer relative to the built-in length, and for the JSON cards only in their text fields.

The insight's prompt and the genre reason's are two messages (insight version 10, genre reason version 6, #767; each version's exact prompt from insight version 8 and genre reason version 5 on is kept in `design/prompts/`): a system prompt, everything fixed for a listener (the role, a guard saying the user message's blocks are data, the style, then the contract, once), and a user message, the data. The others are one message: the data, then the style, then the contract, once (version 5, #399; until then the contract was stated before the style too, word for word). The data it narrates (the track as the player reports it, and the evidence) is fenced in `<track>` and `<evidence>` blocks that the prompt calls data, not instructions; each field and each evidence line is flattened to one line and its `<` replaced with `‹`, so no text from a player or a web page can write a block's tag or pass for an evidence line of its own (`:core`, `InsightPrompt`).

### Insight

Input: the now-playing title, artists and album as the player reports them (not the duration), the normalized fields, and evidence lines as `[ID] (source, scope) text`: the track's evidence less the artist-level lines *The insight stays on the track* leaves out (none with *Don't repeat artist facts* off, #407), and less the player's release line when MusicBrainz's first-release line names it as the album the song first came out on (*No restated release*, version 6, #424), each line with its id in the whole set (version 4, #239). The track and the evidence are the user message; the rest is the system prompt (prompt version 9, #767; version 10 the fixes from its blind A/B). What the system prompt gives the model:

- The role: the insight card on a now-playing screen, for someone listening right now, opening with the most interesting thing the evidence tells about the song (such as what it is about, how or why it was made, who played on it, what became of it), not restating the title and artists the screen shows, with no preamble or filler (version 10, #767). In a phone round of ten tracks, version 8 opened with the release on most of them, and the variants that opened with the song's own story read better on every model: the opening was the main difference. Version 9 also kept the release out of the opening; where the release was nearly all the evidence held, that left cards empty or had the model argue with itself in the reply, and it lost a blind A/B to version 8 (version 8 preferred on nine tracks of twenty, version 9 on three), so version 10 puts the release after the story instead.
- Built-in style: two to four sentences, each fact once, the release, if mentioned, after the story (version 10; version 9: only if it matters to the story); plain conversational English, no hype, no praise words (version 9); dates in the phone's form, as *Dates* writes them, its examples made with the phone's patterns ("17 October 2026, October 2026 or 2026" in British English, "October 17, 2026, October 2026 or 2026" in US English; the app's own form where no phone gives one, the CLI's; `InsightPrompt.dateRule`, #957): the model wrote "October 17, 2026" under *Dates*' "17 October 2026". The evidence lines stay in the app's own form whatever the phone, so no card's key depends on it. Added without a version, as in the song's, artist's and album's prompts, the genre reason's and a genre's definition, and the form is in no key: a card kept keeps its dates as written.
- No markdown.
- End every factual sentence with supporting ids, like `[W1]` or `[W2][S1]`.
- Use only the evidence; no names, dates, places or numbers outside it.
- Never the track's length or running time: the player already shows it (prompt version 7, #722; web search lines and the model's own knowledge gave it).
- No listener, play or view counts, tags, lists of similar artists (version 9, #767), distributors or codes such as the ISRC (version 10): thin insights were padded with them.
- "Original song" lines describe the song as first recorded by someone else; never credit them to this artist.
- "Artist" lines describe the performer, not this recording.
- Web lines may be about a different song with the same name; ignore what does not fit.
- A genre named only in a web line belongs to that page: if mentioned, attributed to the page as the line describes it ("a store listing files it under blues"), never stated as the track's tag or genre (prompt version 8, #800). Web pages are not a label source, so the Genres panel does not show it for the track; a label source's genres are cited as before.
- What a web page says the song means or how it sounds is that page's reading: attributed to the page ("one lyrics site reads it as a farewell"), never stated as fact (version 9, #767; the web genre rule widened, after a variant stated a lyrics-meaning page's reading as the song's meaning); a web page's facts stated plainly with their citation, and no id named outside one (version 10: version 9 attributed every web fact to its page, "One web page says …", and once wrote "S2 says"). A variant with the last as a rule of its own and a rule against talk of the evidence did worse (ten uncited sentences of 155, nearly all "The evidence here is thin."): naming what to leave out drew it in.
- No reading of the model's own added to a fact, such as "which suggests" or "a label that fits its feel" (version 9): version 8 padded thin evidence with such inferences, each cited to the line it read them into.
- If the evidence covers only part of what the insight would say: only the sentences it supports, each cited, even fewer than the built-in style asks for, one good sentence being enough (version 9; "a short insight" until then, and version 8 filled the style's length with sentences about the sources); nothing said of what the evidence leaves out, and no uncited sentence (prompt version 3, #193). Version 2 (#46) ended such an insight with exactly `Little else is documented.`, uncited; the simulator let the model word the gap freely.
- With model knowledge on: at most one sentence from general knowledge, ending with `[AI]` (`[M]` until 0.1.8, #668; the prompt's version stayed, as a card kept under `[M]` reads as one written now). Otherwise none.
- Only the final text, never revised or corrected in the reply (version 9, #798; the checks below stay).
- With no usable evidence: reply exactly `NO_EVIDENCE`, a release date or a credit being enough for a sentence (version 10). Version 9 had "nothing in the evidence worth telling about the song", and never saying what is missing: on its A/B's first run the role's list read as what every card must cover, and three thinly documented tracks were told in seven uncited sentences that the evidence lacked it, hence also "the evidence tells" and "such as" in the role; but it left fifteen of 54 cards empty against version 8's six, and a short card from the release and a credit was preferred to none. When the evidence has nothing to narrate (no line, or only the player's release unless model knowledge is on and the player has reported the artists), no prompt is sent and the app shows its own text.

**Checked before it is shown: a reply that revises itself** ([#798](https://github.com/wkusnierczyk/sleevesight/issues/798), `SelfRevision`). After the grounding check, and after a correction it takes out (*A reply that corrects itself shows its last version*, #597), what the card would show is checked for revision left in it, which the correction cannot take out when its comment cites a line or shares its line with the text (on the phone, 0.1.13: "Wait, I need to correct that: the evidence says … [S3][S4], so I'll keep only what is supported.", then the insight a second time). Four signs, read sentence by sentence on the reply as plain text (Markdown's line marks, `-`, `1.`, `>`, `###`, its emphasis marks and backticks set aside, every space as the grounding check reads it, with a map back to the reply for the cut), so these marks hide no sign (`- **Correction:**`, `### Correction:`, a sentence in emphasis), and outside quotations and emphasised titles or lyrics for the first three (an emphasis is a title or a lyric only when it holds no citation, opens with no label, and ends no sentence: no stop before a capital inside it, and no stop at its close after more than one word or before a citation, so `*Hmm, that's wrong.*` is read and `*Help!*` is a title; a sign worded otherwise than below is not found). Labels and asides are read at every line's start too, inside a sentence as well (`- **Correction:**` on a list's second line), and a line that holds only a label's or an aside's word (`### Correction`) is one: a sentence that opens as only an aside does (*Wait,*, *Oops,*, *Hmm,*, *On second thought*, *Correction:*, *Update:*), or with a label (*Correction:*, *Update:*, an introduction such as *Here is the corrected insight:* or *Corrected reply:*), also when an emphasis holds the aside or the label alone (`**Correction:**`, `*Wait,*`, `**Corrected insight:**`, `**Wait**,`; not `*Wait!*` or `*Wait*,`, which may be titles; the title *Wait, Let Me Fix It* is set aside as one), or breaks off for one (*… on... no, that is not …*); the first person correcting or withdrawing its text ("I need to correct", "let me revise", "I'll keep only", "my earlier draft"); talk of the evidence as what the reply is checked against ("the evidence says that", "not supported by the evidence", "evidence lines"); a later sentence of six words or more that is the opening one again, the same words in the same order (the insight written twice; sentences that only share most of their words may state two facts, "The studio version was recorded at Abbey Road in 1965. The live version … in 1966.", and a restatement with words changed is mostly found by the comment before it). Words a title would capitalise do not count (*I Will Survive*, Monk's *Evidence*). A stated gap ("The evidence doesn't say more about this recording.") is no sign: the contract asks against it, but it reads as prose, and a second call for it would often end in the error state rather than a fair insight. Such a reply is not shown as written. First it is cut, with no further call: what comes up to the end of the last sentence with a sign goes (for a label or an emphasised aside, through it, so `**Correction:** It came out in 1975 [MB1].` and `Here is the corrected insight: It came out in 1975 [MB1].` keep their sentence; for a repeated opening, up to its second occurrence), and what follows is shown and kept when it is usable: some text, with no correction or sign of revision left in it, and no sentence the grounding check finds uncited or citing a line that is not there. The part cut out is kept for the developer, as a correction taken out is (*Show grounding details* says *AI model's self-correction taken out*, and the reports give the text); the cache keeps the reply as written, and it is cut again when served. While the reply streams, the text stops at the first sign in what the card would show, read once no quotation or emphasis is open. Only when nothing usable is left (the correction ends the reply, or what follows fails a check) is the reply asked for once more, a second paid call within the same call (logged as a call; the card counts the tokens of every reply of the call, a `NO_EVIDENCE` between them too; Stop cancels it). If the second reply leaves nothing usable either, the card shows the app's words for an answer that could not be read (*Writing failed: the AI model's answer could not be read.*) and offers Write again; the specific reason (*the AI model's reply corrected itself in its text, twice*) shows only with *Show grounding details*, and the diagnostics log names the sign. A card kept from before that revises itself is cut the same way, or, with nothing usable, not served, and the track is written as though it had none. Measured on the CLI's recorded replies of #399's and #424's runs (917 distinct replies, the measurement #597 used, no new calls), it finds two, both corrections left as written, and no other reply, and both are cut to a usable part with no second call; the grounding check's stored replies have none. Prompt version 9 (#767) also asks for the final text only, never revised or corrected in the reply; the check stays, since the rule makes such replies less likely, not impossible.

### Song, artist and album insights

The three insights under *Key insights* (#522; `ItemPrompt`, version 1, one prompt for the three, `ItemInsights`), each its own call: the insight's fenced data (the track as the player reports it, now with the subject in a `<subject>` block, *Song*, *Artist* or *Album* and its name) and the insight's rules and built-in style as they were until its version 9, word for word (`ItemPrompt.contract`; the key insight's version 9, #767, left this prompt one message, at its version 1), two to four sentences, and the insight's rule on dates (#957), with one rule of the subject's first: the song's tells the song's own story (how it was written and recorded, who plays on it, its release and its versions) and does not open with the headline, which the key insight gives; the artist's is about the artist named only, leaving out lines about another artist credited on the track, and says nothing of the track playing; the album's is about the album only (how it was made, its release, how it was received), nothing of the track playing. The evidence is the subject's own: the song's, the track's less its artist-level lines; the artist's, the track's artist-level lines (those naming the primary artist when several are credited), numbered on their own; the album's, the lines gathered for the album (`architecture.md`, *Source adapters*), numbered on their own. With nothing to narrate, no prompt is sent. Checked as the insight is (`GroundingCheck`), and a reply that revises itself is read as the key insight's is (#798, `readReply`): cut to what follows the revision, else asked for once more, while it streams, once it ends, and when it is served from the cache. ### Genre reason

*Why is this …?* in a genre's panel (#523; `GenreReasonPrompt`, version 6: the insight's version 9 structure, a system prompt the same for every genre and track and a user message with the genre's label fenced in `<genre>`, then the track and the evidence, and the insight's version 9 rules on a web page's reading of the song and on the final text, with a rule on counts of its own, #767; version 5: with the model's own knowledge on, the reason may be written from it, #695; version 4: a label the evidence cannot explain is answered `NO_EVIDENCE`, never disputed, #853; version 3: the insight's rules gained the one on a genre from a web page, #800, and the reason's ban on sentences about the sources lets that genre be attributed to its page). Input, the user message: the genre's label as the chip shows it, the insight's (the track as the player reports it, the normalized fields), and the track's whole evidence, artist lines included (the artists' other work is part of why a track is filed under a genre). For a label only MusicBrainz gives, only once the AI's genre list has it (*Genre list*, below; D12: the genre then comes from the model, not from MusicBrainz's tags). The insight's rules, but the one against readings of the model's own (telling what in the evidence fits a genre is one) and with a rule on counts that leaves out only listener, play and view counts (a source's tags and the artists' company explain a genre), with three of its own first: the genre is a label, not a fact, to be explained only from the evidence; the reply is about the music and the artists, never about the evidence or the sources (what they say, do not say or leave out), but for a genre or a reading of the song only a web page gives, attributed to its page (version 6); and the reply never says or suggests that the track is not of the genre, or belongs under another instead: a label the evidence cannot explain is answered `NO_EVIDENCE` (version 4, #853). With the model's own knowledge on (version 5, #695), three of the insight's rules give way to the reason's own: the evidence first, the model's knowledge only where the evidence does not explain the label and never against it; sentences from the knowledge, as many as the explanation needs, each ending `[AI]` (the insight allows one); and `NO_EVIDENCE` only when neither the evidence nor the knowledge explains the label (the insight's "If no evidence is usable" left out), the first rule reading "explain it from the evidence and, where the evidence does not, from your own knowledge" (until version 6, the evidence was also introduced as the sources' facts, not the only ones; version 6's data guard calls it facts from outside sources, the knowledge on or off); with web search's tool, the model searches before it writes from its knowledge. With the knowledge off the prompt was version 4's in version 5. With web search's tool (#801), the search's instructions are in the system prompt, sent on every turn of the tool loop, and the pages' lines are numbered on after the evidence's web lines, unnamed, so the system prompt stays the same for every track (version 6).
